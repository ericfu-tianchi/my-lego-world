# My Lego World — project rules (read first)

Eric (PM, technical, writes little code) is building a LEGO-style world of the ~20 countries he has visited, as an app for his pixel-style personal OS site. Chinese for conversation, **extremely terse** (he hates long answers: conclusion + the one decision he must make; long content goes in an artifact page). English UI copy in top-tier SaaS style. He owns every major decision: present options with a recommendation, one question at a time, never vibe-code past a decision point. Report with artifacts / review pages he can open, not walls of text.

## Hard rules (non-negotiable)
1. **Moulds must be real.** Every part maps to a real LEGO part number (verified against the LDraw official library) and an official colour. Three tiers (decided 2026-09-11):
   - **L1 official moulds**, basic *and* specialty (minifig accessories, animals, plants, food, clips/bars/hinges, string). Geometry is imported from the LDraw library (CC BY 2.0, credit LDraw.org in the app) via `tools/ldraw/bake.mjs`, never hand-invented. Shared "life layer" vocabulary is open to every country; per-country exclusive moulds ≤ 12. The bake keeps the loader's smooth normals and **re-winds every triangle to agree with its normal**: LDraw builds left/right pairs by mirroring (3819 = mirrored 3818), which reverses winding and lets back-face culling eat the sleeve so the wrist post shows through (Eric's screenshot, 2026-09-16). The renderer additionally draws LDraw moulds two-sided.
   - **L2 decorated elements**: a real mould + our own flat vector print (Canvas 2D code, `src/core/prints.ts`). Follow LEGO print conventions: flat faces only, ≤ 4 flat colours, no gradients/photos, 1–2 characters of real place/shop names or generic words, legible at thumbnail size, never over studs. ≤ 12 designs and ≤ 40 pieces per country. Minifig faces/torsos count here. **Lettering is part of the mould**: a sign, plaque or lantern carries the lettering culture of the real object (painted, carved, brushed — never a default UI typeface), one or two characters or a short word set with `kanji()`, canvas `px ≥ 320`, colour and ground as the real object has them. If it cannot be read at thumbnail size or does not look like the real thing, cut it. Each country records its lettering choice in `docs/countries/<country>.md`.
   - **L3 invented shapes: forbidden.** Official sets contain none.
2. Every part is stud-connected to the scene's ground level (`SceneMeta.base`, e.g. 3 for a floating island) and **nothing interpenetrates**. `npx tsx scripts/validate.ts` must print PASS for every scene; it is a build gate, not a warning.
   - **Overhangs are checked by their real hull** (2026-10-01, a palm frond ran through the Merlion: the frond's box was its 1×2 seat). Any LDraw part whose baked bounds reach past its declared box (leaves, fronds, canopies) is also tested with those bounds against every non-plant part of another element; plants may interleave with plants and with their own trunk (same tag).
   - **Collision uses the real baked geometry, never the declared box**: `ext` (footprint rectangle) and `extY` (vertical extent in plates) come straight from the LDraw bounding box. A declared box that is taller or wider than the part produced false clashes; one that is smaller hid real ones. Tolerances: `SKIN` 0.15 studs sideways (rounded hulls), `VSKIN` 0.5 plates vertically (a stud sinks into the part above).
   - **LDraw origins sit at the part's TOP face.** A part that rests on a stud needs `lift` = its own height in plates, or it is drawn one plate low and buried in its host. Hair, hats and hands hang below their origin and take `lift: 0`.
   - **Held accessories are gripped at their surface.** `HOLD_DEFAULTS[key].gp` is the point on the part that the hand closes on (a handle, a shaft, an edge, a loop) in the part's own frame; `minifig2` throws `grip buried` if that point is more than 0.45 studs inside the part's own box, because a hand inside a tray is not something anyone can build.
   - Hands sit in the hand's clip (`RIG.grip`, derived from the LDraw 3820 header) with the arm posed (d/f/h/u); held parts connect through `fig_hands_*`, never through the body. A figure's own legs/torso/head/hair/hands may overlap each other and nothing else.
3. **Any connection technique real LEGO uses is allowed** (revised by Eric, 2026-09-19: "是不是很官方、还是需要 SNOT，我真的不 care，只要像、好看"): studs-out / SNOT via side-stud bricks and brackets, hinges, clips, bars, tiles as finishing — as long as every piece is a real mould at a legal real position. Complex studs-out sub-builds are authored as LDraw models (`models/*.ldr`, real part numbers and LDU positions) and baked into one catalogue entry with a BOM, the way `shutter_closed` and `cart_wheel_*` are, so the scene grid stays integer studs / plates and the validator treats the sub-build as one hosted part. Half-stud offsets in the grid still go through jumpers.
   **Part selection is a research deliverable, not a default.** The ~120 parts already in the catalogue are *not* a vocabulary. Before building any element, list the parts a LEGO master would use for it — from the full LDraw library (~20k moulds), by reading the official set's inventory and the reference MOCs (which curved slopes, wedges, brackets, tiles, hubs, hinges) — write that list into `docs/countries/<country>.md` §1, then bake what is missing (`tools/ldraw/bake.mjs`, minutes per part). **Selection is iterative, not one shot** (Eric, 2026-10-01): references → a stud-grid sketch of the element (side and top profile, each surface labelled with a part *type*: curved slope, wedge, bracket…) → look up concrete moulds → build → render beside the reference → swap parts and repeat, two or three rounds per element; the part list in §1 is final only when the element passes. The footprint an element needs comes out of that sketch and feeds the layout, not the other way round. Building from the basic bricks alone is the laziness that produced the 40-point Singapore landmarks twice (Eric, 2026-09-19: "千万不能再偷懒了…就用那 60 个基础的东西，所以做得跟屎一样").
4. No AI image generation anywhere in the product. Canvas-drawn prints are code, not generated images. Concept images (gpt-image-2 via Flyer) are used only to choose direction / as references.
5. Form = **B, garden-island diorama** (decided 2026-09-11). **It is one series — by DNA, not by identical numbers** (Eric, 2026-09-18 and 2026-10-01: 别限制死，那样反倒趋同). Every country shares: the same scale (a stud is a stud, minifig-scale life layer with 2–3 figures), the same floating-island treatment (`base` 3, two bricks of earth, a capped stone plinth one cell outside the footprint), three height tiers (hero landmark > tallest tree > house > figures), landmarks compressed to ≤ 0.6 × island width, colours from the real place but never louder than Kyoto, the same render pack and judge. Free per country: the exact footprint (keep the area within **2,000–3,500 studs²**, Kyoto is 56×44 = 2,464, so the islands still read as one set side by side), the outline (it follows the landmarks — a bay, a hard reclaimed edge, no water at all for a desert country), the water share. Per-scene statistics are **diagnostics, not gates**: specialty moulds below ~8 % of pieces means the build is under-detailed (a floor; there is no ceiling — a part earns its place by making the thing look right), decorated elements ≤ 12 designs / ≤ 40 pieces stays as print discipline. **Done bar for a country** (Eric, 2026-09-18, calibrated on Kyoto v10 = 8): judge weighted ≥ 8.0, every dimension ≥ 7, compliance and regularity audits all pass; 8.5 is the stretch, not the gate (judge/JUDGE.md v4) — and Eric's own verdict outranks it.
6. **People are scarce.** A set this size gets **two or three minifigures, never more** — six people standing around read as clutter, not life (Eric, 2026-09-11). Each one must be doing something legible from a normal viewing distance. Set dressing that cannot be read (a plain coloured flag, an unlabelled sign) is worse than nothing: cut it.
7. **Regularity is not optional** (Eric found nine of these in one look, 2026-09-18). Everything vertical is built on one axis — a 1×1 never rides on a corner of a 2×2; width changes go through a jumper or a 2×2 tube over a stud (`stoneLantern`, `tree`). Anything symmetric in reality is symmetric in the model and is placed by a helper, never hand-rotated: hip-roof corners via `hipCorners` (SE 0, NE 1, NW 2, SW 3), railings via `railing` (equal segments, posts at both ends). Parts hosted in frames share the frame's LDraw origin (`anchor: 'origin'`, same `lift`) so they sit flush. A part's box is its real connection, not its body: one socket or tube → 1×1, two anti-studs → 1×2 (an even box centres the socket on a stud boundary, and the bird lands between two studs); `ext` covers the overhang. Stairs are flat plates of equal rise in the structure's colour. A held prop with a handle is gripped by the handle (`HOLD_DEFAULTS.gp` on the handle, not the body). Multi-layer elements follow the real object's colour layering and proportions, checked against a photograph. Verify each of these from a top-down `?only=` render before the judge sees it.
8. **Check your own work in close-ups before calling it done.** After building or moving anything with a minifigure, an accessory or a plant, render a close-up from at least two angles and look at it: is the hand holding the thing or passing through it, is any part buried in another, is the pose one a real minifig can strike. The validator catches geometry; only a render catches "looks wrong". Never hand work to Eric that you have not looked at.
9. **Breathing room is a rule** (Eric, 2026-09-11 evening). Spacing is enforced in code with `Sites` (lib.ts): landmarks, trees, figures and lanterns register a radius; a placement closer than r₁ + r₂ + 2 throws and the scene does not build. Layout defaults — guidance, not gates (Eric, 2026-10-01): about a third of the island open lawn or water; water, where the place has it, as the calm centre; ≤ 1 figure per 6×6 area; trees never touch each other or a roof; three height tiers readable from any angle.
10. **Part budget is a design constraint.** Official landscape dioramas are ~1,500 parts; a scene over ~3,000 is over-built, and a solid earth block eats the budget that should go to detail. Earth is about two brick layers deep (a deeper rim wall keeps the edge reading thick); strata colour bands must be whole brick layers measured from `base`, or the packer is forced to emit plates and the count explodes. Hollowing the interior with a lattice does NOT help — the packer turns a solid volume into 2x8 bricks and a lattice into ragged 1x1s (measured 2026-09-11: solid 3,013 → lattice 3,186).
11. **Research before bricks — a stage, not a glance** (Eric, 2026-09-18). Before anything is placed for a country, write `docs/countries/<country>.md` §1 from `docs/countries/_template.md`: for the scene and every hero element, what the real thing looks like (photos, proportions, colours → LEGO colours), which official sets build it and how, which LEGO Masters / MOC technique applies, and what we borrow; plus one gpt-image-2 composition reference (Flyer `/image-gen`, adapt docs/03). Eric reviews that page before building starts. Palette comes from photographs of the real place, never from what looks LEGO-like. Trees use the generic `tree(kind)` armature (2×2 round trunk on its own axis, four limbs per fork, staggered clusters) with a per-country foliage kind. The reusable method is `docs/country-playbook.md`; the brief starts from `judge/briefs/_TEMPLATE.md`; Japan's notes are the worked example (`docs/countries/japan.md`).

12. **Every brick in the scene is a brick** (Eric, 2026-09-25: 两个巨型柱子违背了乐高的精髓). An LDraw assembly is a way to compute legal positions for studs-out or hinged sub-builds; it is never a part in the scene. `bp.assembly()` explodes it into member placements (one per real brick, with its own transform) plus a validation proxy; the renderer and the dismantle physics see only the members, the validator only the proxy. A landmark that dismantles as one lump is a bug. Design and steps: `docs/countries/singapore.md` §1.9.
13. **Scale before detail** (Eric, 2026-09-25: 你对 set 的 scale 理解有偏差). A 56×44 island holds **four or five iconic places plus the life layer**, not a whole city. Before placing anything, write each element's footprint and part budget; complexity must fit the area — ten petals in a 6×6, a 4-wide container ship, a 3-wide sculpted lion head are unreadable and get cut or enlarged, never squeezed. Each place is paired with one minifigure doing something (the Kyoto pattern). If it cannot be built beautifully in its area, it does not go in.
14. **As real as other builders manage.** For every element, look at how official sets, LEGOLAND Miniland, Rebrickable MOCs and LEGO Masters build the same subject and lift the best technique; offering a "simpler" alternative (a stylised stand-in) is not an option — "做得越像实物越好" (Eric, 2026-09-25). References and the chosen technique are written in the country page before the first brick.
15. **The pipeline carries the lessons, not the conversation.** Every principle Eric has had to repeat goes into a rule here, the playbook, or a check in `scripts/audit.ts`, so the next country starts from the rules instead of another round of coaching (Eric, 2026-09-25: 一次次调教、一次次重复讲这些原则，真的很累). After Singapore closes, `scripts/audit.ts` is a build gate beside `validate.ts`.

## Workflow
- **App shell** (2026-10-02): the home is a desk globe after 21332 (`src/app/globe-view.ts`: "bands" — sea in 1×2 tiles along latitude rings, coasts in round tiles, land 1×1 plates raised, ranges and ice a plate higher; 23.4° tilt, grey 1×2-plate meridian, stepped reddish-brown stand; the camera is fixed and drag/scroll turns only the ball). A pin turns, zooms and sinks the ball until a sliver of crown shows under the set; the set enters with Rebuild (the take-apart heap played backwards). Take apart is zero-g: a burst, then procedural drift for ever. The entrance (Rain) drops the bricks from above the screen on an eased clock that starts with the click, spiralling the same way as the idle rotation, the set riding the crown and growing with it. Take apart (Nebula) bursts at a fixed force and drifts for ever. `?scene=<id>` opens a set directly over its globe; `&noglobe` for clean element renders. A new country = a scene in `SCENES` + a `PLACES` entry. Dismantle physics: Rapier SIMD in a worker, render-side interpolation; `node tools/bench-physics.mjs <scene>`. `npx vite build --mode single --outDir dist-single` → `My Lego World.html`.
- **Gate: `npm run gate`** (comment lint, typecheck, assembly audit, validator for the live scenes) before any render shown to Eric.
- Build in `src/scenes/*.ts` using `src/scenes/lib.ts` grammar; LDraw assemblies go in with `bp.assembly()` (or `placer().asm`), never `bp.part()`; run validator, `npx tsx scripts/check-assemblies.ts` after any assembly re-bake (and `scripts/audit.ts` once it exists); `npx vite build`; serve `dist/` on :8125; `node tools/shot.mjs out.png "scene=kyoto&az=..&el=..&dist=..&tx=..&ty=..&tz=..&dismantle=<sim s>&strength=..&sil=1"`.
- New parts: `LDRAW_DIR="$PWD/vendor/ldraw-lib/ldraw" node tools/ldraw/bake.mjs <id> …` (the library is vendored in the repo and git-ignored; a session scratchpad copy gets wiped and then nothing can be re-baked) → baked geometry in `src/parts/ldraw/`, then a `ldrawPart(...)` entry in `src/core/catalog.ts` with hand-filled stud/accept semantics.
- **Checkpoints with Eric** (revised 2026-09-19 after Singapore v1 scored 40/100 on its landmarks): (1) the research page before any brick, and **for every landmark a one-page analysis + build scheme for sign-off** (real proportions from photos, 5+ brick versions of the same subject from Google / Rebrickable / LEGOLAND / LEGO Masters, what each does for the signature details, our scheme level by level, part types) — Eric found Google references in a minute that the first pass missed (2026-09-19: 你自己要多分析，产出你自己的见解，让我拍个板); (2) **one screenshot of each building as soon as it is finished in the test scene**, before it goes onto the island — so a wrong direction is caught after one building, not after the whole island; (3) the finished island before the judge round. Between those, iterate alone. Never show a structural draft as done: what he sees must already look like the thing.
- **References are not optional** (Eric, 2026-09-19): before building any landmark, generate 1–2 gpt-image-2 references of "that building as a LEGO element" (Flyer `/image-gen`, saved under `docs/refs/<city>-<element>.png`) and look up LEGO Masters / MOC / official-set photos of the same subject (WebSearch); write the proportions and the borrowed techniques into `docs/countries/<country>.md` §1 first. Singapore's Merlion and MBS were built from memory and came out unrecognisable; Kyoto's pagoda, built from 21060 / 10315 references, did not.
- **Signature details are the point**: a landmark must carry the one thing everyone knows it for (MBS's infinity pool on the SkyPark, the Merlion's open mouth and mane, the Supertree's flaring canopy), built precisely, not implied. Every cell of every landmark must be `inIsland`; the island outline may bend to fit a landmark (Eric: terrain need not be rectangular or identical across countries).
- **Beauty bar before anything is shown**: put the render beside the set photo / MOC photo / gpt-image-2 target. If it would embarrass us in that pair, it is a draft, not a result — keep going. "Reads roughly" is 40 points (Eric: v2 and v3 of Singapore looked the same to him).
- Quality loop: element sample → render pack (judge/JUDGE.md §渲染包; element shots and silhouettes with `?only=<tag>`) → self-check every image → judge agent (`.claude/agents/lego-judge.md`) → fix → repeat until the done bar. Judge never sees code and names part *types*, never numbers (we resolve numbers against LDraw). Eric's own pass over the live model outranks the judge: two rounds missed nine regularity faults he saw at once.
- Camera for `tools/shot.mjs`: position = target + (sin az·c, sin el·dist, cos az·c) — az 0 is +z, π/2 +x, π −z, 3π/2 −x. World x = grid x − W/2, z = grid z − D/2, y = plates × 0.4. The table (y = 0) counts as ground for the validator alongside `SceneMeta.base`.
- **Eric's issue loop** (2026-09-11): he lists problems from the live model; for each I find the root-cause level (placement / grammar / catalogue semantics / validator / renderer / judge / rule), fix at that level so the class of problem cannot recur, record it in the owning file, re-render only affected views. Never rebuild a scene from zero; a broken foundation means rebuilding one element, not the island.
- Minifigs: `minifig2()` = real LDraw legs / posed torso+arms / hands / head (`tools/ldraw/minifig-asm.mjs`), printed face + torso, any baked hair, accessories via `HOLD_DEFAULTS` (pose, grip point, side rotation). Hands must not touch anything but the body; held things must not touch the body.
- Keep HANDOFF.md current at the end of every working session. **Commit at milestones** (a delivered round, a major pipeline change, a rule change) with a message that says what changed and why; this is Eric's personal repo (`main`, no remote yet — ask before pushing). Never commit HANDOFF.md or `.snapshots/`; end commit messages with the attribution line the session prescribes. Backlog was caught up 2026-09-25 (`62228da`…`9d33238`).
