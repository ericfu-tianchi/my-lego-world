### 1.1 Scene in one sentence
A clear late-October afternoon on Seoul's own north–south axis, the city's founding rule 배산임수 (mountain behind, water in front): the pine-and-granite **Bugaksan** with the **City Wall** on its crest along the back; **Gwanghwamun** facing the camera at back-centre, and on the square before it **BTS's comeback stage** — the giant LED "picture frame" through which the gate is seen, seven idols mid-move, fans with purple light sticks; **Bukchon** hanok roofs stepping down an alley at back-right under gold ginkgos; **Namsan** at mid-right, wooded, with the white **N Seoul Tower** as the tallest thing on the island; and across the front the wide calm **Han** with Hangang Park's lawn and cycle path, a chimaek picnic, and a Hangang Bus catamaran on the water.

Why it works: every palace building faces south and every classic tower view looks north across the river, so a default camera in the south sees all four classic Seoul sightlines the right way round (`seoul-real` §1.2): square → gate → mountain; tower across the Han; tower down a Bukchon alley; gate seen through the stage frame (the real 2026 press photo, `seoul-kpop/real-frame-gate-through.jpg`).

### 1.4 Island outline and ground (layout A, "배산임수 axis")
- **North at the back.** The back edge follows **Bugaksan's ridge** — the outline bulges back where the peak rises, City Wall on the crest, red pines on the slope, pale granite outcrops. **Gwanghwamun** back-centre-left facing the camera with a palace-wall stub each side; **the stage** on the square in front of it, offset west of the axis as it really stood (the gate stays readable); **Bukchon** back-right on rising ground; **Namsan + tower** mid-right, offset in x from Bukchon so the tower never hides the alley; **the Han** across the whole front, its north bank bowing toward the camera in the middle, leaving through the left and right plinth faces; Hangang Park as a strip along the bank.
- Rejected: B — river through the middle with a Gangnam sliver (Shanghai's two banks); C — tower on the water at a corner (exactly Shanghai's Pearl composition).
- **Ground per district:** square and palace forecourt **Light Bluish Gray** irregular slab mosaic with Dark Bluish Gray joints (bakseok); Bukchon alley LBG tiles stepping in flat 2-plate rises; hills Olive Green / Dark Green under pines with LBG / DBG granite; park lawn Green + Olive Green with a few Dark Tan patches; cycle path DBG with a Yellow dashed centre line; bank stepped LBG; river Sand Blue.
- **Size (first estimate; the element sketches set the final number):** ≈ **76 × 64** (≈ 4,000 studs² of land and water inside the outline) — above the 3,500 default, as LA and Prague, because the tower needs a ≥ 64-wide island for its 96 plates and the gate alone is 30 × 18.

### 1.8 Prints (≤ 12 designs, ≤ 40 pieces) and lettering culture
- Planned: faces ×3 (idol smile, calm, grin), torsos ×5 (idol outfit, guard robe, hanbok jeogori, two casual), plaque **光化門**, kkotdam meander band, chicken box = **11 designs**, ≈ 30 pieces. The stage screen is **not** a print (1 × 1 tile mosaic).
- Lettering culture: the palace plaque is **gold-leaf brush regular script on black**, read right to left (`seoul-real` §7) — three characters, against the 1–2 rule; the real object has three, and 門 alone loses the meaning (default: keep all three at `px ≥ 320`, cut if it does not read at thumbnail). The chicken box: a short hangul word (치킨) in a delivery-brand marker style. No hangul on the tower (it is read by silhouette).
- **Likeness (BTS):** no member names, no faces, no logo printed (LA's rule: no real person's likeness — Kobe was not printed). What says "BTS": the real stage on the real square, seven, purple light sticks.
