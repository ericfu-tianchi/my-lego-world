## 1. Research (before building)

### 1.1 Scene in one sentence
A floating island on **the Seine**: the bronze lattice of the **Eiffel Tower** (the hero) rises over a corner of the Champ de Mars with its clipped trees and sand paths; the river runs through the middle between two-level cream stone quays, crossed by a low **stone bridge with round bastions over its piers**; on the far bank the cream twin towers of **Notre-Dame** stand over a paved parvis, and on the near corner a **Haussmann building** with black iron balconies and a grey zinc mansard keeps a **café terrace** under a dark green awning. Dark green **bouquiniste boxes** sit open on the parapet under a plane tree. A waiter carries a tray, a cyclist with a baguette in the basket crosses the bridge, a bookseller holds up a book. Late-spring day: cream stone, zinc grey, dark green iron, one bronze tower.

### 1.4 Palette (LEGO names, from the photos)
- Stone: **Tan** (Notre-Dame, Haussmann upper floors, quays, bridge) with Light Bluish Gray for shaded tracery; **White** for the Haussmann ground floor.
- Eiffel: **Dark Tan** is the closest official colour to the sunlit bronze (within ~15 per channel); **Dark Brown** matches the shade; LEGO's own 10307 and Miniland used **Dark Bluish Gray**. Dark Tan risks washing out against the cream stone; decide on a rendered leg sample with all three side by side (landmark checkpoint). Not Reddish Brown (the 1889 colour).
- Roofs: **Dark Bluish Gray** zinc (Sand Blue as an option for the bluer zinc); chimney pots Dark Orange.
- Iron and street furniture: **Black** (balconies, lamps); **Dark Green** (bouquinistes, Morris column, Wallace fountain, café awning). Gold only as small accents (**Pearl Gold**: awning letters, one finial).
- Water: Sand Blue / Dark Bluish Gray under trans-clear. Ground: Tan sand paths, Light Bluish Gray setts, Green lawn.
- **Not**: Reddish Brown tower; bright Blue or Medium Azure water; Bright Green lawns; red or orange tile roofs (that is Prague); Dark Tan as stone (pre-restoration grime); a red awning (Kyoto already owns red); a tricolour flag (an unlabelled flag is set dressing that cannot be read — rule 6).

### 1.7 Lettering culture (rule 1, L2)
- Café awnings carry the name on the valance in **gold serif capitals painted on dark green** (Les Deux Magots). Recorded choice: one valance tile reading **CAFÉ** in a Didone-style serif (the Paris shopfront letter), Pearl Gold on Dark Green, `kanji()` at `px ≥ 320`; no invented café name.
- Street plates (blue enamel, white letters, green border) and "METROPOLITAIN" are iconic but too long to read at thumbnail → not used.
- Morris column posters: colour blocks with one bold word at most ("OPÉRA"); if it does not read at thumbnail it becomes plain colour panels.
- Eiffel's 72 gold names: unreadable at our scale → the frieze is a Pearl Gold line of 1×1 tiles, no letters.

### 1.13 Eiffel Tower — analysis and scheme v1 (signed off 2026-10-03)
- **References at our size** (`paris-moc/NOTES.md` §1, §10.2): 21019 (30 cm: clip-leaned legs, grille tiles, hose arches), Ngoc's ≈ 35 cm design (**X-panel faces on hinged lower legs, liftarm chords for L1 → L2**, X-panel shaft — the best readable lattice at this size, but no arch), Spencer_R (≈ 45 cm: legs of flex tubes, the only continuous curve), Wolfson (stacked slopes: reads as a pylon — what not to do), 10181 (stepped legs + lattice-fence strips: reads as steps), 10307 (vocabulary), Miniland, the 2024 tan tower on the Seine quay (colour evidence: warm tan reads right in daylight next to cream stone). gpt refs: `paris-eiffel-lego-a.png` (Dark Tan, whole tower: keep the proportions of the bands and the grille shaft; legs too straight), `paris-eiffel-lego-b.png` (Dark Brown close-up: keep the heavy L1 band with gold tiles, the lattice-fence gallery, the arch with a lacy edge, tan stepped footings).
- **Proportions in our units** (H = 108 plates = 43 studs): outer leg corners **16 studs** apart at the ground (16 / 43 = 0.37; real 0.378); legs 3 × 3 at the ground (real pier 25 m), inner gap 10 (real 74 m → 9.5 × 43/40). **L1 at 19 plates** (0.175 H), platform 10 × 10 with the gallery overhang (real 70.7 m → 9.2). **L2 at 38**, 6 × 6 (real 41 m → 5.3). **Top platform at 91**, 2 × 2. Campanile + mast to 108.
- **Leg lean**: the concave outer edge as a **polyline of four straight segments per leg** — ground → 9 plates (≈ 58° from horizontal), 9 → L1 (≈ 66°), L1 → L2 (≈ 76°), L2 → top (≈ 86°) — each a box girder built upright in an LDraw assembly and rotated about a **locking hinge pair** (44301 / 44302) at its foot (the MBS-core precedent; 21019, 21044 and Ngoc do the same), exploded into real bricks (rule 12). A continuous curve from flex tubes (Spencer_R) would need bent-hose geometry the bake cannot make today; noted, not planned.
- **Arches**: one **Arch 1 × 12 × 3** (14707 / 6108) per face: 10-stud opening, 9-plate rise ≈ half the L1 height (real ratio); every Eiffel build without the arch reads as a pylon (§10.2), so it is non-negotiable. It stands in the face plane one stud inside the outer edge at its springing, its ends tied to the inner leg chords; a row of 1 × 1 round plates on its inner edge stands for the lace (gpt ref B), dropped if it reads as noise.
- **L1 band** (the identity): a 10 × 10 ring, open in the middle, 4 plates deep: a course of 45° inverted slopes as corbels under it, a band plate with a row of **Pearl Gold 1 × 1 tiles** for the 72 names, then the gallery = **lattice fence 1 × 4 × 1** (3633) on a 1-stud overhang with 1 × 1 round posts at the corners.
- **L2**: a 6 × 6 ring, 2 plates + a low lattice-fence rail.
- **L1 → L2**: four leg chords of **Technic thin liftarms** (the row of holes reads as rivetted girders — Ngoc), open between them; the Λ-slot between the legs stays open (real).

## 2. Build notes (country-specific techniques)
- **Eiffel Tower** (`tools/ldraw/asm-paris-eiffel.mjs`, one assembly exploded into its bricks; test scene `?scene=fr-lm&only=eiffel`).
  - Round 1 (judge 5.5): leg = leaning box girder of four fences; arches stood vertical outside the leaning legs and read as hoops ("ears" in the front elevation). A leaning box meets the ground with one corner, so its feet looked dropped.
  - Round 2 (judge 6.4): **built from the outside in**: every face of a stage is one plane leaning back by φ (base 30°, L1→L2 16°); the legs are where two planes meet. In each plane: a lattice strip per leg parallel to the corner line, and in the base stage the curved-top Arch 1 × 12 × 5 between the legs. The strips sit two studs in from the corner and one stud behind the face so the arch's legs can stand in front of them; the corner line carries a chord. This is what makes the front elevation read as the real A with the arch under L1.
  - Round 3: shaft as two more leaning stages — corner chords + one centre strip per face (open between), then chords alone closing to the top platform (no ledge on the way); corner chords are plates laid along the corner with their studs out (10307's rivets) instead of punched liftarms; a lattice band across each face between the arch's crown and L1; L1 one plate thinner, the gold-name frieze alternating by distance from the corner so all four corners match; L2 8 × 8 with a fence rail; footing studs tiled except under the feet.
  - Rounds 4–5 (Eric's reviews, no judge): 21019's clip-and-handle sticks (v4) and a solid SNOT spire core; Eric: the sticks were not hooked, the top plate too long, the legs ugly, the top solid instead of open.
  - **v7 (2026-10-03), rebuilt from the connections out.** Legs: the 2 × 2 × 5 lattice support on ball joints at both ends (ball plate on the stone/deck, socket plate under the girder; ball plate on its studs, socket plate hung under the next deck); the lean is solved so both joints sit on the parts' own ball centres and the head lands on the deck grid. Corners are **mirrored**, not rotated (v6's rotation made a pinwheel). Upper shaft: **four Support 1 × 6 × 10 Girder Triangular** (64449) leaning in on hinge-brick pairs at both ends, capped by a 4 × 4 plate — the crane-jib truss is the one mould that is an Eiffel face in one piece. Arches: Arch 1 × 12 × 5 on hinge bricks at the footprint edge, leaning back 26° with the faces, outside the legs. Footprint grew 18 → 20 (Eric's "grow, don't squeeze") so the arches clear the legs. 260 bricks, tip 111.5 plates, L1 0.19 H, L2 0.38 H.
  - Geometry lessons: (1) thin diagonal legs cross every vertical plane through the face, so a vertical arch either clashes with a leg or stands outside it as an "ear"; the arch must lean with the face, outside the legs. (2) Four leaning panels whose bottoms meet at a corner collide as soon as they lean, unless their edges recede faster than the faces close: the triangular girders' feet stand on jumpers half a stud past the L2 deck edge. (3) `check-assemblies.ts` tests the triangular girder in quarter-stud columns sampled along its edges (its chords are long quads with vertices only at the ends).
