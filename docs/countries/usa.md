### 1.1 Scene in one sentence
A late-summer afternoon on one slice of LA, from the sea to the hills: the **Pacific** fills the front-left corner as a gentle concave bay, the **Santa Monica Pier** runs out over it on dark timber piles with the white **Pacific Wheel** (the hero) and the yellow West Coaster; a very wide **tan beach** with a baby-blue **lifeguard tower** and a surfer coming out of the surf; behind it the **Venice boardwalk** under a row of tall thin fan palms — the sunken **skate bowl**, two **graffiti art walls** on the sand, the fenced **Muscle Beach** pen with a lifter; at the foot of the hills a short strip of **Walk of Fame** stars; and in the back-right the golden-brown **Hollywood Hills** with the white **HOLLYWOOD** letters on their south face, the radio mast on the summit, and **Griffith Observatory** on the lower spur. Four people, every one of them moving: surfing, skating, lifting, roller-skating.

Why this works for Eric's brief: everything in front of the hills is outdoors, in the sun and in motion; the only buildings are an amusement pier and an observatory. It reads as LA from the two signatures nobody can miss (the letters on the hill, the wheel over the sea) and *feels* like LA from the beach life between them.

### 1.4 Island outline and ground (Option A "true diagonal", from `la-real` §1.3)
- **Outline from the real axis.** Sea and pier south-west → beach → boardwalk → hills north-east. North stays at the back; the **coast cuts the front-left corner as a concave arc** (Santa Monica Bay) and the **hills fill the back-right**, rising as terraced earth. The pier runs **west along the grid axis** (bricks stay on the grid; the real pier points south-west) out over the bay and off the island's edge, as if it continues; the wheel's plane is parallel to the deck, so the default south camera sees it face-on, as from the beach. The real sign faces south, so it faces the default camera with no cheating. Rejected: sea along the whole front (the pier points at the camera and foreshortens to a stub); sea on the west edge (the beach is seen edge-on).
- **Ground per district:** sea (Sand Blue + Dark Blue band under Trans-Clear, Sand Green nearshore, 2–3 broken White surf lines) → wet sand 1–2 studs Dark Tan → dry sand **Tan** (the widest band) → bike path Dark Bluish Gray → Ocean Front Walk Light Bluish Gray tiles with palms in sand pockets → a lawn strip Green + Olive Green → Hollywood Blvd sidewalk Light Bluish Gray with the stars → hills Dark Tan + Olive Green / Dark Green scrub, Medium Nougat trail → observatory terrace White / Light Bluish Gray, lawn panels Green.
- **Size (first estimate; the element sketches set the final number):** ≈ **80 × 56** (≈ 4,000 studs² of land and water) — above the 3,500 default per rule 13, because the wheel alone needs Ø 22 with a 14-wide pier and the hills need room to read as hills. Base 3, two bricks of earth, capped stone plinth one cell outside, as the series.

#### 1.12.0 What the sketches changed (one scale for the set)
- **Pacific Wheel Ø 25.6, not 22.** A 20-gon with real joints needs whole-stud chords: 3 → Ø 19.2 (plates on single studs), **4 → Ø 25.6 on Technic** (10247 / 31389 route, true pin pivots → level gondolas). Top ≈ G + 83.
- **The West Coaster sets the pier.** The only official coaster curve is 90° R12, so a U-turn is 27.4 studs wide; a closed oval round the wheel needs a **32 × 48 park deck** → pier ≈ **32 × 62**. Decision 2.
- **Hills ≈ 50 × 36 and the letters beside Griffith, not behind it:** from the south camera Griffith hides anything behind it below ≈ 42 plates; in reality the Observatory is south-east of the sign. **Griffith's terrace drops to G + 8** so its dome (37, finial 39) stays below the letters (31–42.5), the real order. Griffith with lawn 26 × 26; main dome 10 studs so it stays ≈ 2× the 5-stud slit domes; **five windows per wing** (every photo; the seven came from a MOC).
- **Skate bowl 19 × 12** (a 4-stud flat floor for the skater), floor at −5 plates. Inverted curved slopes give convex walls and 43085/43086 are too tall: walls are shallow cheese slopes low + 45° slopes high + a raised coping ring.
- **Lifeguard tower 23 plates, art walls 17** (a Brick 1×6×5 per wall).
- **Palms are their own kind** (`palm('fan')`): the classic palm leaves reach 7–17 studs (a coconut crown); Washingtonia = jumper → Dark Brown + 5 Dark Tan 1×1 round bricks → Reddish Brown inverted 2×2 cone skirt (49309) → Olive Green 32607 + Green 30176 pinwheels → 6255 cap; ≈ 35 plates, crown ≈ 6.3 studs, 18 parts.
- **Pipeline change adopted: a wrist turn for minifig hands** (real minifig hands rotate in the wrist; our rig cannot, so held props stay upright). Needed for the surfer's under-arm carry and the lifter's overhead press; `tools/ldraw/minifig-asm.mjs` gains a quarter-turn wrist and an arms-up pose; props get pre-rotated bakes. ## 2. Build notes (country-specific techniques)
Element build notes live beside their references: `docs/refs/la-santa-monica/BUILD.md` (pier, wheel, coaster, arch, 66), `la-hollywood-sign/BUILD.md` (letters, mast, hill technique), `la-griffith/BUILD.md` (observatory, Walk of Fame), `la-venice/BUILD.md` (bowl, art walls, Muscle Beach, tower, walk), `la-real/BUILD.md` (fan palm, figures, wrist turn). Island: `src/scenes/la-island.ts`, judge reports `docs/refs/la-island/build/judge-r*.md`. The lessons that hold for every country:
- **Copy proven builds after two failed guesses** worked four times here: Brickman's studs-up letters (after a clip-frame version read as noise), LEGOLAND's radial palm burst (after umbrella and shaving-brush crowns), the official 60328 tower proportions, LEGOLAND's Griffith.
- **A mould must exist in the colour** (Griffith): 5906 / 88293 / 86500 are not made in Reddish Brown; the telescope domes are 3960 dishes. Check BrickLink colour lists before the scheme commits to a colour.
- **Assembly proxies baked as a box** (`bake.mjs --hull`): a 340-member assembly's merged proxy mesh was 16 MB; nothing renders the proxy, so it is 12 triangles now.
- **Hills are solid** (a hollow shell left 125–436 floating parts), and every riser carries a slope in its facing direction so no flank reads as a stepped wall.
