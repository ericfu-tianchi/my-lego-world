# Singapore · Marina Bay — country notes (research approved by Eric 2026-09-18; build starts next session)

## 1. Research (before building)

### 1.1 Scene in one sentence
A floating garden island around **Marina Bay**: Marina Bay Sands as the hero on the back skyline, a grove of three Supertrees as the middle tier, the Merlion spouting into a bay that cuts into the island, a Helix-style footbridge across the inlet, a row of pastel Peranakan shophouses with a hawker stall in front, rain tree and palms, orchids; three people (a hawker frying, a tourist photographing the Merlion, an ice-cream uncle at his cart), two otters on the bank. Day, humid green, white and pastel against dark trans glass.

### 1.3 Palette (LEGO names)
- Primary: **White** and **Light Bluish Gray** (towers, promenade, Merlion, canopies), **Trans-Black** (glass).
- Secondary: **Magenta** + **Dark Purple** (Supertrees), **Dark Azure / Dark Blue** under **Trans-Light Blue** (bay), **Bright Pink / Medium Azure / Bright Light Yellow** (shophouses) with **Dark Orange** roofs.
- Accents: **Red** (hawker roof, stools), **Lavender / Magenta** orchids, **Dark Pink** bougainvillea, **Green / Dark Green / Lime** foliage, **Tan** sand of the five-foot way.
- **Not**: Bright Red as a primary (that is Kyoto's), Dark Turquoise roofs, a third lawn green.

### 1.4 Scale, layout, tiers
- Island 56×44 as Kyoto; the bay inlet enters from the front edge and pools at the centre; MBS on the back-right skyline, the Supertree grove back-left, shophouses + hawker on the west shore, Merlion on the east promenade spouting west into the bay, Helix bridge across the inlet's narrow neck at the front, rain tree front-right, palms flanking the Merlion.
- Tiers: MBS (~50 plates) > Supertrees (36) > shophouses (24) > figures. Landmarks compressed; five-foot way, stall, stools, cart at minifig scale.
- Prints (≤ 12): 3 faces, 3 torsos (hawker apron, Peranakan kebaya, tourist tee), hawker signboard 「鸡饭」, shophouse plaque (Chinese shop name gold on black + English below), ice-cream cart side, Majolica tile band → 10.
- Lettering culture: hawker signboards are **printed** bold Hei/Song on yellow or red; shophouse plaques are gold Chinese calligraphy on black plus English serif; Merlion and MBS carry no text.

### 1.8 Merlion — rebuild research (2026-09-19, after v2 was judged "完完全全看不出")
**Why v2 failed.** A 2-wide tower with a printed face: no mane mass, no forward lean, no wave base; the jet was a vertical clear pole; a tail fin rose *behind* like a ramp. Nothing in the silhouette said "lion head over fish body". Root cause: built from memory, no references (Eric, 2026-09-19: 我觉得你前期的调研没有做足).

**Real statue** (Merlion Park; 8.6 m, 70 t, white; head 2.3 × 1.7 m) — `docs/refs/singapore-merlion-real-profile.jpg`, `docs/refs/merlion-moc/real-merlion-side-profile-night-wikimedia.jpg`:
- Profile is a forward-leaning **C**: the muzzle is the most forward point, the chin recedes, the chest bulges, the belly narrows, the foot curls *forward* into the waves (the tail fin with its round hole sits at the foot on the water side — not a fin rising behind).
- Head + mane ≈ 40 % of the statue height; the flat-cut mane hem at ≈ 50 %; body 60 %.
- Depth (front–back) ≈ 0.45–0.5 × height at the head + mane, ≈ 0.3 at the body; width ≈ 0.2 × height → tall, narrow, deep.
- Mane = **three stepped tiers of vertical ridges** sweeping back and down; the crest is highest at the top back; the head top slopes down to the brow; small ears.
- The jet leaves **horizontally** from the open mouth, arcs, lands about one statue-height out in a splash.
- Base: a low **blue/white stylised wave plinth** (≈ 1/6 of the height) on a grey stone drum — the only colour on the monument.

**Brick versions studied** (`docs/refs/merlion-moc/NOTES.md`, images alongside):
- Eric's LEGO-compatible model (`docs/refs/singapore-merlion-brickmodel-eric.png`): head ≈ 45 % of height; mane = a hood of layered plates draping to mid-body; scales = 1×1 round plates studs-out; jet = trans-light-blue 1×1 plates in a parabola; blue/white curved-slope waves; **no protruding tail**.
- Rebrickable MOC-193738 (brickbicycle, ~4 wide × 14 bricks): mane = plates stepped back down the spine; muzzle of wedge plates; 1×1 round-tile eyes; nozzle = a 1×1 round brick in the mouth.
- MOC-155340 (DonnaxNL): cheese + 1×2 curved slopes stepped down the back; flower-plate scales on SNOT; trans tube jet into a flower-stud splash; blue/white wave plinth on a grey drum. Closest to our register.
- MOC-23376 (18 parts): a white curved head leaning forward + a horizontal trans-blue jet + a grey plinth is *all* it takes to read.
- LEGOLAND Miniland: plates only, all white, mane a plain rounded slab, black round-plate eyes, dark mouth recess, striped wave plinth — the minimum that still reads.
- Ethan Yee (LEGO Certified Store competition, RWS diorama): vertical-ridge mane of long curved slopes, forward lean; jet omitted.
- 21057 Singapore has **no** Merlion; no official LEGO Merlion exists.

**Borrowed / ruled out.** Borrowed: forward lean + mane mass + horizontal jet + wave plinth (every build); stepped-back mane tiers (193738, 155340); vertical ridges (Ethan Yee) done stud-down as 1×1 round-brick columns; foot curl forward (all). Ruled out: SNOT scales and tile eyes (rule 3 — eyes and scales become prints); a tail fin rising behind (wrong anatomy); a vertical jet pole.

## 2. Build notes (country-specific techniques)
_Element stage, 2026-09-18 (test scene `?scene=sg-parts`, one element with `?only=<tag>`; builders in `src/scenes/singapore.ts`, shared grammar in `lib.ts`)._

- **New moulds baked (LDraw):** otter 67631, frypan 4528, ice-cream scoops 6254, ice-cream cone 11610 (added: the scoops need a stud under them, and the cone's shaft is what a hand grips), popsicle 30222, palm trunk 2536b, palm leaves 2518 / 6148, shutter 3856, plate 4×4 cut corner 30503, wheel plate 4600 + rim 4624. Two assemblies of real parts in fixed relations: `shutter_closed` (3856 turned into the frame plane, hinge on −x, flush against the frame's +z face, sharing the frame's origin — hosted like the lattice pane) and `cart_wheel_l/r` (4624 on each pin of 4600, sharing the plate's origin). New hosted pairs are registered in the validator's `HOSTS`.
- **Rejected after baking:** dish 4×4 inverted 3960 — in the only orientation our grid allows it is a dome, not the funnel a Supertree needs; palm top 2566b — its four leaf slots need a 25° tilt, and used as a pedestal its slot lips read as a claw holding a disc; flame 6126b — lies on its side in LDraw (needs an x-rotation) and at 26 mm is a torch, not a stove flame.
- **Palm (`palm()` in lib.ts, reusable):** 2×2 round plate base (the trunk's pin in its centre hole) → 2536b segments stacked at exactly 4 plates (the segment body is 4 plates; the nesting cup shows as the classic ring notch) → a 3L bar hidden in the last segment's core (the hole meant for the top's pin) → 1×1 round plate → 2×2 round-plate hub → two large leaves opposite, two small leaves crossing one plate up (each leaf base is a 1×2 plate) → two coconuts and a young 3-leaf frond. Leaf blades are not collision-checked (only their bases are), so clearance is a render check.
- **Rain tree = `tree(kind 'raintree')`:** same armature as Kyoto's sakura; foliage Green / Dark Green / Bright Green, three leaves deep, no flowers; trunk 7 segments and the widest tier on top (all four limbs 4 long) so it reads as the flat umbrella of *Samanea saman*. ≈ 33 plates: taller than the shophouse, below the Supertrees.
- **Supertree:** 2×2 jumper footing → 1×1 round bricks Magenta / Dark Purple with a plate-thin element after every brick (3-leaf plate, flower plate, round plate in Green / Lime = the clinging ferns), so all trunks share 4-plate boundaries and the skywalk (a 1×8 plate) threads through two trunks 7 studs apart → jumper back to the grid → two 2×2 round bricks (the flare) → 4×4 round plate (Dark Purple lattice rim) → four 2×2 round pads each on two rim studs, one stud out, with green round plates → the funnel reads 6 wide and open. A bare 4×4 disc read as a lamp post (the brief's taboo). Heights 38 / 34 / 30 plates for 7 / 6 / 5 bricks.
- **Shophouse terrace:** three 5-wide bays (white pilaster + 4 interior), 6 deep + a five-foot-way row under white round columns, front toward the bay. Ground floor 12 plates (two 1×2×3 frames per bay as folding timber doors; the middle shop stands open, the others shuttered in Dark Brown; a 3-plate fascia carries the plaque); white cornice slab; upper floor 10 plates with one white-framed window and Dark Green louvred shutter centred between plaster; Dark Orange 33° hip roof through `hipCorners`. 31 plates to the ridge. Colours: Bright Pink / Medium Azure / Bright Light Yellow (Koon Seng Road). A 4-wide bay could not hold a symmetric window and door, so bays are 5.
- **Hawker stall:** four white round-brick posts (5 bricks), white 1×6 beams front and back (a roof strip with no post under it floats), red plate canopy, yellow 1×4 signboard brick on the canopy's front edge; counter = 1×4 brick + two plates (5 plates) so the wok held in a forward hand (pose `f`, pan level at 6.3 plates) clears a Trans-Orange flower-plate burner; the wok is wider than its cell, so nothing stands beside the burner. Tables = round-brick post + 2×2 plate (tube over stud), one bowl or cup each (bowls and cups are wider than a stud and clash diagonally), red and blue round-brick stools.

**Pipeline change first — exploded assemblies (no more monoliths).** Assemblies (`tools/ldraw/asm-*.mjs`) keep computing real positions, but the scene must contain every brick:
1. `bake.mjs` for a `.ldr` also writes a member list `src/parts/ldraw/asm/<name>.json`: `[{ id, color, p: [x, y, z], m: [9] }]` in our frame (LDraw → ours: scale 1/20, y and z flipped, same as the geometry bake; colour = LDraw code → our key: 15 White, 0 Black, 4 BrightRed, 33 TransDarkBlue, 43 TransLightBlue, 26 Magenta, 85 DarkPurple, 72 DarkBluishGray, 71 LightBluishGray, 2 Green, 288 DarkGreen, 10 BrightGreen, 27 Lime).
2. Every member id gets a catalogue entry `ld_<id>` via `ldrawPart` with a new anchor `'raw'` (geometry in the LDraw frame, no centring, no lift), so geometry × member transform reproduces the LDraw placement exactly. Bake each id once (`bake.mjs <id>`).
3. `Placement` gains `xform?: { p: [3], m: [9] }`, `member?: string` (group id) and `proxy?: true`. `bp.assembly(key, x, y, z, rot)` pushes one proxy placement of the existing baked assembly part (the validator keeps checking the group as one box, as today) plus N member placements transformed by the scene position/rotation.
4. Renderer: a member composes its instance matrix from `xform` instead of the grid; `half` for physics = half extents of the member's transformed bbox. Proxies are not rendered and not simulated; members are skipped by the validator. `stats.ts` counts members, not proxies; the HUD part count likewise.
5. Then MBS, Merlion and the Supertree canopy all come out as real bricks: "Take it apart" drops them one by one.
