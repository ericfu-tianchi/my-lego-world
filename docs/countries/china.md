### 1.1 Scene in one sentence
The Huangpu runs straight through the middle of the island from front to back, and the two banks face each other across it: on the **east (right) bank, Pudong** — the Oriental Pearl Tower at the water's edge and behind it the **trio**, Jin Mao, the World Financial Center and the Shanghai Tower, rising in steps, over a riverside lawn; on the **west (left) bank, Puxi** — the **Bund**'s granite row (HSBC dome, Customs House clock tower, Peace Hotel's green pyramid) along a curving promenade, and behind it a **shikumen lane** with laundry over it. Late autumn, golden plane trees on the Puxi side. Three people: an auntie fan-dancing on the Bund, a resident pegging out laundry in the lane, an office worker in a suit on the Pudong riverside.

Why the split works: it is how Shanghai is seen and remembered — a century of architecture looking at itself across 400 m of water (1920s granite, low and horizontal, against 1990s–2010s glass, tall and vertical). The contrast of the two halves is the composition; the river is the calm centre (rule 5).

### 1.3 Palette (LEGO names, from the photographs)
- Pudong (cool, glass): **Light Bluish Gray** / **Flat Silver** (Pearl columns and rings, Jin Mao, Shanghai Tower), **Sand Blue** + **Trans-Clear** (SWFC, Shanghai Tower glass), **Magenta** (the Pearl's glass only).
- Puxi (warm, stone and brick): **Tan** (Bund granite; to verify on a swatch — the real stone is a greyer beige than Tan), **Light Bluish Gray** (HSBC dome), **Dark Bluish Gray** (shikumen brick, tile roofs, flood wall), **Dark Red** (shikumen bands and pediments), **Dark Green** + **Dark Orange** (Peace Hotel pyramid and trim), **Black** (doors, rail).
- Shared: **Yellow / Bright Light Orange / Dark Orange** plane leaves, **Red** (the fan), Green base + Olive Green patches for autumn grass.
- Water: **Trans-Clear over Sand Green / Dark Bluish Gray**.
- **Not**: imperial Yellow or glazed Green roofs, Bright Red palace walls (Beijing / Kyoto), Dark Turquoise, a Medium Azure "tropical" river, dragons, rows of red lanterns.

### 1.5 Prints (≤ 12 designs, ≤ 40 pieces) and lettering culture
- Planned 8: faces × 3, torsos × 3 (auntie's tracksuit jacket, resident's cardigan, office worker's suit and tie), Customs House clock face (white dial, black Roman numerals, ×4 pieces), lane arch name 「步高里」 (a real 1930 shikumen lane; carved into the stone and filled red, Wei-bei / Kai script).
- Lettering culture: lane names are **carved into the arch stone and painted**, never typeset; Bund buildings carry **no lettering** (the clock is the only print a Bund facade needs); Pudong towers carry none.

#### 1.9.0 One scale for the set — why the island grows
- **Houses are minifig scale and cannot shrink**: a minifig door is 18 plates on its own, so a two-storey shikumen is ≈ 42 plates to the ridge (v2 assumed 24 — wrong). The height tiers (hero > tallest tree > house > figures) then force everything above it up: plane trees ≈ 50, the Bund above the trees, Pudong above the Bund.
- **Pudong keeps its true plan proportions** (slender, research §trio: ST 6.5–7 : 1, SWFC 6 : 1, Jin Mao 6 : 1) and the true height ratios between the four towers. **Shanghai Tower 110 plates** → SWFC 86, Pearl 82, Jin Mao 74 (632 : 492 : 468 : 420). Widths follow: ST 6-stud base, SWFC 6-stud wide face, Jin Mao 5, Pearl spheres 4–5 studs (0.13–0.15 H, between Brickman and Miniland; 6 studs would need ≈ 95 plates at Miniland's ratio — rejected as fatter than every reference).
- **Bund above the trees**: Customs House 66 (block 30 + tower), Peace Hotel 64, HSBC block 32 with its dome to ≈ 50. Fronts keep the real bay count at one stud per bay (HSBC 17 bays, Customs 7, Peace 5); HSBC is the only compressed width (true ≈ 34 studs at this height → 20).
- Tiers: ST 110 > SWFC 86 > Pearl 82 > Jin Mao 74 > Customs 66 > Peace 64 > plane tree ≈ 52 > HSBC dome ≈ 50 > shikumen ≈ 42 > figures 12. The skyline steps up from left (Puxi) to right (Pudong).
- **Island ≈ 84 × 54 = 4,536 studs²** (1.8 × Kyoto; above the 3,500 default per rule 13 / Eric 2026-10-02) — the width is the sum of the footprints below, not a target; height cap 0.6 × 84 studs = 126 plates ≥ ST 110 ✓. **Parts ≈ 5,500** (island + plinth ≈ 2,000, landmarks ≈ 3,100, trees ≈ 350, life layer ≈ 80; Singapore is 3,730).
