# Gyeongbokgung: Gwanghwamun gate and Geunjeongjeon throne hall, reference notes
2026-10-06 · research only, no code · images in this folder · [M] = from memory, not verified in a source this session · [est] = estimated from a photo, not published

**Verdict up front:** build **Gwanghwamun** as the single palace landmark: base **26 × 12 studs**, ≈ 30 × 18 with eaves and haechi, **≈ 47 plates** to the ridge ornaments, **≈ 1,900 parts**. Geunjeongjeon only reads on its two-tier balustraded platform, and that platform is 1.75 × the hall's width; it does not fit 20 × 16 at minifig scale (§5).

**Two corrections to the brief:**
1. **Gwanghwamun's roof is hipped (우진각지붕), not hip-and-gable.** All four faces slope and there is no gable triangle. Only Geunjeongjeon has the hip-and-gable roof (팔작지붕), with a red board gable on each side (`real-geunjeongjeon-side.jpg`).
2. **Columns are a deep iron-oxide red (석간주), not bright red.** Sampled at 153,70,50 in sun and 40,22,23 in shade, which maps to **Dark Red** (Reddish Brown is the fallback). Miniland's Bright Red is a toy colour.

### 0.1 Gwanghwamun (광화문)
**Published** (Encyclopedia of Korean Culture E0002439, search snippets): the gate-house is **3 bays × 2**, two storeys, multi-bracket (다포), **hipped roof**. The ground floor is 174.1 m² and the upper 110.7 m². **Stone base 7 m** high. **Centre arch 18 × 17.5 尺 (≈ 5.45 m wide × 5.3 m high), side arches 14.5 × 16 尺 (≈ 4.4 × 4.85 m).** Front column bays are 25 / 27 / 25 尺. Every ridge carries 취두 (ridge-end ornament), 용두 and 잡상 (japsang, the hip figurines). Rebuilt in 2010. The plaque was replaced on **2023-10-15** and is now **black ground with gold letters** [M]. A forecourt terrace (월대) was restored in front at the same time, and the haechi were moved to its front corners [M].

**Measured from `real-gwanghwamun-piotrus.jpg`.** Scale: centre arch 160 px = 5.45 m, so 29.4 px/m. The camera stands slightly left, so the right half is a little compressed.

- **Ratios that matter:** base ≈ **0.45 of total height**; base W/H ≈ 3.5; the **lower eave is wider than the base** and the **upper eave is about as wide as the base**; the upper ridge is half the base width. The centre arch is 1.24 × the side arches in width, and all three are round-topped (홍예) on short piers.
- **Eave curve (앙곡) and corner rise (추녀):** the eave line is flat across the middle third and sweeps up into the corners. The upper eave rises **68 px (≈ 2.3 m) over a 517 px half-span, about 13 %**. The lower eave rises **40 px (≈ 1.4 m), about 7 %**. The upper ridge sags slightly between two raised 취두. In plan the corners also push outward (안허리) [M, visible in `real-gwanghwamun-pablin.jpg`].
- **Japsang:** a short row of grey clay figures sits on each hip of both roofs. About **6–7 per hip** are visible in the photo [est].
- **Yangseong (양성):** all ridges, main and hips, are white lime plaster. In overcast light they sample at 196,194,189, the same value as the granite. This is the clearest Korean tell (§3).
- **Plan / site:** the gate sits in the middle of the palace wall (궁장), with walls running off both sides and corner turrets beyond. A pair of **haechi (해치)** stands in front on plinths, each ≈ 3 m with plinth [est]. **Bugaksan** rises directly behind in the classic view. The **pavilion is narrower than the base** (≈ 0.7 W) and sits centred on it, with a walkway round it behind the parapet.

### 0.2 Geunjeongjeon (근정전)
**Published** (ko.wikipedia via fetch): **5 × 5 bays**, two storeys, **hip-and-gable roof**, **7 japsang on every hip of both roofs**, columns 16 尺. It stands on a **two-tier platform (상·하월대)** with carved balustrades, the 12 zodiac animals and other beasts on the posts, and a triple stair (centre royal ramp) at front and back plus side stairs.

## 1. LEGO Architecture 21016 Sungnyemun (2012), Adam Reed Tucker · official, micro (325 pieces, 17.6 × 10.7 cm)
URL: https://brickset.com/sets/21016-1 · inventory via https://www.brickmissing.com/llms/sets/21016-1-sungnyemun.md · file `official-21016-box.jpg`
- **The only official Korean building, and the same type as Gwanghwamun**: a Joseon gate, hipped two-storey roof, on a granite base with a round arch.
- **Base:** LBG plates, bricks and panels (`4865a`, `43337`, `6231`) laid as ashlar with darker grey accents. The arch is a single **`3659` Arch 1×4** (LBG ×2). Dark Green slopes form the hillside walls.
- **Roof:** flat stacked **Black / Dark Bluish Gray plates** (`3832`, `3795`, `3034`). The tile texture is **18 × `2540` Plate 1×2 with Handle in DBG** along the eaves.
- **Upturned corners:** **`6019` Plate 1×1 with Clip Horizontal** (Black ×9) at the corners. The clip's curl reads as the hip tip.
- **Yangseong / japsang:** **24 × `6141` White round 1×1** along the ridges and hips. **Ridge ends:** **`59900` Cone 1×1 Black ×2** as the 취두.
- **Dancheong:** a striped band of **Dark Red, Dark Tan, Dark Green 1×2 plates** (`3023`) and Dark Green 1×4 tiles under each eave.
- **Lesson:** at micro scale the gate reads from **grey base + dark layered roofs + white ridge dots + a green/red band**. It has **no eave curve**: the eaves are straight plates. We must do better at minifig scale, but its colour layering is the official LEGO reading of a Korean gate.

## 2. ABSAddict, "Seoul Korea South Gate (Sungnyemun)" MOC (2020) · ≈ 1:88, scaled from a 1×6×2 arch
URL: https://www.eurobricks.com/forum/forums/topic/177911-moc-seoul-korea-south-gate-sungnyemun/ · blog https://castlelego.blogspot.com/search?q=sungnyemun · files `absaddict-*.jpg`, `absaddict-namdaemun-*.png`
- **Base:** LBG and DBG bricks in irregular ashlar coursing, a **1×6×2 arch** (`3307`/`15254` class) as the gate tunnel, DBG grille courses at the parapet, and side ramps of stepped plates. Good granite read.
- **Roof curve:** "six build/tear-down cycles… *creative plate layering*". The eaves step up one plate towards the corners, so there is a real sag. It reads but looks stair-stepped up close (`absaddict-20200512_194551.jpg`).
- **Corners:** white 1×1 round plates stacked at the hip tips. **Japsang/yangseong:** **White 1×1 round plates** spaced along the hips and the ridge, with Black horns as 취두.
- **Dancheong:** **Orange** rafter-end slopes, Blue/Red/White 1×1 round tiles in a band, Dark Red columns. Too loud: Orange is not in the real palette.
- **Roof colour Black**, which is wrong: the real tiles are DBG.
- **Lesson:** **round-tile dots for rafter ends** under the eave read instantly as "East Asian timber eave". A **stepped-plate sag** is the first-order curve technique. The plaque is a printed 1×2 tile on clip-and-bar, angled forward like the real one, which is a good borrow.

## 5. Seon-Bi Won "Korean Tea House", Taeseok Kang (The_Limit_ts_kang), LEGO Ideas (10,000 supporters, 2025-08-14) · minifig scale
URL: https://www.brickfanatics.com/lego-ideas-project-south-korean-architecture · files `seonbiwon-ideas-1..4.jpg`
- **Not the palace, but the best Korean *roof* at our scale.** The eave **visibly sags and sweeps up into the corners** (`seonbiwon-ideas-2.jpg`). The corner is a **fan of short tile rows stepped upward**, not a single flick.
- **Tiles:** Dark Blue ribs (sugiwa) running *down* the slope over a grooved field, with round caps at the eave. Under them sits a row of **White 1×1 round rafter ends** (서까래) with Dark Blue/Bright Red tips. The ridge is a raised rib with Dark Bluish Gray end blocks (취두).
- **Body:** **Green / Sand Green bracket band** with Dark Blue and Red accents, **Reddish Brown columns**, lattice doors, and a stone plinth of LBG/White masonry.
- **Lesson:** this is the technique to borrow for the **sag + corner sweep** and the **rafter-end dots**. The designer said the roof curve was "the greatest challenge" and took 3 months. We take its colour layering but with **DBG tiles** and **Dark Red columns**.

## 6b. Official moulds for Asian roof corners
New Elementary on **`4190` Plate 2×2 with Slope Inverted Corner** (the "pagoda plate", DBG, from 21060 Himeji and 10315 Tranquil Garden). It is LEGO's own upturned-corner element. Related: `73682` Slope Curved 3×3 Corner with Inverted Cutout, `70681`, `30099` Arch 1×5×4 Inverted, `93273`, `29119/29120`. **Caution:** 4190 is the Japanese-set corner. On its own it reads Himeji, so use it only together with the Korean signals below.

## 7. Korean vs Japanese (Kyoto) roofs: what must differ
Kyoto's pagoda used straight `spanPlates` eaves one stud past the wall, a ring of slopes, `hipCorners`, and a cheese/curved-slope flick at the tips (`docs/countries/japan.md`). A Korean roof must show:
1. **A sag across the whole eave**: flat in the middle third, sweeping up through the outer thirds (≈ 7 % lower, 13 % upper of the half-span). Kyoto has a straight eave with a tip flick.
2. **White yangseong on every ridge and hip**, with a dark 취두 block at each end of the main ridge. Japanese ridges are dark tile.
3. **Japsang rows** on the hips: grey, 3–4 at our scale.
4. **Dancheong bracket band**: Sand Green / Dark Green with red-blue-white dots, plus **white rafter-end dots** under the eave. Kyoto is vermilion and white walls.
5. **Dark Red columns** (Kyoto used Bright Red), and **lighter, shallower roofs** with long overhangs. A Japanese temple roof is taller and heavier relative to the body.
6. **Granite base with round arches** (gate) or **balustraded stone terraces** (hall). Japan used plaster walls and a timber engawa.

## 9. Pick and what we borrow
**Best-looking brick version: Seon-Bi Won (§5)** for the roof. It is the only one where the eave really sags and sweeps, and it does that at minifig scale with real moulds. **21016 (§1)** gives the official colour layering for a Korean *gate*, and **jjukjang (§3)** gives the full part vocabulary. Nobody has built a good minifig-scale Gwanghwamun: §1 and §2 are Sungnyemun, §3 is the hall, and the Ideas set was not seen. The combination is ours.

**Borrow**
- Seon-Bi Won: the **sagging eave with a stepped fan into each corner**, **white rafter-end dots** under the eave, and **tile ribs running down the slope** (we use DBG `61409b` grille slopes).
- 21016: **grey ashlar base + dark layered roofs + white ridge line + a green/red band**, and the order of those bands.
- jjukjang: **Sand Green dancheong ground**, **SNOT grille-tile lattice**, **inverted 6×1 / 4×1 curved slopes** for the sweep, and **hinged roof planes** where the pitch needs it.
- ABSAddict: the **printed plaque on clip-and-bar, tilted forward**, and irregular two-grey **ashlar coursing**.

**Avoid**
- Straight eaves (§1, §3, §6): they read Japanese or generic.
- Black roofs (§2, §6), Bright Red columns (§4), Orange dancheong (§2), Bright Green (§3).
- Using `4190` alone as the corner (that is Himeji's corner).

## 10. Gate vs hall vs both
| | Gwanghwamun | Geunjeongjeon | Both |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signature | arched granite base + two hipped roofs + haechi, with Bugaksan behind: **the face of Seoul**, which pairs with Gwanghwamun Square (Sejong / Yi Sun-sin) | the throne hall on its **balustraded two-tier platform** | the axis gate → hall |
| Footprint at minifig scale | **30 × 18** with eaves and haechi | hall 12 × 8 needs platforms ≈ 21 × 22 (true 1.75 W), and the ground storey must be stretched × 2.5 for a minifig door | ≥ 30 × 45 even with the courtyard compressed (Ideas set: ≈ 43 × 28 studs at micro) |
| Parts | ≈ 1,950 | ≈ 2,800–3,500 (balustrade posts, 12 zodiac statues, 5 × 5 bays of brackets) | ≈ 5,000 |
| Reads at thumbnail | yes: three arches + two roofs | the platform reads as a grey slab unless the balustrade is detailed | — |
| Life layer | haechi + gatekeeper (수문장) changing of the guard in red/blue | officials on rank stones | — |

**Recommend Gwanghwamun.** It is front-facing and compact (3 × 2 bays), the solid base makes stud connection simple, it carries every Korean roof signal, it has the strongest silhouette, and it pairs with the Gwanghwamun Square life layer (a 수문장 guard minifig in red with a banner, or a tourist in hanbok). Geunjeongjeon is the better *building*, but its platform is the signature, and the platform costs more area than the whole gate. Both would take ≈ 40 % of a 2,500 studs² island and leave no room for Namsan, Bukchon or the Han.

## 11. Risks and open questions
1. **Hero height vs the rest of Seoul.** 47 plates is tall for a gate. If N Seoul Tower or Lotte World Tower goes in, the height table (playbook §2) must place Gwanghwamun as the "historic class" (≈ 35–50) below the towers. **Decide the Seoul height table first.**
2. **Eave sweep technique is unproven in our renderer.** The inverted curved slopes give a concave *underside* line. The top edge of the roof still needs a one-plate step in the outer thirds plus the corner flick. **Needs a test-scene render beside `seonbiwon-ideas-2.jpg` and `real-gwanghwamun-piotrus.jpg`** before committing. A hinged eave segment is the fallback.
3. **Haechi has no mould.** It must be brick-built from L1 parts; get a 2-stud sculpture to read as a seated lion-dog in two iterations, or cut it (rule 6: unreadable set dressing goes).
4. **Plaque lettering:** 光化門 is three characters, and the rule says 1–2. The real plaque *is* three, so use the real object's lettering (gold carved on black, 2023+) at `px ≥ 320` on a 1 × 4 tile, and test legibility at thumbnail. Alternatively drop it. **Eric decides.** Also **decide 2010–23 white plaque vs current black**: recommend current.
5. **Arch heights stretch ≈ 1.4×** to pass a minifig. Check `6060` (curved top, flatter) and `2339` (round) side by side; if the shapes differ too much, use `2339` for all three with the centre on a taller pier.
6. **Colour availability [M] to verify on BrickLink:** `3062b` Dark Red, `15070` DBG, `98283` LBG, `61409b` DBG (listed). `30528` Arch 1×8×6 exists only in Black/Sand Purple: not usable.
7. **Base depth (≈ 12 studs) is [est]** from oblique photos. No published depth was found. The pavilion's side span (2 bays × 10 尺 ≈ 6 m) seems too small and may be a snippet error. Treat the 8-stud pavilion depth as provisional.
8. **The 2023 forecourt (월대)** with balustrades in front of the gate is new [M]. Including it adds the Geunjeongjeon-style balustrade cost; recommend leaving it out and placing the haechi on the plaza.
9. **Miniland Korea's own Gyeongbokgung and the Yongsan store diorama** were not seen. One more image search might give a LEGO-made gate to compare against before building.
