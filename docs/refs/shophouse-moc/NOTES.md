What to copy: each unit is **narrow and tall** — one bay roughly 6 m wide, two storeys plus a pitched **terracotta-orange tile roof** (dark orange), sometimes a small gable vent. Ground floor: **five-foot way** (a continuous covered walkway) behind square **pilasters**, then a low front wall with a gate and a tiny forecourt. Upper floor: **three tall shuttered windows** per bay, timber louvred shutters in a dark green / dark blue / white contrasting with the wall, with a fanlight or Chinese-tile band above. **Cornice**: a deep decorative band under the roof (pastel plaster mouldings, coloured glazed tiles, festoons). Strong **vertical rhythm**: pilaster – window – window – window – pilaster, repeated house after house, each house a different pastel.

- Size: three life-size bays, ~3 m tall, styled as giant bricks (stud texture printed on the wall).
- Colour scheme worth stealing: **three bays, three body colours — blue / coral-orange / lime green**, each with shutters in the *complementary* pastel (yellow on blue, yellow-and-green on coral, yellow on green), white window frames.
- Shutters: full-height **louvred double shutters** flung open, louvres rendered as horizontal slats — in bricks this is the **1×2 grille tile / 1×2 grille brick** stacked in the shutter colour, framed by **1×1 plates/tiles** in white.
- Cornice: a horizontal band in a darker tone with a **row of round studs as dentils** (bright orange band on coral; olive on green).
- Five-foot way: implied only by the front porch; the pop-up is one storey. Peranakan **glazed tile skirt** under the windows (a strip of colourful 4-colour geometric tiles — in bricks these are **1×1 tiles/plates in a 4-colour mosaic**).
- `tilewall.jpg`: the Peranakan mosaic wall — square motifs built from **1×1 round plates / 1×1 tiles** in red, pink, green, blue on a ring of white. Direct template for an L2 decorated element (a printed 2×2 tile) or a 4×4 plate mosaic.
- `fan-mocs.jpg`: the interior, with a shuttered window (green louvred shutters, yellow lower panels) and a blue-white floral tile dado.

## Build 2 — Rebrickable MOC-267766 "Shophouse stationary holder" (Legomodernist)

File: `moc-267766-legomodernist-shophouse.jpg`
Source: https://rebrickable.com/mocs/MOC-267766/Legomodernist/shophouse-stationary-holder/

- Size: one bay, about 10 studs wide × 12 studs deep, ~14 bricks tall (two storeys + roof). Directly Singapore-inspired.
- Five-foot way: the ground storey is set back one full stud row behind two **round columns / 1×1 round bricks** stacked as pilasters, leaving a walk-through arcade with a tan tiled floor — exactly the real feature.
- Shutters: upper floor has three tall openings filled with **dark-brown lattice / window frame elements** (1×2×2 windows with lattice panes); ground floor **window shutters as 1×2×3 shutter elements** in tan flanking the door.
- Cornice / awning: a **row of dark-red 1×1 round bricks or cylinders laid horizontally** under the upper windows reads as the clay-tile awning between storeys; a thin **tan 1×2 plate band with 1×1 bracket "capitals"** on each pilaster.
- Roof: **dark-red plates stepped** with **1×2 tiles standing on end as ridge tiles**, a side gable in tan.
- Colours: tan body, dark red roof/awning, dark brown windows, one red fire hydrant on the pavement.

## Build 3 — Rebrickable MOC-182259 "Pinang Peranakan Mansion" (porklegoguy)

File: `moc-182259-porklegoguy-pinang-peranakan-mansion.png`
Source: https://rebrickable.com/mocs/MOC-182259/porklegoguy/pinang-peranakan-mansion-no-interior/ (2,062 parts, 32×32 baseplate, Penang — same Straits-Eclectic vocabulary as Koon Seng Road)

- Size: 32 studs wide, two storeys, ~24 bricks tall incl. hip roof.
- Colours: **sand-green walls, white columns and cornice, red-brown louvred shutters, bright-red roof, pearl-gold ornament** — a very legible Peranakan palette.
- Five-foot way: a full-width open loggia behind four **white round columns** on both storeys; the ground floor floor is a tan/white checkerboard of 1×1 tiles.
- Shutters: **dark-brown 1×2×3 shutter elements** in pairs on every window, set into arched openings (1×2×2 arch brick over each).
- Cornice: **white 1×2 plate + 1×2 tile band** with a **gold scroll / plant-stem "filigree"** row and small vase elements as roof finials.
- Roof: **bright red plates stepped as a hip roof**, ridge higher than the eaves by about 5 plates.

## Takeaways for a 56×44-stud island (landmark ≤ 0.6 × island width, minifig life in front)

1. **Three narrow bays, three pastels.** The terrace reads as Koon Seng Road only when at least three houses stand side by side, each a different pastel (pink / sky blue / sage / salmon / lilac in official colours: bright pink, medium azure, sand green, coral-ish "medium nougat"/light orange, lavender). One house alone is just a house. Three bays at 6 studs each = 18 studs wide, well inside the 0.6 × 56 = 33-stud cap.
2. **The five-foot way is the story layer.** Set the ground storey back one stud behind square **1×1 brick pilasters** so the walkway is real: a minifig stands *under* the upper floor. This is where the 2–3 figures live (a kopi drinker, a cyclist), and it is the signature detail everyone knows.
3. **Shutters are grille tiles.** Upper-floor windows: three per bay, 1 stud wide × 3 plates tall each, filled with **1×2 grille tiles** on their side (louvre lines) in a colour that contrasts with the wall (dark green on pink, white on blue), framed by white 1×1 plates. Skip lattice-pane windows — at this scale they read as generic modular buildings.
4. **Cornice = one contrasting band with dentils.** A single **1×2 plate band** in a darker tone under the eaves with **1×1 round plates as dentils / 1×1 plates as festoons** is enough; do not stack ornament. Roof is **dark-orange plates stepped as a pitched roof**, two bays share one ridge line for regularity.
5. **One Peranakan tile print** as the L2 decorated element: a 2×2 or 1×2 tile with a 4-colour geometric motif (from `tilewall.jpg`) repeated as the dado band under the upper windows and on the forecourt wall — the only place the colour goes loud; walls stay pastel so the island is never louder than Kyoto.
