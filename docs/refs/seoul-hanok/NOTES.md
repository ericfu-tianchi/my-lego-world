## 1. Real Bukchon hanok — what it looks like

Photos: `real-bukchon-01/05/11gil-a/11gil-b/gate/slope`, `real-ginkgo-autumn`.

**Street facade, bottom to top** (the view every visitor photographs; houses turn their back wall to the alley):
1. **Stone base (gidan)** — grey granite, follows the slope in steps.
2. **Lower wall** — ~40 % of wall height: grey granite ashlar blocks with recessed mortar (`05`, `11gil-a`), or fieldstones set in brick (`slope`).
3. **Kkotdam band** — one band of red-brown brick with a **white Greek-key meander** or interlocking circles (`11gil-a/b`). The palace version (Jagyeongjeon) is a whole red-brick wall with white motifs.
4. **Upper wall** — white plaster panels between **natural honey-coloured timber posts and beams**, with **high horizontal lattice windows** (grid lattice, paper behind).
5. **Eaves** — deep, on round **rafters with their ends showing**; natural wood in Bukchon (palaces paint them).
6. **Roof** — dark grey giwa: alternating convex cover rows (sugiwa) and concave pan rows; **every cover row ends in a white mortar oval** at the eave (`real-ginkgo-autumn` is the close-up). Hip-and-gable (paljak) form; the **eave line curves up toward every corner** in plan and in section. The **ridge is heavy**, edged with a white mortar line, and its **ends kick up**.
7. **Gate (daemun)** — two natural-wood leaves with an iron ring, set in the wall under its own small tiled roof (`gate`, `05`).

The alley is grey asphalt or granite pavers, steep, and looks down over the roofs to the towers and N Seoul Tower (`01`): the composition.

**Proportions** [measured roughly on `05` and `11gil-a`, using a 1.7 m tourist]:
- wall from street to eave ≈ 3.4 m: stone 1.3 m, meander band 0.4 m, white/timber 1.7 m;
- eave projection ≈ 0.9–1.2 m;
- roof rise above the eave ≈ 0.8 × wall height (roofs are big);
- lot frontage 10–15 m.

**Colours** (photos → LEGO):

| Element | Photo | LEGO colour |
|---|---|---|
| giwa tiles | dark grey | **Dark Bluish Gray**. Not Black, which reads Japanese temple (Lejoverse); not Dark Blue (Seon-Bi Won's choice reads as toy) |
| ridge and tile-end mortar | white | **White** |
| timber | honey | **Medium Nougat**; Dark Tan for weathered |
| plaster | white | **White** |
| granite | grey | **Light Bluish Gray**, Dark Bluish Gray joints |
| brick band | red-brown | **Dark Orange** or Reddish Brown, white meander |
| gate leaves | wood | Medium Nougat, with a **Black** ring |
| paving | asphalt / granite | Dark Bluish Gray / Light Bluish Gray |

**Do not use:** Bright Red or vermilion on a house (Kyoto torii colour), or green-and-red dancheong (palaces and temples only, not Bukchon homes).

### 1.1 Hanok vs Kyoto machiya / temple: the differences that must show

| | Korean hanok (Bukchon) | Japanese (our Kyoto set) |
|---|---|---|
| Roof curve | eave **sweeps up continuously** toward each corner in plan and section; ridge ends kick up | machiya eaves straight and low; temple corners flick only at the tip (Kyoto: one-plate upturn) |
| Tile ends | **white mortar oval at the end of every cover row**; white line along the ridge | flush round end tiles, mon-stamped, no white; ridge dark |
| Rafters | round rafter ends exposed under deep eaves | hidden behind a fascia board |
| Walls | **stone/brick lower wall + patterned kkotdam band + white plaster with honey timber** | dark timber lattice (kōshi) street fronts; tea house white wall with dark posts |
| Base | raised stone gidan; **ondol**: a firebox (agungi) at the wall foot and a **chimney (gulttuk)** standing apart; narrow wooden veranda (toenmaru) | engawa deck on posts, no chimney |
| Windows | high horizontal lattice strips; paper inside (changhoji) | shoji panels full height; kōshi slats |
| Colours | grey, white, honey wood, brick red | Bright Red, Dark Red, Black, dark wood, Dark Tan thatch |
| Gate | daemun in the wall under its own tiled roof | torii (shrine), not domestic |

The **white tile-end dots, the curved eave and the kkotdam band** are what carry "Korea" at thumbnail size. If any of the three is missing, the house reads as Japanese or Chinese.

### 2.1 Chosen technique (the best of 1–6)
- **Roof planes:** hinged plates at ~30°. Use 3937 / 6134 hinge bricks (80113), or 6019 clip + 2540 handle at the eave (21016), authored as an LDraw assembly so every member is a real brick on a real hinge.
- **Tile lines:** Dark Bluish Gray **49307** in lines down the slope on every other stud column (80105 / Seon-Bi Won). The bare stud columns between them read as the pan troughs (Lejoverse).
- **Tile-end dots:** a **White 98138** "Tile 1 x 1 Round with Groove" ✓ (or 6141 ✓) at the foot of each line, studs-out on a 36840 bracket ✓ or 87087 "Brick 1 x 1 with Stud on 1 Side" under the eave. This is the signature.
- **Ridge:** Dark Bluish Gray 1 × N bricks with a White plate line under the cap and 1 × N tiles on top. **Ends up-kicked** with **11477** "Slope Brick Curved 2 x 1" ✓ (as 80106 does), mirrored on both ends.
- **Corner sweep:** this is what makes it Korean, not Japanese. The last two tile lines at each corner sit on a hinge raised one plate, and the corner tip is a **50950** "Slope Brick Curved 3 x 1" ✓ pointing out and up, placed with `hipCorners`. Kyoto lesson: the lift is 1–2 plates, never a horn.
- **Rafter ends:** Medium Nougat **11211** "Brick 1 x 2 with Two Studs on One Side" in a row under the eave, carrying **6141 Medium Nougat** rounds (natural wood). Not painted: Bukchon is residential.

## 3. Hanok element sketch at minifig scale ("Bukchon house on the slope")

**Plan (ㄱ-shape without a valley join; valleys are hard to build cleanly and Bukchon houses show the alley a straight wing anyway):**
```
 alley (slopes down →, steps of 2 plates)
 ┌───────────────────────── street wing 9 × 4 body ──┐ G G ┌ wall ┐
 │ granite / meander / lattice windows facing alley │ gate│      │
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ 2×1 └──────┘
   courtyard 8 × 5 (granite pavers) · jar terrace in one corner · red pine
 ┌──────────── anchae (main wing) 12 × 5 body on 4-plate gidan ────────┐ chimney
 │ toenmaru veranda · round posts · lattice doors · ondol firebox      │  (2×2)
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
 footprint incl. 2-stud eaves ≈ 16 × 14 studs
```

| Plates | Level | Part types (verified numbers) |

## 5. C · Trees, garden, jar terrace

**Versions:**

**Real things:**
- **Ginkgo** (은행나무) — Seoul's street tree. The **upright, egg-to-conical** crown when young (not an umbrella like sakura), pure **golden Yellow** in late October, fan-shaped leaves carpeting the paving.
  - LEGO: new `kind: 'ginkgo'` — the same armature with steeper limbs and clusters of Yellow 2423 / 2417 ✓ / 32607, a few Bright Light Orange.
  - **Fallen leaves = Yellow 25269 "Tile 1 x 1 Corner Round" ✓**: a quarter-circle *is* a ginkgo fan.
  - Trunk Dark Brown / Reddish Brown.
- **Korean red pine** (소나무) — a tall trunk with **orange-red upper bark** (Reddish Brown low, **Dark Orange** high), leaning and kinked, a flat dark-green cloud crown at the top (`real-pine-densiflora-2`).
  - LEGO: the `pine` kind with the upper trunk Dark Orange. The kink goes through jumpers only (rule 7: one axis per segment).
  - Classic image: the pine leaning over a hanok wall.
- **Zelkova** (느티나무) — the village guardian tree; broad dome, green to rust in autumn. Optional; overlaps with ginkgo in shape class.
- **Cherry** — Yeouido / Seokchon Lake, but **it reads Kyoto**. Do not use it in the Seoul set.

**Recommendation:** one hero ginkgo (gold) at the alley head + one red pine over the courtyard wall. Autumn sets the palette: gold, grey roofs, honey wood.

## 6. D · Figures: what LEGO moulds exist

**Hanbok:**
- **Real:** a short jacket (**jeogori**: White or pastel) tied with a long ribbon (**otgoreum**, contrast colour) over a **high-waisted full skirt (chima)** that starts at the chest. Rental colours at Gyeongbokgung: Bright Pink / Dark Pink chima + White jeogori, Medium Azure, Purple, Red-with-gold hem (`real-hanbok-tourists*.jpg`).
- **Skirt mould:** **36036** "Minifig Hips and Skirt" — the long skirt slope, plain mould, fine for chima, the Kyoto-era parasol pose works with it.
  - Alternatives: 24068 "Hips and Skirt Wide with Side Hoops" is a ballgown, too wide. **3678b** "Slope Brick 65 2 x 2 x 2" is the older dress-as-brick, cruder.
  - Friends legs (15875 etc.) are a different figure system: excluded.
- **Torso (L2 print):** the jeogori + otgoreum on the upper chest, chima colour from the ribs down so that it flows into the 36036 skirt.
- **Hair:** **2633** "Hair Long with Parted Bangs Swept Back in Bun", **13251** "Hair Female with Elaborate Knot Bun", **27186** "Hair with Large High Bun" ✓.
- **Props:** **30089b** "Minifig Camera Snapshot Type 2" ✓; a phone = Black 3069b with a screen print.
- **Men:** jeogori + baji (wide trousers) = plain legs + torso print. The **gat** (black horsehair hat, tall crown, wide flat brim) has no mould. The closest is **30167** "Minifig Hat Wide Brim Flat" in Black: a flat wide brim, but a dented low crown. It reads as a gat at distance. Optional.

**Royal guard (sumunjang ceremony at Gwanghwamun)** (`real-guard-*.jpg`):
- **Real:**
  - **ankle-length red robe** (some blue, yellow, green by rank), a black belt, yellow / green inner sleeves;
  - a **black wide-brim felt hat (jeollip)** with a red tassel and a tall **peacock-feather / horsetail plume**; the captain's hat is red with a gold badge;
  - a **long fake beard**;
  - a **spear / woldo glaive**, or a sword at the hip.
- **Moulds:**
  - **36036** skirt in **Red** for the ankle-length robe;
  - a torso print (red robe, black belt, chest badge, yellow cuffs);
  - head print with beard;
  - **30167** hat in Black (Red for the captain);
  - weapon: **6123** "Minifig Polearm Halberd" or **4497** "Minifig Spear with Round End"; **30173b** "Sword Katana Type 2" in its scabbard is wrong culturally — use the polearm.
  - The **plume has no legal mount on 30167** (no pin hole in the mould; 22411 "Plume Horsetail with Bar" and 30126 "Feathers with Pin" need a hole). So: **no plume**, or choose a hat with a pin (44553 "Hat Tall Hat with Small Brim and Pin" has the wrong brim).
  - The guard reads by red robe + wide black brim + halberd. Recommend accepting no plume.
  - Placement: the guard needs a palace gate (Gwanghwamun) to make sense. If the set has no palace gate, **cut the guard** (rule 6: a figure must do something legible).

**Busker (if used):** **90370** "Minifig Microphone" on a mic stand (Bar 4L 30374 ✓ + 2 × 2 round base); speaker = 2 × 2 brick with a grille tile.

## 7. Recommended vignettes and the life layer (2–3 figures, rule 6)
1. **Hanok alley** — a hanbok tourist (pink chima, bun hair) **photographing** down the alley toward the city (camera raised, side-on), at the head of the slope by the gold ginkgo.
2. **Pojangmacha** — the ajumma cook ladling tteokbokki; optional customer on a stool.
3. Either the **jar terrace** (a woman lifting a lid) or the **royal guard** (only if a palace gate is in the set).
