# The Bund — HSBC, Customs House, Peace Hotel, promenade: reference notes

2026-10-02. Research only. Images in this folder; older refs in `../shanghai-real/` and `../shanghai-21039/`.

How I measured: I put a 50 px grid over the frontal or near-frontal photos and read off pixel heights (e.g. `../shanghai-real/HSBC_Building,_the_Bund.jpg`, `real-customs-front-2017.jpg`, `../shanghai-real/Peace_Hotel.jpg`, `real-hsbc-customs-sunlit.jpg`). I cross-checked the heights against zh/en Wikipedia figures. Close street-level shots shrink anything set back, like the Customs House tower, so where a far shot and Wikipedia disagree with a close shot, the far shot and Wikipedia win. All ratios are ±10 %.

**One scale for all three** (anchored on Customs House block height 36.2 m and total 79.2 m, zh-wiki): **1 plate ≈ 2.05 m, 1 stud ≈ 5 m.** At this scale the Customs House tower comes out at 38–39 plates and the Peace Hotel at 37–38 plates. Both match the planned heights, so the scale holds.

## 0. Real buildings — proportions to hit

### Customs House (海关大楼, No. 13, 1927)
- **Heights** (zh-wiki): main block ≈ 36.2 m to the cornice, clock tower ≈ 43 m (a 5-storey square tower), total ≈ 79.2 m without the flagpole. The far photo `real-hsbc-customs-sunlit.jpg` gives block/total = 315/715 = **0.44** (zh-wiki: 0.46). The close frontal shot gives 0.67 because its set-back tower is foreshortened, so I don't use it for heights.
- **Block**: frontal W : H(cornice) = 745 : 630 px ≈ **1.18 : 1** → front ≈ 43 m ≈ **8.5 studs** wide, **≈ 18 plates** to the main cornice.
- **Facade, bottom to top** (frontal, fractions of block height): rusticated base with a **Doric portico**, 4 fluted columns between 2 square antae (5 openings) plus a mezzanine above, **≈ 0.31**; one transition storey with a cornice/balcony line, **≈ 0.13**; **four storeys of recessed window strips between giant pilasters, ≈ 0.41**; frieze and heavy cornice, **≈ 0.15**. **7 bays** on the river front: 5 recessed strips in the middle and 1 in each slightly projecting end bay. Squat **corner pavilions** sit on the roof at both ends (≈ 0.14 of block height, grey stone, not green).
- **Tower** (far shot, fractions of the 79 m total): lower square stage with flanking piers **≈ 0.28** → **clock stage ≈ 0.17** → belfry with arched openings **≈ 0.06** → small octagonal cupola **≈ 0.03** → flagpole (+≈ 0.1). Clock stage width ≈ 0.15 of block width. **Clock face 5.4 m across** (zh source) ≈ 0.12 of block width → **≈ 1.1 studs at true scale**. Clock faces on all 4 sides: white dial, black Roman numerals and rim.
- At 38 plates: block 18, lower tower 10–11, clock stage 6–7, belfry and cupola 3–4. True-scale clock stage ≈ 1.5 studs wide. A 2×2 clock on a 2–3-wide stage exaggerates the clock about 2× (21039, Wange and Miniland all do this).
- Lettering: "CUSTOM HOUSE" in gilt sans letters on the bronze entrance gate (`real-customs-gate-lettering.jpg`). It can't be read at our scale, so leave it off; the clock is the only print.

### HSBC Building (汇丰银行大楼, No. 12, 1923)
- en-wiki: **six Ionic columns** run through storeys 2–4 under a **triangular pediment** at the dome's base; granite exterior; 7 storeys (zh-wiki); site 9,438 m². I found no published frontage figure. Measured frontal **W : H(parapet) ≈ 985 : 327 ≈ 3 : 1**.
- **Bays**: **17 window bays** (7 + central frontispiece of 3 + 7). Slightly projecting end pavilions with pilasters. Three arched doors in the base under the portico.
- **Facade, bottom to top**: rusticated ground floor and mezzanine **≈ 0.27**; three storeys with the central giant columns (the frontispiece ≈ 0.2 of the width) **≈ 0.52**; attic storey above the main cornice **≈ 0.21**, with small dormer-pavilions and a parapet.
- **Dome group**, measured from the main cornice upward and relative to block height H: square **drum stage** with pediment and windows **≈ 0.32 H** (width 0.18 of the facade); balustraded ring **≈ 0.14 H**; **dome ≈ 0.17 H** (diameter ≈ 0.67 of the drum width, rise ≈ 0.46 of its diameter, so slightly flatter than a hemisphere); **lantern ≈ 0.12 H** (≈ half the dome width); finial with a red star (+0.14 H). Lantern top ≈ 1.55 H above the ground.
- **Scale problem** (estimated, unverified): HSBC is roughly 2× the Customs House frontage (≈ 90 m by footprint and photos). At the one scale above, the block is ≈ 13–15 plates high and **≈ 16–18 studs** wide. **An 8-wide HSBC at true proportion would be ≈ 7 plates tall** and look like a slab. The choice is a true-proportion wide front, or a compressed front (keep the portico, dome and 2–3 bays per side). Every brick version compresses.

### Peace Hotel North Building / Sassoon House (和平饭店北楼, 1929)
- zh-wiki: **77 m total**, 9 storeys (13 on the river side), **19 m dark-green copper pyramid**.
- **Bund (east) face**, aerial frontal `../shanghai-real/Peace_Hotel.jpg`: W : H(lantern top) ≈ 350 : 885 ≈ **1 : 2.5** → face ≈ 30 m ≈ **6 studs**, ≈ **37 plates**. Chamfered corners.
- **Divisions** (fractions of total): granite ground floor with **3 arched openings ≈ 0.10**; main slab of vertical window strips (5 strips, the middle three paired) to the main cornice **≈ 0.44** (top at 0.54); **set-back tower stage ≈ 0.16** (≈ 0.63 of face width), with one tall arched window and a carved crest at the top; **pyramid base band ≈ 0.04**; **pyramid ≈ 0.19**; **lantern ≈ 0.07** (red and black, square); flagpole on top.
- **Pyramid**: base ≈ 0.44 of face width, height ≈ 1.1 × base. Pitch ≈ **65–66°**. A **65° 2×1×2 slope** (6 plates of rise per stud of run) on a **4×4 base** gives 12 plates of height, ratio 1.2 against the real 1.1. That is the closest real-mould match; verify the mould number in LDraw. Trim: **thin red-orange lines along the four hips and the eaves band**, a red lantern with black openings.
- Pyramid colour sampled: overcast (12, 82, 93) teal-dark, sunlit (76, 116, 96) muted green. **Dark Green** is the best LEGO match. Sand Green (Miniland's pick) is too pale, Green too saturated.

### Granite colour — Tan vs Light Bluish Gray (photo samples)
Chromaticity r = R/ΣRGB, b = B/ΣRGB. LEGO: Tan (.386, .267), Dark Tan (.371, .286), Light Bluish Gray (.324, .342), Very Light Bluish Gray (.338, .329).

| sample | RGB | r, b |
|---|---|---|
| HSBC wall, overcast frontal | 194,187,182 | .346, .325 |
| Customs pier, old ref, soft light | 205,197,194 | .345, .326 |
| Peace wall, overcast aerial | 148,135,128 | .358, .310 |
| HSBC wall, 2017 frontal | 159,142,121 | .379, .288 |
| Customs pier, morning sun | 192,161,122 | .405, .257 |
| HSBC / Customs walls in shade (`The_Bund.jpg`) | ~90–120 grey | .32–.34, .33–.35 |
| promenade paving, light | 223,212,199 | .352, .314 |
| promenade paving, dark band | 164,168,163 | .331, .329 |

- **Finding**: the stone is a *warm grey*. In soft light it sits about one third of the way from Light Bluish Gray to Tan. Sunlit it matches Tan or Dark Tan; in shade it matches LBG. No LEGO colour is exact.
- **Recommendation: Tan walls for all three**, plus **Dark Tan** for the rusticated base courses and cornice shadow lines, and **Light Bluish Gray** for the HSBC dome group (the dome samples are clearly cooler than its walls: R−B ≈ +18 vs +38). Reasons: Tan is the warm direction the stone actually leans, it is the series convention (21039 uses it), and it separates Puxi from Pudong's grey glass.
- **Risk**: in bulk, Tan reads *yellow*. Wange 6231 and Miniland look butter-yellow beside the photos. Break up large Tan areas with Dark Tan / LBG lines and recessed dark window strips.
- Alternative: all-LBG + white (Baka 33205's HSBC). It reads as stone but cold, and it merges with the Pudong towers. Not recommended.
- The Peace Hotel is visibly warmer and browner than HSBC (aerial r .358). Tan with more Dark Tan suits it.

### Promenade and flood wall
- en-wiki: a tall levee built in the 1990s means **the embankment now stands about 10 m above street level** (as stated; photos suggest the walkway is about one storey above the road, so treat 10 m as the wall height from the river side, unverified). Renovated for Expo 2010, road space was given to a widened promenade.
- **Curve**: the river front is a long concave arc (`real-bund-curve-aerial.jpg`). The buildings follow the road, the promenade follows the water.
- **Paving** (`real-bund-promenade-paving.jpg`, `../shanghai-real/The_Bund.jpg`): large pale warm-grey granite slabs laid in a grid, crossed every few metres by **darker grey bands** running across and along. LEGO: Light Bluish Gray / Very Light Bluish Gray tiles with Dark Bluish Gray bands. Planters and timber-topped granite benches along the landside edge.
- **Railing** (`real-bund-promenade-railing.jpg`, `The_Bund.jpg`): dark grey metal **posts with a top rail and 2–3 horizontal bars** on a low granite kerb. Older photos (`real-bund-floodwall-from-water.jpg`, `real-bund-three-together-promenade.jpg`) show a curved-top railing with a red cap. Use the current horizontal-bar type.
- **Flood wall from the water**: a plain vertical grey wall with a thin coping.
- **Lamps**: tall slim grey poles with a single lantern head (current). Older photos show a black cast-iron multi-globe standard.

## 1. LEGO 21039 Shanghai — LEGO (official, 2018, 597 pcs)
- Files: `lego-21039-bund-brickarchitect.jpg` (BrickArchitect review, brickarchitect.com/2018/review-21039-shanghai-skyline/), `../shanghai-21039/tbb-20-Detail-HSBC-640x482.jpg`.
- HSBC: a hollow Tan box whose walls are **grille tiles on side-stud bricks** (vertical ribs = columns and window strips), and corner **1×1 round bricks**. The dome is a small **2×2 dome brick** on a ring of round plates / 1×1 round bricks.
- Customs House (TBB's description): **3 wide**, 1×1 bricks with side studs carrying **grille tiles**, topped with **1×1 round tiles** and a **telescope** spire.
- Reads at a glance: dome next to tower, vertical ribbing, all-Tan pair. Fails: no clock, no portico, no dome pinnacle (TBB), no Peace Hotel at all.
- **Lesson**: vertical texture (grilles, ribs) on Tan is what says "granite Bund" at any scale. At our scale it must turn into real recessed window strips.

## 2. LEGOLAND Shanghai Resort Miniland — LEGO model builders (2025, 1:20)
- Files: `miniland-shanghai-bund-day-overview-qq.jpg`, `miniland-shanghai-bund-day-crop.jpg` (news.qq.com/rain/a/20250526A07LF500), `miniland-shanghai-lujiazui-night-yicai.jpg` (yicaiglobal.com; this is the night hall with Lujiazui, I found no night shot of the Bund).
- The whole Bund row stands on a **curving riverbank**: a **dark grey flood wall** with a light grey walkway on top and a pale fence, boats in the river.
- Buildings in **Tan / pale yellow**. The Peace Hotel pyramid is **Sand Green** with a lighter lantern, on a tall slab of vertical window strips. The Customs House tower has a **white clock face**. The HSBC dome is light grey/white.
- Reads: the pyramid and the curve read instantly from across the hall. Fails: the colour reads butter-yellow; the pale green pyramid loses the real dark copper.
- **Lesson**: the curve plus the dark wall plus pale paving does the "Bund" job by itself. The pyramid needs to be darker than Miniland's.

## 3. Wange 6231 "Customs House, Shanghai" — Wange (Chinese brand, 1,243 pcs, 36.8 × 27.2 × 37.5 cm)
- Files: `wange-6231-customs-house-3q.jpg`, `wange-6231-customs-house-base-detail.jpg` (usablocks.com MOC-6231; set ID confirmed on merlinsbricks.com/sets/wan-6231).
- Facade: **giant pilasters of stacked 1×1 / 1×2 Tan bricks set in front of black/trans recesses** (the deep window strips). Side bays use printed window tiles. Rustication = **masonry-profile bricks** in the base. A Light Bluish Gray **cornice line of plates** and a dark grey roof edge.
- Tower: ≈ 6 wide, **2×2 printed clock tiles** on two faces, a red belfry, a green cap, a flag. Corner pavilions with arched windows.
- Reads: the pilaster rhythm and clock read well. Fails: Sand Green pavilion caps (the real ones are stone grey); the tower is too short and squat against the block (≈ 0.5 instead of 0.55 of height, and too wide); the overall colour is yellow; no visible portico columns.
- **Lesson**: borrow the pilasters-in-front-of-dark-recess facade and the masonry-brick base. Avoid the coloured caps and the stubby tower.

## 4. Baka 33205 "Shanghai HSBC Bank" — Baka (Chinese brand, 10,423 pcs, ≈ 76.8 cm wide)
- Files: `baka-33205-hsbc-front.jpg`, `baka-33205-hsbc-3q.jpg` (lepinland.com product page; Amazon lists it as MISINI 33205).
- Full-length front with all bays, end pavilions, a central **colonnade of white columns**, a pediment, a **white ribbed dome on a columned drum** with a gold finial, and three arched doors. Built in **white + Light Bluish Gray with Dark Bluish Gray bands**; lit yellow windows.
- Reads: the silhouette is right because it doesn't compress the width (≈ 3:1). Fails: the cool grey/white palette looks like a different city; the dense striping is busy.
- **Lesson**: dome on a drum, paired-column portico, end pavilions, 3 arched doors. These are the HSBC signatures. Keep the 3:1 feel even if compressed.

## 5. Loz 1039 "和平饭店 Peace Hotel (Old Shanghai)" — Loz (mini blocks, 2,768 pcs)
- File: `loz-1039-peace-hotel-fantasy.jpg` (lozshop.com).
- A green-and-cream corner shop with a domed cupola, signboards and an awning. **No pyramid, no granite slab, no resemblance to the real building**; it is a generic "old Shanghai" fantasy.
- **Lesson (avoid)**: the name is not the building. The green copper pyramid on a tall granite slab *is* the Peace Hotel. Without it, nothing reads.

## 6. LEGO 10278 Police Station — LEGO (Modular Buildings, 2020, 2,923 pcs) — facade technique
- File: `lego-10278-police-station-facade.jpg` (brickarchitect.com/2020/review-10278-lego-police-station/). Technique notes from Jay's Brick Blog / Brickset reviews.
- A Tan + Light Bluish Gray stone facade. **Carved stone detail made from the undersides of 1×2 jumpers set SNOT**; **Tan pillars** and brick-built columns at the ends; corner columns on a modified plate with a side-mounted inverted 2×2; **brackets** giving brick coursing in two directions; an entrance awning on "espresso filter holder" round plates with bars, angled by brackets on wedge plates.
- Related, from reviews: 10224 Town Hall uses a **printed dish** clock face with a white border on a grille background, and pillars of Tan double curved slopes. 10255 Assembly Square uses SNOT corner bricks and an inverted dome on round tiles with pins. 10297 Boutique Hotel uses rounded 1×2 plates as wall texture.
- **Lesson**: rustication = jumper undersides or masonry bricks on SNOT; columns = round bricks or curved slopes; cornice = inverted slopes / brackets with tiles; clock = a printed round tile or dish framed by a white ring.

## Borrow / avoid

### Customs House
- **Borrow**: Wange's giant pilasters in front of dark recessed strips (7 bays); a masonry/jumper rusticated base; a **real portico of 4 round-brick columns** between antae (none of the versions builds it; we should); 4 printed **2×2 round-tile clock faces** on a 2–3-wide clock stage (about 2× exaggerated, like every version); belfry → small cupola → flagpole; **grey** corner pavilions; a heavy cornice line in Dark Tan / LBG.
- **Avoid**: green or red caps (Wange); a squat tower. Keep the tower ≈ 0.55 of the total and stack the stages 0.28 / 0.17 / 0.06 / 0.03.

### HSBC
- **Borrow**: Baka's silhouette (end pavilions, central colonnade, dome on a drum, 3 arched doors); 21039's ribbed walls; a Light Bluish Gray dome group; a red star finial.
- **Avoid**: a cube-shaped "8-wide box" (true proportion is ≈ 3:1 wide); an all-white or LBG building.
- **Decision for Eric**: true-scale front ≈ 16–18 studs, or a compressed ~10–12 studs (portico + 2–3 bays per side).

### Peace Hotel
- **Borrow**: Miniland's tall slab of vertical strips with the pyramid as the only colour accent; a **Dark Green 65° pyramid on a 4×4 base** with red-orange hip and eave trim (tiles or plates) and a red lantern.
- **Avoid**: Sand Green (too pale); Loz's fantasy shop-house; a 45° roof (the real pitch is ≈ 65°).

### Promenade
- **Borrow**: Miniland's curve with a dark flood wall, light paving and a fence. Light Bluish Gray tiles with Dark Bluish Gray cross bands; a dark grey bar railing (posts + 2–3 rails); slim single-lantern lamp posts; timber benches.
- **Avoid**: a straight edge; a white or blue railing (pre-2010 style).
