# Paris in LEGO: how other builders did it (research notes, 2026-10-03)

Scope: official sets (Icons, Architecture, Creator Expert modulars), LEGOLAND Miniland (Windsor), fan MOCs (Brothers Brick / Flickr / Brickset / New Elementary / Rebrickable), LEGO Masters.
Confidence tags (same as Prague, renamed to the brief's letters): **[S]** = stated by the source page; **[P]** = I read it off the photo myself; **[M]** = memory / LEGO knowledge, unverified — check against LDraw before baking. Part numbers are given only where a source states them or tagged [M].
Access limits: Rebrickable and LEGO Ideas block scripted fetches (no photos or inventories); Brickfanatics serves an empty page to curl; LEGO Masters stills not found (see Gaps).

**Scale reminder for us.** Island 56 wide → landmark ≤ 0.6 × 56 ≈ 34 studs ≈ 27 cm; at 84 wide ≈ 50 studs ≈ 40 cm. The real tower is 330 m tall on a 125 m square base (ratio ≈ 2.6), so a 34-stud tower stands on a ~13 × 13 footprint, a 50-stud one on ~19 × 19. That is **21019 size (30 cm on a 14 × 14 base)**, not 10307 size (150 cm). Every build below is read against that.

## 1. Eiffel Tower

### 1.1 10307 Eiffel Tower (Icons, 2022) — designer Rok Žgalin Kobe
Scale ~1:216 [M], 149 cm, 58 × 58 cm, 10,001 pcs / 277 lots, **no new moulds**, 7,543 parts in dark bluish grey. [S] https://brickset.com/article/84338 , https://www.newelementary.com/2022/11/lego-icons-review-10307-eiffel-tower.html , https://www.brickfanatics.com/10307-eiffel-tower-follows-design-principles
Images: `official-10307-brickset-3.jpg` (whole), `-10.jpg` (leg + arch close-up), `-11.jpg` (first-level underside), `-13.jpg` / `-15.jpg` (first/second levels), `-19.jpg` (upper legs + second-level gallery), `-23.jpg` (upper shaft), `official-10307-ne-1stleg.jpg`, `-ne-2ndleg_a.jpg`, `-ne-bow.jpg`, `-ne-bars.jpg` (New Elementary, colour prototypes in dark tan).

- **Legs = hollow box girders.** Each leg is four separately built faces. The corner chords are **stacks of plates turned studs-out**, so the exposed studs read as rivets; plates with a vertical clip sit in the stack to hold the bracing. Faces are joined by **clip–bar to an 8 × 8 grille plate** at each end, the plate-with-clip rotating slightly to close the square. [S][P `-ne-1stleg`, `-ne-2ndleg_a`]
- **The leg's lean comes from clips, not from a curve.** Each leg segment is straight; the "curve" of the real leg is approximated by successive straight segments meeting at the platforms, angled by clip/bar joints ("where the arms connect, we did it with LEGO clips, which then transfer all the forces … down into the base"). [S designer quote][P]
- **X-bracing, two systems** [S parts list + P]:
  - inside the leg faces: **bars (2L with stop, 3L, 4L) between bar holders / candlesticks / lightsaber hilts**, angled off the chords — reads as a Warren/K truss (`-10.jpg`, `-ne-1stleg.jpg`).
  - on flat panels (between the legs under level 1, the bands at levels 1–2): **Technic axle connector hub with 4 bars turned 45°**, set in a row so the bar ends of neighbours meet — a continuous X-lattice with a round boss at each crossing (`-ne-bow.jpg` top, `-11.jpg`, `-13.jpg`). 112 hubs in the set. [S]
  - Counts [S NE]: 704 plate 1×1 with vertical clip, 660 bar holder with hole + bar handle (new element), 345 candlesticks, 256 bar 2L with stop, 172 bar 3L, 152 bar 4L, 168 lightsaber hilts, 112 four-bar hubs, 32 bar holder with clip. "168 similar cross-braces", each fixed in two places. [S Brickset]
- **The great arches between the legs**: a **roller-coaster ramp element** (NE: 26559 [S]) as the arch, with **3 mm rigid hoses (23L, 12L)** carrying the curve down the leg to the ground; a fan of short bars on the outside of the arch to the level-1 beam reads as the spandrel struts (`-ne-bow.jpg`, `-10.jpg`). Fragile until connected. [S][P]
- **First level**: traditional techniques, frame built upside-down so it meets the legs; 1×1 bricks with scrolls + 3L bars as the decorative frieze; 72 ingots = the 72 engineers' names. Gallery = **lattice fence panels + 1×1 round posts**; corbels under the deck = repeated small brackets/inverted slopes (`-13.jpg`, `-15.jpg`). [S][P]
- **Second-level gallery**: **lattice fence 1×4 panels** behind a row of **bucket handles** as railing hoops; under it, a corbel band of curved/inverted slopes (`-19.jpg`). [P]
- **Upper shaft**: the four legs have merged; studs-out plate chords with bar/holder diagonals; a 2×2×10 lattice mast inside (`-23.jpg`). Top: bucket handles round the antenna, 2×2 round plates at corners, flag. [S][P]
- **Base**: tan stone foundations under each leg, sand-coloured paths, olive/green lawns, black lamp posts (bar + 1×1 round), trees from flower-stem clusters (`-3.jpg`, `-11.jpg`). [P]
- **Nails**: true transparency and real X-lattice at every level; the arch; the gallery rings. **Misses** (for us): scale — at 150 cm a bracing bay is 2–4 studs; at our 30–40 cm it shrinks to ~1 stud, where bar-and-holder trusses no longer fit.

### 1.2 21019 The Eiffel Tower (Architecture, 2014) — **closest to our size**
30 cm, 321 pcs, 14 × 14 base. [S] https://brickset.com/article/9428 . Images `official-21019-arches-under.jpg`, `official-21044-vs-21019-eiffel.jpg` (right-hand tower).
- **Legs = plates on clip-plates**: each leg is a short stack of grey plates whose ends are **plates with clips** gripping **plates with bars** on the platform, so the leg leans at whatever angle the clip allows; leg faces textured with **1×2 grille tiles** (lattice implied, not built). [S][P]
- **Arches = Technic flexible hose** sprung between the four leg feet; it also stiffens the base. [S][P `-arches-under`]
- Platforms: square rings of plates with a **6 × 6 / 4 × 4 grille-pattern plate** as the deck seen from below; first level band = grey "brick with grille" rows. [P]
- Tower tapers in three straight stages; lift shaft = Technic bricks with holes in dark grey (visible between the upper legs). [P]
- **Nails**: silhouette, the A-shape and the arch at 30 cm with very few parts. **Misses**: no X-bracing at all, legs are flat slabs; reads as a lattice only from far away.

### 1.3 21044 Paris — micro Eiffel (2018)
`official-21044-eiffel-base-arches.jpg`, `official-21044-vs-21019-eiffel.jpg` (left). [S][P]
- **Arches = four printed curved windscreens** (trans-clear with a printed lattice band) — clever, but a print, not structure. [S]
- **Legs = click-hinge plates (finger hinges) with 1×2 grille tiles stacked on them**: the hinge sets the lean, the grilles give the ribbed texture. Platforms = 1×4 "brick with groove / grille" rows. [S][P]
- Usable idea: **hinge at the foot + hinge at the platform** is the simplest legal way to lean a leg; the windscreen trick is out for us (L2 prints only on flat faces).

### 1.4 10181 Eiffel Tower (2007)
3,428 pcs, ~108–120 cm, 1:300 [S]. Reviews give no lattice part detail; recalled as Technic-beam-and-plate lattice with grille-plate decks and a central lift column with red/yellow lifts [M]. Superseded by 10307; no image saved.

### 1.5 LEGOLAND Windsor Miniland Eiffel (2014)
`miniland-eiffel-commons.jpg` (CC BY 2.0, mrgarethm, Commons "Lego Eiffel Tower (42019842392)"). 4 m, 230k bricks. [S]
- [P] Built in **dark bluish grey / black**, not light grey; every leg face shows **continuous X-bracing panels**; the great arches are solid stepped arcs; the first-level gallery is a deep band; the tower stands on a plain grey plaza with minifigs **tiny against it** — even Miniland (1:20 buildings) compresses the tower hugely relative to people.
- Colour cue: real tower is "Eiffel bronze" (brown-grey). Miniland and 10307 both chose **dark grey**; 10307's prototypes were dark tan (`-ne-*`). For us: dark bluish grey (Kyoto-quiet) or dark tan/"dark stone" — decide against photos.

### 1.6 Sean Kenney, Eiffel Tower (2015) — professional large-scale
2.2 m, 67,470 pcs, glued copies of prototyped modules. [S] https://www.seankenney.com/eiffel-tower/ ; images `moc-kenney-eiffel-lattice.jpg`, `moc-kenney-eiffel-base.jpg`.
- **X-bracing = two long rounded-end plates/thin liftarms crossed and pinned at the centre**, laid on the face of a frame of **1×1 / 1×2 bricks stacked with studs-out SNOT steps** — big, bold X's that read from across a room. [P]
- **Legs and arch curve = stepping**: the leg's inner edge and the arch are stepped bricks (staircase profile), not hinges; the arch is a stepped band two bricks deep. [P]
- First-level frieze = repeated "X in a square" tiles/plates; gallery = light-grey railing with dark-grey posts. People = micro figures (1×1 round + head) → the scale is far below minifig. [P]
- **Lesson**: at large scale a pinned "X" of two flat strips is the most legible bracing. At our scale the same idea maps onto **four-bar hubs** (one part = one X) or **crossed bars in clips**.

## 2. Notre-Dame

### 2.1 21061 Notre-Dame de Paris (Architecture, 2024)
4,383 pcs, built in the order of the real construction. [S] https://brickset.com/article/109391 , https://newelementary.com/2024/05/lego-architecture-review-21061-notre.html . Images `official-21061-facade.jpg`, `-buttresses.jpg`, `-transept-rose.jpg`.
- **Colour: tan with a few dark-tan bands, roof flat silver/light grey tiles; spire dark grey with sand-green apostle statuettes.** (Prague notes already flagged: tan, not grey.) [S][P]
- **West façade** [P `-facade`]: three portals = **pointed arches from two mirrored curved/tapered slopes meeting at the top**, doors recessed behind; **gallery of kings** = a row of tan **1×1 round plates/cones** under the rose level; **rose window = trans-clear 4×4 dish printed with tracery** (identical on all three roses [S]), framed by plain tan bricks; two side bays = pointed arches with bars; **open arcade above = slim tan bars (round 1×1 columns) with Y-shaped capitals** (bar holders / clips); **parapet bands = lattice fence 1×4×1 (X pattern)** across the top and on the towers; **towers**: black recessed tall lancets (two per tower) framed by tan 1×1 round bricks; corner pinnacles = cones; buttress caps = rounded-top 1×1 bricks.
- **Flying buttresses** [S][P `-buttresses`]: **candlestick bars (upper) + droid-arm (lower)** spanning from the nave wall to **pinnacled buttress piers**; piers = tan stacks of 2×2 / 1×2 with **spear/spike tips on 1×1 round plates** as pinnacles; **bar holders with hole** sticking out = gargoyles/water spouts.
- **Transept** [P `-transept-rose`]: printed rose dish; **gable = a wheel (wagon wheel) in a triangle of tapered slopes**; pinnacles left/right; the triangular gable over the door uses studded wedge plates.
- **Nails**: the façade's three-tier rhythm (portals → rose/kings → arcade → towers), roses, buttresses. **Misses**: rose windows a bit small (review); at minifig scale the 4×4 dish is right only for the side roses.

### 2.2 Rocco Buttliere, Notre-Dame + Île de la Cité (2019)
Micro scale (~1:650 [M]). [S] https://www.brothers-brick.com/2019/06/03/brick-built-ode-to-notre-dame-de-paris/ ; image `moc-buttliere-notredame-2.jpg`.
- [P] Cathedral in **light bluish grey** (the stone as it reads in daylight), **sand-blue roofs**, a dark-grey spire; **the river setting** is the point: **trans-clear tiles over a dark base for the Seine**, **tan / dark-tan stepped quay walls in curved runs**, black road, a grey bridge crossing the river, flowering trees (pink/lavender) on the quay; "old hinge tile used as bollards". [S]
- **Borrow**: the **quay wall** — a stepped tan stone wall with a top coping and a walk below at water level — is exactly the Seine edge our island needs.

## 3. Arc de Triomphe — 21036 (Architecture, 2017)
386 pcs. [S] https://brickset.com/article/30562 . Image `official-21036-arc.jpg`.
- [S][P] **White** (real stone is cream; LEGO chose white with **tan stripes** for string courses); arches = **large white arch bricks** (one tall arch per face, smaller side arches on the short faces) with a **ribbed vault** visible inside (curved slopes underneath); **sculpture groups = white minifig statuettes in light-grey recessed niches on plinths**; frieze = grey **grille tiles** in white frames; **cornice = 30 white rocker bearings / hinge bottoms** studs-out; attic = **jumper plates bottom-out** (sculpture texture) and **1×1 round plates studs-out** as shields; **eternal flame = gold round dish + trans-orange flame**.
- Verdict: competent but "linear" build (reviews). [S]
- **Borrow at minifig scale**: niche-with-statuette for the reliefs, rocker-bearing/hinge cornice, jumper-bottom attic texture; colour **white + tan** or **light tan/"light nougat"** — check photos (the real stone is warm cream).
- Miniland: Windsor's Arc is 62,100 bricks [S]; no photo saved.

## 4. Louvre
- **21024 Louvre (2015, 695 pcs)**: Pavillon de l'Horloge + glass pyramid; "pyramid uses many clear pieces" [S] https://www.brickfanatics.com/21024-louvre-review/ (images not retrievable).
- **21044 micro Louvre** `official-21044-louvre.jpg` [S][P]: **pyramid = trans-clear "pyramid-ridged" tile / slopes** (a single mould + small trans slopes for the side pyramids); palace = tan walls of **1×1 tan bars/round columns** over a dark-tan backing, **dark-grey sloped roofs** (2×2 / 2×4 slopes), a **dark-grey dome** on the central pavilion.

- **Borrow**: pyramid from **trans-clear / trans-black slopes on a square base** (33° or 45°; real angle ≈ 51°) with **black or dark-grey thin edges** (bars/tiles) for the mullions — or the trans-clear pyramid mould if LDraw has one at a usable size; **triangle pools in grey frames with trans-light-blue tiles**; palace in tan with dark-grey hipped pavilion roofs.

## 5. Haussmann building + Paris café

### 5.1 10243 Parisian Restaurant (Creator Expert modular, 2014)
2,469 pcs. [S] https://brickset.com/article/8147/review-10243-parisian-restaurant-part-2 . Images `official-10243-front.jpg`, `-mansard.jpg`, `-cafe-front.jpg`, `-terrace.jpg`.
- **Mansard roof** [S][P `-mansard`]: **dark-blue curved slopes, alternating 1×3 and 1×4**, laid on a **barrel (quarter-round) profile** so the lower slope is steep and the top flat; three **white dormers** — centre one with a **pointed-top window and lattice glass**, side ones with **round oculus framed by white ornament (shell/scroll pieces)**; **chimney stacks** of light-grey bricks with **1×1 round plates/bars as pots**; cornice under the roof = light-grey tiles over a **dark-grey twisted/ribbed band** (rope-like texture). Real Paris zinc is **grey** — LEGO's dark blue is artistic licence; Toltomeja and Lepra use grey/black (below).
- **Façade** [P `-front`]: olive-green + white + light-grey quoins; **tall French windows with white mullions and flower boxes**; **one wrought-iron balcony** (black spindled fence) in the centre; white pilasters with capitals.
- **Café** [P `-cafe-front`, `-terrace`]: ground floor with **white pilasters + brown-framed glazing**, **black chain on short black posts** as the terrace barrier, **flower-box planters (red-brown 1×4 + green)**, **bistro tables = round tile on a bar**, **chairs = blue minifig chairs**, a red canopy over the door with a sign hanging from it, **green vine "awning" on black bars** over the windows, **black lamp posts with yellow lanterns and hanging flower baskets** (2×2 dome as basket [S]). Roof terrace with black railing.

### 5.2 10362 French Café (Icons, 2025)
1,101 pcs, mini-doll ("almost-minifig") scale, façade only. [S] https://www.brothers-brick.com/2025/02/07/lego-10362-french-cafe-a-slice-of-paris-review/ . Image `official-10362-45.jpg`.

### 5.3 LepraLegoMOCs, "Boulevard des Lumières" (2020) — the Haussmann reference
18,535 pcs, 66.7 × 40.9 × 64.8 cm, minifig scale, modular-compatible. [S] https://www.brothers-brick.com/2020/02/03/live-the-parisian-life-on-the-boulevard-des-lumieres/ , https://brickester.gumroad.com/l/boulevarddeslumieres . Images `moc-lepra-boulevard-lumieres-1.jpg` (exterior), `-2.jpg` (section).
- [P] **This is the canonical Haussmann grammar** — copy it:
  - **Angled (cut) corner** with the same façade wrapping round; ground floor + entresol in **white rusticated stone** (horizontal grooved bricks, deep joint lines); upper floors **tan ashlar** (masonry-profile bricks) with **pilasters and carved capitals** (scroll pieces);
  - **continuous black wrought-iron balconies on the 2nd and 5th floors** (black spindled fence 1×4 on a tile ledge), single **balconettes** elsewhere; **tall arched and square French windows** in white frames with mullions;
  - **mansard roof in black/dark-grey slopes**, steep lower slope, **dormers with their own small pitched roofs** in a regular rhythm, chimneys on top; section `-2` shows the profile: steep slope ~2/3 of the attic height, then flat;
  - street: **dark-green Morris column** (round posters under a dark-green dome with a finial), **dark-green lamp posts**, **striped canvas awnings** (blue/black and dark red), benches, trees in square grates.
- **Nails** everything Haussmann. **Misses** (for us): far too big — at our island scale a Haussmann block must shrink to ~8–12 studs wide × 4–5 floors.

## 7. Borrow list (part *types* per landmark; resolve numbers in LDraw)

**Eiffel Tower (27–40 cm, 13–19 studs square base)** — hybrid of 21019 (structure at our size) and 10307 (lattice vocabulary):
- Legs: **box girders of plates studs-out** (studs = rivets) as corner chords; legs leaned by **plate-with-clip / plate-with-bar** joints at foot and at platforms (21019, 10307) or **click hinges** (21044). Two straight segments per leg (ground → L1, L1 → L2), then the merged shaft.
- Lattice: **Technic axle connector hub with 4 bars** at 45° for flat X panels (L1 band, between-leg panels, lower leg faces where a bay ≥ 2 studs); **1×2 grille tiles / grille plates** where the bay is under 2 studs (upper shaft); optional **bars in vertical clips** for diagonals on the lower legs.
- Arches: **3 mm rigid hose or Technic flexible hose** sprung between the leg feet (21019 at our size), outer spandrel struts = short bars in clips (10307).
- Platforms: L1 = deep band with **lattice fence 1×4×1** + **corbel row (inverted slopes / brackets)**; L2 = smaller band, **bucket handles** as gallery hoops; top = **2×2 round plate cluster + bar antenna + small flag**.
- Colour: dark bluish grey (10307, Miniland) or the real bronze-brown — decide from photos.
- Base: tan stone footings under each foot (10307), the Champ-de-Mars as tan paths + green lawn.

**Notre-Dame façade (if chosen)**: tan + dark-tan; **4×4 trans dish (printed rose = L2)** for the rose; portals from **mirrored tapered/curved slopes** forming pointed arches; **lattice fence 1×4×1** parapets; **round 1×1 bars + clips** for the open arcade; **spike/cone pinnacles on 1×1 round plates**; **candlestick + droid-arm** flying buttresses; black lancets in the towers.

**Arc de Triomphe**: white/cream + tan string courses; **large arch bricks** (one per face), **curved slopes** in the vault; **niches with white statuettes** for reliefs; **rocker-bearing / hinge-bottom cornice**; **jumper plates bottom-out** attic; **gold dish + trans-orange flame**.

**Louvre**: **trans-clear (or trans-black) slopes** on a square for the pyramid, **black thin bars/tiles** for mullions; **triangular pools** in light-grey frames with trans-light-blue; tan palace with **dark-grey hipped pavilion roofs** (slopes + studded top).

## 8. Gaps (candid)

- **No minifig-scale Eiffel Tower exists anywhere**, nor can one: at 1:42 it would be ~7.8 m. Every reference is a compressed landmark (21019 at 30 cm is the closest to our budget). Proportions — leg splay, platform heights (L1 at ~0.17 H, L2 at ~0.35 H), arch shape, taper — must come from photographs, not from a LEGO model. We are designing the **readable lattice at 30–40 cm** ourselves: 21019 skipped it (grille texture), 10307 needs 150 cm. Prototype the four-bar-hub X on the lower legs first and check it from the default camera; if it turns to noise, fall back to grille texture on the upper half.
- **No LEGO Masters Paris/Eiffel build found**, and Rebrickable / LEGO Ideas images were unreachable (bot protection), so mid-size Eiffel MOCs (e.g. MOC-44068) were not inspected.
- **Notre-Dame at minifig scale**: none found (21061 and Buttliere are micro). Façade only would be ~40+ studs tall at true minifig scale — it must be compressed like the Eiffel.
- **Not found in LEGO at all**: Wallace fountain, Guimard Métro arch (only unbuilt Ideas proposals), Pont Neuf / Pont Alexandre III at minifig scale. These are ours to design.
- Colours disagree across builders (zinc roofs: LEGO dark blue vs MOC grey/black; Notre-Dame: tan vs light grey; Arc: white vs cream). Settle each from photos of the real place (rule 11), not from the sets.
- Part numbers in this file are mostly unverified [M]; the New Elementary roller-coaster ramp (26559) is the only one stated by a source.

### 10.1 Notre-Dame — nine more builds (four of them minifig or mid scale)

**A. Stebrick (Stefano Mapelli), Notre-Dame de Paris (2020)** — the mid/minifig-scale reference. 63,181 pcs (50,103 visible + 13,078 hidden structural), 51.8 × 145.7 × 101.9 cm, "66 × 184 × 92 studs", modular in five blocks (façade, nave, transept, choir, apse); a façade-only version is MOC-46722 (18,970 pcs). [S] https://www.eurobricks.com/forum/forums/topic/178335-notre-dame-de-paris-modular-building-dimension ; Rebrickable MOC-43974 / MOC-46722 (blocked). Images are **Stud.io renders, not photos** [P]. Built physically by Lionel Baudot (shown in Metz cathedral, 2023) [S, search snippet]. `moc-stebrick-notredame-front.jpg`, `-facade-34.jpg`, `-transept-rose.jpg`.
- Colour: **tan** walls, **light-grey** statues, **dark-grey** roof, **black** spire. Real proportions held: façade ≈ 1.7 × as tall (to tower tops) as it is wide [P].
- **Portals**: three deep pointed arches, the centre one larger; each **archivolt is several stepped rings** (concentric arch layers receding into the wall), doors **dark-grey with a vertical bar grille**; jamb statues = small grey figures on brackets either side [P].
- **Gallery of kings**: one band across the whole width = **a row of ~28 small pointed niches, each holding a light-grey statuette** (minifig-statue scale); niches separated by slim tan columns [P].
- **Rose level**: centre rose = **round opening with a dark backing and tan radial spokes + an outer ring** (front view), lit stained glass in the ¾ view = trans-coloured 1×1 round plates behind tracery [P]; side bays = **pairs of tall lancets** under small rosette medallions, quatrefoil/cross stars on the piers [P].
- **Open arcade**: a full-width band of **slender tan columns carrying small pointed arches, open to the sky behind** — the screen is real depth, not texture [P].
- **Towers**: each face = **two very tall black lancets** framed by bundles of thin round columns; corner buttresses stepping in; a balustrade with small grey gargoyles at the top [P].
- Transept rose (`-transept-rose`): **radial spokes of tan bar/clip elements** round a hub, set in a circular frame of curved pieces, crocketed gable above with a smaller rose [P].
- **Borrow**: the vertical rhythm and proportions (portal zone ≈ 30 %, kings band thin, rose zone, arcade, towers), the real-depth arcade, two-lancet towers. Too big as-is (66 studs wide).

**B. Castor Troy, Notre-Dame in "Paris Steampunk 1889"** (with Domino39; the whole layout ~700,000 pcs) [S TBB https://www.brothers-brick.com/2018/08/11/return-to-an-alternate-paris-in-1889-with-this-sprawling-steampunk-scene/ ; Flickr 43039626915]. `moc-castor-paris1889-notredame.jpg`. **Minifig scale, real photo** [P].
- **Light bluish grey** throughout (the stone as photographed in daylight); façade ≈ 35–40 studs wide, figures stand at the doors [P].
- **Portals**: pointed arches whose **archivolts are lined with a beading of small round elements** (1×1 round plates / studs on the curved arch band), a **Technic gear / wheel in the tympanum**; jambs of stacked round pieces [P].
- **Gallery of kings**: a row of **light-grey statue figures in pointed niches** (minifig-size statues) — instantly readable [P].
- **Rose**: dark recessed circle with a **radiating spoked element** in the centre; **side "roses" = Technic gears / wheels** above the lancet pairs, with sculpted grey ornament below [P].
- Towers: tall paired lancets; bridge and Seine in the foreground [P].
- **Borrow**: gear/wheel parts as rosettes; beaded archivolt; statue row in niches. Steampunk ornament (vines, gears everywhere) is the builder's theme — don't copy the clutter.

**C. LeshyBricks (Michal), Assassin's Creed Unity cathedral (2026)** — **minifig scale, closest to our budget** [S TBB https://www.brothers-brick.com/2026/02/24/a-cathedral-worth-leaping-from/]. `moc-leshy-acunity-notredame.jpg`. Notre-Dame-*inspired* (AC Unity), not a faithful copy [P].
- Façade ≈ 22–24 studs wide on a ~32 × 40 base, towers ≈ 2.5 × the width tall [P] — this is roughly the size an island landmark can take.
- **Light bluish grey ashlar from mixed tiles / plates on SNOT walls** (irregular joints read as old stone) [P].
- **Rose = round opening with a dark backing and a thin bar cross (X)**; **portal = one round-headed arch with a dark-red door, under three triangular gables (wedge plates / tiles) with a pinnacle on each** [P].
- **Statues in arched niches** on the towers (grey minifig statues) [P]; towers with tall single lancets, black behind [P].
- Plaza: grey tile paving, black iron railings, 15+ minifigs (French-Revolution crowd) [P].
- **Borrow**: proof that a twin-tower façade reads at ≈ 24 studs wide with minifigs at its feet; textured ashlar; the rose as a simple dark disc with bars. **Misses**: no gallery of kings, no arcade, one portal instead of three — exactly the Notre-Dame signatures we must keep.

**D. Ivan Angeli, Notre-Dame (Warsaw, January 2020)** — Guinness "largest LEGO Notre-Dame": 2.72 × 3.78 × 1.43 m, 400,000+ bricks, **1:33.51**, ~250 kg, 500 hours [S] https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/587793-largest-lego-brick-sculpture-of-notre-dame , https://warsawinsider.pl/lego-record-smashed/. `moc-angeli-notredame-transept.jpg` (no façade photo retrievable).

### 10.2 Eiffel Tower — 25–60 cm and the official 10181

**J. 10181 Eiffel Tower (2007, 3,428 pcs, ~108 cm)** — technique now seen in photos. `official-10181-front.jpg`, `-topdown.jpg`, `-legs-wip.jpg`, `-leg-detail.jpg`.
- [P] **Dark bluish grey; tan stone footings.** **Each leg = two (or four) square columns of 2 × 2 / 1 × 2 bricks joined across by lattice fence 1 × 4 (X pattern) strips**; **the leg curve = every few courses the column steps outward by a plate's offset, with inverted slopes / slopes filling the steps** — the curve is a staircase, not a hinge (`-legs-wip`, `-leg-detail`).
- **Arch** = the inner edges of the stepped legs plus a stepped band under L1 (`-front`). Platforms = rings of 1 × N plates studs-up (`-topdown`). Above L2: open square frames of bricks, holes = Technic bricks [P].
- Reads as a mass of steps from far; lattice only at the fence strips. Superseded by 10307 for good reason.

**K. Ngoc Truong (C.Ngoc), Eiffel after Mini_Wizardy's "MINI Eiffel" (2018, render)** — ≈ 35–40 cm on a ~28 × 28 plate [P]; "should stand on its own", intended in **dark tan** [S Flickr 42380759285]. `moc-ngoc-eiffel-render.jpg`.
- [P] **Lower legs = flat panels of X-lattice (lattice fence / grille panels) on each face**, set at the lean by **hinge plates at the foot and under L1**; feet on small tan/grey plinths. **Upper legs (L1 → L2) = Technic thin liftarms (row of holes) as the four corner chords**, open between them — the holes read as rivetted girders. **Shaft above L2 = four faces of X-lattice panels** forming a square tube, a 2 × 2 dish + round cap at the top, antenna bar. Platforms = rings of plates with **lattice fence 1 × 4** railing. A trans-clear stack in the centre = lift column.
- No arch under L1 in this render [P] — a miss.
- **Borrow**: **fence/X-panel faces on hinged legs** for the lower stage, **liftarm chords** for the middle stage — the best "readable lattice at ~35 cm" seen.

**L. Spencer_R, Eiffel Tower (2008 revision)** — "primarily constructed from LEGO flex tubes" [S Flickr 4719734276]. `moc-spencer-eiffel-flextube.jpg`. ≈ 40–50 cm on a ~24 × 24 base [P].
- [P] Dark bluish grey; **every leg and the shaft are bundles of flex tubes / flexible rods held by clips and bar holders at intervals** — so the legs **bow in a real continuous curve** from the foot to L2 and taper into the shaft without any steps; cross-pieces between rods give a ladder/lattice look. **Great arches = flex tube arcs** between the legs under L1. L1 deck sand-green tiles in a dark frame; tan footings; Champ-de-Mars lawn and a pond.
- **Borrow**: **the only continuous leg curve at our size** — flex hose sprung between clips, as 21019 does for the arch but applied to the leg chords too. Watch the bend radius; the tube must enter clips at real positions.

Sources: the **official LEGO instruction PDFs**, downloaded from lego.com/service/building-instructions → `eiffel-instructions/` (21019 `6089600.pdf`, 21044 `6532393.pdf`, 10307 `6437106/6437120/6501712.pdf`, 10181 `4520837/4520838/4529089.pdf`, ~350 MB, **don't commit**), page renders in `eiffel-instructions/hi21019/`, `hi21044/`, `hi10307/`, `hi10181/`. ### 11.1 21019 The Eiffel Tower (30 cm, 14 × 14 base): the closest analogue to ours
Inventory [S Brickset]: 32 × **60478 Plate 1×2 with Handle on End**, 8 × **92280 Plate 1×2 with Single Clip on Top**, 20 × **61252 Plate 1×1 with Clip Horizontal**, 12 × **12825 Tile 1×1 with Clip**, 4 × **32200 Technic Axle Flexible 12** (silver), 8 × 99781 / 4 × 99780 brackets 1×2–1×2, 28802 bracket 1×2–1×4, 30 × 2412b grille tiles, 2 × 4733 brick 1×1 with studs on four sides (dark grey), 44294 axle 7 (lift core), 4151 grid plate 8×8 (L1 deck), 21462 lightsaber blade (antenna).
- **(C) Legs = "bar-ended sticks"** [P p.36–37, 44–47]. Each leg segment is a stack of plates and tiles (1×2, 1×1, a 1×4 tile on top) with a **60478 Plate 1×2 with Handle on End at each end**. The bottom handle clicks into a **92280 Plate 1×2 with Clip on Top** on the stone footing (p.26, step 11: 4 × 92280 on the four footings). The top handle clicks into a clip plate (92280 / 61252) on the **underside of L1, which is built upside-down** (p.33 step 10: 4 × clip plates at the deck's corners, then the legs added and the platform flipped, p.46). Each clip is a hinge with a horizontal axis, so the lean is free and **is set by the geometry: footing below, platform above**. L1→L2 legs are the same sticks (p.36–38).
- **Foot block** (p.44–45, 4×): plate 1×3 + 1×2 handle plates + **99780/99781 brackets** give studs on both inner faces. A plain 1×1 tile goes on one face and a **12825 tile with clip** on the other, studs-out: these are the arch sockets.
- **(B) Arch = 32200 Technic Axle Flexible 12, sprung between two feet** [P p.48, zoom `hi21019/arch48-48.png`]. LDraw 32200 has **bar-diameter "skinny tips"** (stud3a, 4 LDU radius), so each tip snaps into the **12825 clip on the side bracket of a foot**. The rod bows up and the crown sits just under the L1 band, which it does not touch or need. Both ends are real clip connections on the legs, and the base is not involved. The set has 4 rods, one per face.
- **(A) Upper shaft = 8 bar-ended plate sticks, two stages** [P p.49–62]. Stage 1: four sticks (plate 1×6 + tile 1×6, a 60478 at each end) are clipped flat to a 4×4 hub, then folded up. Their tips are gathered by clip plates on the four faces of a dark-grey **4733 brick 1×1 with four side studs**, sitting on the 7L axle core. Stage 2: four longer sticks (1×8 plate/tile) do the same up to a second 4733 node, then a dish and the antenna. **This is exactly the "two sloping rods with sky between" look Eric rejects**: 21019 accepts it at 30 cm and does not solve (A).
- Platforms: 28802 / 99780 brackets carry **2412b grille tiles** studs-out as the frieze bands (p.32, 41–42).

### 11.2 21044 Paris, micro Eiffel (~15 cm): how LEGO makes a narrow spire read dense
Inventory [S]: **18649 Plate 1×2 with Handles on Opposite Ends** (8), **44861 Plate 1×2 with Single Clip on Top (thick)**, 60478, 4733 (dark grey), **2412b Tile 1×2 Grille**, 28921 bar 6L with flange, 28626 round 1×1 with through hole, 93273 curved 4×1 double (the lower leg bows).
- **(A) Spire = SNOT core + outside chords** [P p.93–96, `hi21044/z96L.png`, `z93s.png`]. Four upper chords (plate 1×4 + 1×2 plates, **18649 handles both ends**) clip vertically into clip plates at the L2 corners (step 105). The core is built separately: **4733 bricks stacked on a 28921 bar**, with 1×1 round plates as spacers, 4 × plate 1×1 + 4 × clip plates at the bottom, and **8 × 2412b grille tiles on the 4733 side studs** (step 113). The core drops down between the four chords (step 114). Result: a **closed, ribbed, tapering block** with chords hugging it, so no sky between the rods. The taper comes from the core narrowing (two 4733s side by side → one).
- **(C) Legs** = clip-hinged plate sticks (18649 / 60478 into 44861 clips), the same logic as 21019.
- (B) Arches: printed windscreens (not usable for us, §1.3).

### 11.3 10307 Eiffel Tower (150 cm): the full-lattice logic, scaled down only partly
- **(A) Upper shaft** [P book 3 p.181–228, `hi10307/b-187/213/215/216.png`]. Each face is **built flat as a panel**: two long **plate-stack chords converging to a V**, studs out (the studs read as rivets), with clips along both edges. Lower bays: **X-crosses = a bar through a quad bar hub (68888 Bar 1L Quadruple with Axlehole Hub [M: part in step 589 looks like 3803/68888]) plus bar ends in "bar tube with clip" (11090)**, clipped onto the neighbour chord. Upper narrow bays (p.213–215): **a zig-zag of short struts = 1×1 round plate + 11090 Bar Tube with Clip (14 per side)**. The struts of the two chords interleave, so the gap fills with a continuous W-lattice. The panels then lean against a **central lattice mast** (stacked girder sections, p.198–207), clipped at the foot and at a brown top frame (p.216, with a ✓/✗ for which face goes out). **The density comes from the struts filling the bay plus a mast behind them**: from any angle you see a lattice in front of a lattice.
- **(B) Arches** [P book 2 p.114–125, `hi10307/c-114/115/118/120/122/123.png`]. The arch is a **26559/26560 Roller Coaster Ramp** (LDraw: "Train Track Roller Coaster Ramp 4×16×6 S-Shape"), **hung under the L1 girder**. A plate beam carries a row of vertical struts (round 1×1 + bar + **bar holder with clip**) whose clips grab the **rungs of the coaster track**. That subassembly slides under the L1 band (p.120). Instruction text [S p.120]: *"The arches between the legs are not load bearing, they are purely decorative – this is also true of the real tower."* From the track's ends a **3 mm rigid hose** [M part: "Hose, Rigid 3mm D."] runs down the leg's inner chord, clipped at about three points and plugged into a socket at ground level (p.122–123). So the arch is **fixed at the top (hung from L1) and at the legs (clips)**, never laid on the ground.
- **(C) Legs**: straight segments, angled at the platforms by clip/bar joints (§1.1, designer quote).

### 11.4 10181 Eiffel Tower (2007, 108 cm)
- **(B)(C) Legs and arch are one brick structure** [P book 1 p.40–79, `hi10181/sheet.png`]. Each leg is a column of bricks/plates that **steps (corbels) inward course by course**; the stepped inner edges of two legs form the arch. The top is a **long plate beam with 3633 Fence Lattice 1×4×1 strips** bridging the two leg tops (step 29). Every connection is studs; there are no hinges or clips. Black long plates = lift rails. Reads stepped at our size (see §10.2 J).

### 11.6 Recommendation for our 32 cm tower (18 × 18 base, ~108 plates)
Our size equals 21019's, so **copy 21019's connection logic (C, B) and 21044's spire density (A), plus 10307's strut trick where a bay is ≥ 2 studs.**

**(A) Upper shaft above L2: dense and tapering**
1. **SNOT core (21044)**: a central column of **4733 Brick 1×1 with Studs on Four Sides**, threaded on a bar/axle core (**30374 Bar 4L** / 87994 Bar 3L, or a Technic axle). Each 4733 face carries **2412b Tile 1×2 Grille**, studs-out. Taper: a 2×2 core (four 4733s) for the lower half, a 1×1 core above, a **4×4 → 2×2 plate step** between them. This is what fills the sky. [S 21044]
2. **Four corner chords outside the core**: plate stacks with **18649 Plate 1×2 with Handles on Opposite Ends / 60478 Plate 1×2 with Handle on End** at the ends. They clip at L2 into **92280 / 44861 Plate 1×2 with Single Clip on Top** and at the top into clip plates on a **4733 node**, so the lean comes from two real clips. [S 21019, 21044]
3. **Brace the 3–5-stud-wide lower half like 10307's narrow bays**: short struts of **6141 Plate 1×1 Round + 11090 Bar Tube with Clip** zig-zagging between chord and core. Where a face is ≥ 4 studs, use a **3633 Fence Lattice 1×4×1 / 3185 Fence Lattice 1×4×2** panel on brackets (99780/99781) as the face (10181, Ngoc). [S 10307, P]
4. Optional central mast for the lift: **2580 Support 2×2×5 Lattice Pillar** or **30517 Support 2×2×10 Girder Triangular** inside the 2×2 core zone, if the core is opened (10307's mast logic).

**(B) The four arches: fixed by real connections**
1. **Recommended (21019): 32200 Technic Axle Flexible 12** (or 32199 11L / 32580 7L by span), with its **bar-size tips in 12825 / 15712 Tile 1×1 with Clip**, mounted studs-out on **99780/99781 brackets** on the inner faces of two adjacent leg feet. Both ends are clipped and the crown floats under the L1 band. **Pipeline caveat**: the LDraw file is straight (4-LDU segments made to be flexed), so we need a bend-along-a-curve bake/assembly for it. No catalogue entry does that yet.
2. Alternative, keeping a rigid curved part (10307 logic): **hang it from L1, don't stand it on the base.** Our **6184 Arch 1×12×5 with Curved Top** should then stand **vertical in the face plane** (not tilted 30°), with the **two crown studs** it has (LDraw 6184: `stud.dat` at x = ±10 on the top centre, checked) plugged into the underside of the L1 band. Its two feet sit on studs of the foot plinths or on brackets on the legs' inner faces. That is a legal stud connection at both ends, as 10181 does with plates. Tilting 30° leaves no legal stud seat, which is the problem Eric saw.
3. Not recommended: 26559/26560 roller-coaster ramp (4×16×6, too big at 18 studs), and printed windscreens (21044, an L2 print on a curved face).

**(C) Leaning legs**
1. **Clip + bar at both ends (21019 / 21044)**: the leg is a plate stack with **60478 / 18649** handle plates at the ends. The bottom clicks into **92280 Plate 1×2 with Single Clip on Top** on the tan stone footing. The top clicks into 92280 / **61252 Plate 1×1 with Clip Horizontal** on the underside of L1 (build the platform upside-down). The angle is set by footing-to-platform geometry, so any lean is legal.
2. If a fixed angle is wanted: **44301a + 44302a locking hinge plates (1 finger + 2 fingers)** at the foot and under L1 (Ngoc §10.2 K; not seen in any official Eiffel instruction). Locking clicks hold the angle on their own.
3. Feet: a foot block of **99780/99781 brackets** gives side studs for the arch clips (B1) and 2412b grille faces (21019 p.44–45).

Open questions for Eric: B1 (flex rod, needs a curve bake) vs B2 (6184 hung vertically); and whether the 2×2 core in A1 should be dark grey (21019 / 21044 use dark grey for the core and light grey for the chords) or one colour.
