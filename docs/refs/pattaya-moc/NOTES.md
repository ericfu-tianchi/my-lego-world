# Pattaya in LEGO: how other builders did it (research notes, 2026-10-05)

Scope: Thai temples (Sanctuary of Truth, Wat Arun, Wat Phra Kaew, Lanna wat, Thai-style church), LEGOLAND Malaysia Miniland Thailand, Thai AFOL exhibitions, beach and sea at minifig scale, a hillside letter sign, street food, snorkelling, Thai life-layer props (tuk-tuk, conical hat, elephant, boats).
Confidence tags: **[S]** = stated by the source page; **[P]** = I read it off the photo myself; **[M]** = memory or LEGO knowledge, unverified. Part numbers are given only where a source or a Brickset inventory states them, or tagged [M]. **Every number named here was checked against the vendored LDraw library; see the table at the end.** Brickset/BrickLink numbers that are not in LDraw are marked "no LDraw file".
Access limits: LEGO Ideas returns 403 to curl and WebFetch (no Ideas photos or part lists); Rebrickable is blocked (as before); the Bangkok Post sits behind a paywall redirect. Flickr search pages can be scraped for IDs, and Brickset inventories parse cleanly; most of the hard facts below come from those two.

**Scale reminder.** Island 56–80 studs wide → hero landmark ≤ 0.6 × width ≈ 34–48 studs. The Sanctuary of Truth is 105 m tall on a cross-shaped (*chaturamukh*) plan of roughly 100 m [S Wikipedia: 105 m; plan size M]. At minifig scale (1 stud ≈ 0.33 m) it would be ~320 studs tall, so it has to be compressed hard, the way Miniland compresses (Miniland itself is 1:20, i.e. 2.5× our scale, and still compresses Wat Arun).

---

## 0. Bottom line

- **No LEGO Sanctuary of Truth exists anywhere**: no official set, no Miniland model, no MOC on Brothers Brick, Flickr, Eurobricks or Thai-language search (EN "Sanctuary of Truth lego/brick/nanoblock", TH "ปราสาทสัจธรรม เลโก้"; 2026-10-05). We would be the first. [S, negative search]
- **LEGO has never made a Thai temple set** ("LEGO has never explored traditional South-East Asian architecture in official sets", Brothers Brick 2025). [S] No Architecture set, no BAM/40-series Thai build turned up.
- The reference builds that exist, best first:
  1. **Birgitte Jonsgard, "Wat Lek" (Lanna temple in minifig scale, Jan 2025)**: *the* reference for the roof, chofa, naga stair and carved gable at our scale.
  2. **LEGOLAND Malaysia Miniland Thailand** (since 2012): Wat Arun prang, two ubosot halls, sala, yaksha guard, royal barge, tuk-tuk, longtail boat, at 1:20.
  3. **Jared Chan, Wat Benchamabophit / Songkran (ACGHK 2015, miniland)**: stacked orange-tile roofs, gold bargeboards.
  4. **Anne Mette Vestergård, Holy Redeemer Church Bangkok (2024, micro)**: green/orange/red fish-scale roof bands and white chofa from Unikitty tails [S].
  5. **Thai LEGO User Group (TLUG)** exhibition, CentralPlaza Lat Phrao 2018: 11 Bangkok landmarks (Wat Phra Kaew, Wat Arun, Golden Mount, Wat Benchamabophit, Democracy Monument…), 500,000+ bricks [S]; only the Democracy Monument photo found.

## 1. Thai temples

### 1.1 Birgitte Jonsgard, "Wat Lek" (Flickr birgburg, 2025-01, minifig scale) ★ best reference
URLs: https://www.flickr.com/photos/birgburg/54262812061/ (title "Wat Lek", caption "Lanna temple in minifig scale") · Brothers Brick https://www.brothers-brick.com/2025/01/18/whats-that-a-lego-wat/ · complex video https://www.flickr.com/photos/birgburg/54203080290/
Images: `moc-jonsgard-wat.jpg` (¾ view, BB full size), `moc-jonsgard-wat-front.jpg` (gable front), `moc-jonsgard-naga-stair.jpg`, `moc-jonsgard-complex.jpg` (wat + chedi + bodhi tree), `moc-jonsgard-chedi.jpg`, `moc-jonsgard-alms.jpg` (monks' alms round), `moc-jonsgard-bamboo-ngob.jpg` (farmer in a conical hat by bamboo).
Builder statement: "steep, tiered roof with ornate chofa jutting out like horns … snaking dragons along the stairs". No part list published. [S]

- **Stacked gables (the Thai/Lanna signature)** [P]: three gables per end, each one lower and set *forward and outward* from the one behind it, so the roof steps down in two directions (along the ridge and down the slope). Each gable is a self-contained steep triangle (~60°), not one long slope.
- **Roof field** [P]: a steep slope in **Reddish Brown with Dark Brown mixed in**, textured as staggered rows of small shingles. It reads as 1 × 2 / 1 × 1 tiles or cheese slopes laid in offset rows on a base turned to the roof angle by hinges (the studs of the base show between shingle rows in places). Exact part not readable; [M] most likely **cheese slopes 1 × 2 × ⅔ (85984) / 1 × 1 × ⅔ (54200)** or 1 × 2 tiles on hinged plates (2429/2430, 44301a/44302a).
- **Bargeboards (the raised frame along each rake)** [P]: **Dark Red plates turned studs-out**, laid along the slope as a stepped band with the studs facing the viewer. The exposed studs read as the gilt bosses of the real carved frame. A black/dark-grey edge sits under the band.
- **Upswept eave ends** [P]: at the foot of each bargeboard the band kicks outward and up, carrying a hang hong finial. Built as a short separate piece at an angle (clip/hinge), not a curved mould.
- **Chofa (ridge-end horn) and hang hong (eave-end hooks)** [P]: **Pearl Gold** S-curved horn-like accessories, ~3–4 studs tall on the ridge, smaller and hooked at the eave ends. On the ridge each sits on a small gold round knob. Which mould is not stated. Candidates in the right shape and in LDraw [M]: **Minifig Tail Dragon (35756)**, **Bar 0.5L with Curved Blade 2L (87747, BrickLink "barb/claw/horn")**, **Plant Stem Curved with 2 Thorns and 2 Swirls (28870)**, **Minifig Helmet Viking Horn (53451, BrickLink "barb/horn small")** for the small teeth. Identify by eye against the LDraw renders before choosing.
- **Gable carving (khrua)** [P]: on the Dark Red field, **Pearl Gold scrolls** (curled bars: [M] Plant Vine Curled with Bar 1997, Plant Stem Curved 28870) and **gold four-lobed leaf/flower ornaments** ([M] Plate 1 × 1 Round with Tabs 33291, Plate 1 × 1 with 5 Petals 24866, or 1 × 1 swirl plates 15470/53119). The Dark Red field itself is studs-out plates with round 1 × 1 accents: the "SNOT carving" approach.
- **Columns** [P]: **Pearl Gold fluted columns**, reading as stacked **2 × 2 round bricks with grille (92947)** with collars; flanked by Dark Red square posts. Balustrade: gold 1 × 1 baluster elements between Dark Red rails.
- **Naga stair rail** [P]: **White**, brick-built: a body of offset 1 × 2 rounded plates/curved slopes stepping down the stair ("scales"), raised heads built from **brackets and plates with white horn/tusk pieces** as the crest (`moc-jonsgard-naga-stair.jpg`). Stair treads **Dark Orange** tiles on White.
- **Walls**: White and Tan masonry walls; dark window grilles (bars/fences). Base: white plinth with tile-capped steps.
- **Chedi** (`moc-jonsgard-chedi.jpg`) [P]: a White stepped square base indented at the corners (redented), each tier a plate or brick smaller, a white bell, then a **Pearl Gold** stacked spire (cones and round plates). Small gold corner chedis on the base corners.
- **Life layer** [P]: orange-robed monks with bowls (alms round), a farmer in a **tan conical hat (93059)** beside bamboo (tall green bars + **30176 Plant Brick 1 × 1 Round with 3 Bamboo Leaves**), a bodhi tree, offering tables.
- **Nails**: the stacked gable, the chofa silhouette, the carved gold-on-red gable, the naga, all at minifig scale. **Misses (for us)**: it is Lanna (northern), not Central Thai / Ayutthaya like the Sanctuary of Truth; the roof is plain reddish brown, while the Sanctuary of Truth is natural teak with no paint.

### 1.2 LEGOLAND Malaysia, Miniland Asia – Thailand (1:20, since 2012)
Images (Wikimedia Commons, all CC BY-SA 2.0): `miniland-my-watarun-commons.jpg` ("Wat Arun Legoland Malaysia", milst1, 2013), `miniland-my-r0005560-commons.jpg` (Flickr 15875651732), `miniland-my-26279637960.jpg`, `miniland-my-25949607983.jpg` ("Malaysia – Legoland", Flickr 26279637960 / 25949607983). Also `moc-ragne-miniland-watarun.jpg` (Flickr ragne.lego 8404618004).
Facts [S]: Miniland Asia, 1:20 scale, 17 Asian countries, >30 million bricks; Thailand shown with the Temple of Dawn (Wat Arun). https://www.legoland.com.my/explore/theme-park/rides-attractions/miniland-asia/
- **Prang (Wat Arun)** [P]: a stack of **redented (stepped-corner) square tiers**, each tier a band of White/Tan bricks with a stripe of **Green, Orange, Yellow, Red** (the porcelain mosaic). The tiers shrink one stud a side per band, so the stepped corners read as the carved indentations. Upper tower: **vertical ribs**, a pattern of White and Orange/Red 1 × 1 elements, closing to a rounded top with a small gold finial. Four satellite prangs repeat the same recipe at half size.
- **Mosaic texture**: small coloured elements (1 × 1 round plates / tiles, studs) set into the white bands, and **rows of small Yellow elements** as the frieze of guardian figures. Rich at 1:20; at our scale one coloured band per tier is the maximum.
- **Ubosot halls (bottom right of the first image; front of the third)** [P]: **two-tier stacked roofs, Red field with a Green border band and a Dark Blue/Orange edge**, gable fronts with **Yellow/Pearl Gold bargeboards** and a row of short gold teeth (bai raka) along each rake, **gold chofa hooks** at the gable tips and eave ends, White walls, red-and-white gable panel. Fences: Red/White.
- **Life layer** [P]: royal barge (Suphannahong) with a brick-built Yellow swan prow; a **small longtail boat with a red canopy** on the water (`miniland-my-25949607983.jpg`, right); a **tuk-tuk** on the quay; a white **sala** (open pavilion) with a stepped roof; a yaksha guardian; Thai minifigs with umbrellas.
- **Nails**: Miniland's colour discipline (white + tile bands), the redented prang, the Red/Green roof. **Misses**: the halls' roofs are flat studded fields, with little upsweep at the eaves.

### 1.3 Jared Chan, "Songkran Festival, Thailand" (Wat Benchamabophit, ACGHK 2015, miniland figures)
URL: https://www.eurobricks.com/forum/forums/topic/112912-songkran-festival-thailand/ (photos on Brickshelf, jaredchan/Songkran) · images `moc-jaredchan-songkran-5364.jpg` (overview), `-5372.jpg` (front), `-5387.jpg` (roof detail), `-5426.jpg` (palms, water).
- [S] Customised Wat Benchamabophit, built for the LEGO booth at ACGHK 2015; miniland elephant and figures by Vincent Lai and YM Pak.
- **Roof** [P]: **multi-tier stacked roofs in Orange with Dark Orange and Reddish Brown rows**: studded rows stepping down, each row a plate higher and a stud in, giving the stacked overlapping look of glazed tiles. The central gable is the tallest, with the side wings one tier lower.
- **Gable** [P]: a white bargeboard with a red inner line and **gold hook teeth (bai raka) along the rake**, a tall **gold chofa horn** at the apex, gold carved tympanum, gold window frames on white walls (Benchamabophit's white marble).
- Ground: tan studded plates with **trans-clear and trans-light-blue 1 × 1 plates and 1 × 2 tiles scattered for splashed water** (Songkran). Palms from the official palm leaves on brown segmented trunks (`-5426`).
- **Lesson**: at a size close to ours, the stepped studded roof plus white/gold gable already reads Thai. The chofa is the one detail that cannot be skipped.

### 1.4 Anne Mette Vestergård, "LEGO Holy Redeemer Church Bangkok" (2024, microscale)
URL: https://www.brothers-brick.com/2024/08/28/thai-style-church-gets-the-lego-architecture-treatment/ · Flickr https://www.flickr.com/photos/74909731@N02/53943528057/ · image `moc-vestergard-holyredeemer.jpg`
- [S]: "square columns, stacked gables, enameled tiles, and decorative chofa along the roof edges"; **"Unikitty tails in white"** as the Thai flourishes (no LDraw file for the Unikitty tail under that name).
- **Roof** [P]: **fish-scale rows of small round-ended tiles** (reads as 1 × 1 round tiles or 1 × 1 tiles with rounded end, 24246 [M]) laid in overlapping rows on the slope, in bands: **Dark Green field, Orange border, Dark Red outer border**, exactly the glazed-tile banding of Central Thai roofs. The stacked lower gables repeat the same band as a frame.
- **Bargeboards** White, with a row of **white curved horns (bai raka) standing up along each rake**, every ~2 studs, and a white spiked finial on the ridge.
- **Lesson**: the **colour banding (field + border + outer border)** is what makes a roof read "Thai" at small size. Directly usable on a teak build too, as Dark Brown field / Reddish Brown border / Medium Nougat edge.

### 1.6 What this means for the Sanctuary of Truth
The real building [S Wikipedia/Thai sources]: all wood (teak, *takhian thong*, *daeng*), no nails, 105 m, cross plan (*chaturamukh*), carved on every surface; four porches, each a stack of steep gables; a central tower (prang-like *mondop* spire) with tiers of figures.
- **Colour**: no builder has done a natural-teak Thai roof. Borrow Vestergård's banding in wood tones: **Reddish Brown field, Dark Brown border/shade, Medium Nougat or Dark Tan for lit carving, Pearl Gold only for finials** [proposal, needs photo sampling].
- **Roof**: Jonsgard's **stacked gables with studs-out bargeboards** + Chan's **stepped studded tiers**. For the shingle texture: cheese slopes / grille tiles in offset rows on hinged plates.
- **Chofa / hang hong**: curved horn accessories (§1.1 candidates) on the gable tips and every eave kick-up; a row of small horns (53451) or 1 × 1 tooth plates (49668, 15070) along each rake as the bai raka.
- **Spire**: Miniland's **redented stacked tiers** (one stud in per tier) topped with cones (4589, 3942c, 59900), round plates/dishes (6141, 4740, 14769) and a bar/antenna finial.
- **Carving** at our size: studs-out plates (exposed studs = bosses), 1 × 2 grille tiles (2412b), masonry bricks (98283), 1 × 1 scroll bricks (20310), 2 × 2 round grille bricks (92947) for carved posts, headlight bricks (4070) for recessed niches.

### 2.2 Water and shoreline techniques
- **Sarah Beyer, Tahiti-style holiday hut** (Flickr 149907857@N08, BB 2018) · `moc-beyer-tahiti-overview.jpg`, `moc-beyer-tahiti-shoreline.jpg` [P]: water = **1 × 2 tiles in offset bands, Trans-Light-Blue near the shore → Trans-Medium-Blue → Trans-Dark-Blue offshore**, laid over (blue) studs so the studs show through as ripples; the **surf line is a row of Trans-Clear 1 × 1 round plates** standing on the tiles; the sand is **Tan and Dark Tan studded plates**, studs left showing; a palm with a Reddish Brown stacked trunk and large palm leaves; a stilt hut. [S BB: "layered transparent tiles creating a wonderful shoreline effect"]
- **Huynh Khang & Ky Duy Phong, pirate ship diorama** (BB 2023-01-13, Flickr 52620801740) · `moc-khang-phong-choppy-sea.jpg`: "tile pieces for the calmer bits of the sea; clear and white studded plates for the choppier parts". [S] Foam = White + Trans-Clear studded plates. [P]
- **Eurobricks "what do you use as sea" (topic 172304)**: wet sand = Dark Tan at the waterline, Tan a few studs back; trans tiles or studs over the base; raised trans-clear slopes as waves. [S]
- **rt_bricks, Villa Amanzi (Phuket, Flickr 11278456696)** · `moc-rtbricks-villa-amanzi-phuket.jpg`: a Phuket clifftop villa over a **grey limestone karst** of SNOT slope rockwork (Light/Dark Bluish Grey), jungle in Dark Green/Green/Olive, and a narrow sea strip of **Trans-Light-Blue tiles with Trans-Clear accents** at the cliff foot. [P] Best reference for a Thai coast with rock (Pratumnak headland, Koh Larn).
- **Official**: 31063 uses an *opaque* Medium Azure inlet; 41428 uses Medium Azure + Aqua. [S inventory][P] Our renderer draws trans at 0.62 opacity without depth write (Shanghai pipeline trap), so **trans tiles over an opaque blue/tan base**, not stacks of trans, is the safe form.

### 2.3 Palm trees (proven recipes)
1. **Official modern (60328, 41428)**: Reddish Brown 2 × 2 round brick base → stack of **round pin joiners (29219)** as trunk segments (they also give a slight bend) → **palm top (2566b) or a bar holder** → **finger leaves (10884/42949) or small palm leaves (6148)** radiating. [S inventory + P]
2. **Classic Pirates/Paradisa**: **2563a palm base + 6135/2536 trunk segments + 2566b top + 2518 large palm leaves**, or 30338 base 4 × 4 + 30339 quadruple leaf. (2518c01 is the complete tree.) [M on sets; parts verified in LDraw]
3. **Jared Chan Songkran** (`-5426`): classic segmented trunk + large leaves, green. [P]
4. **Full Plate Builds tutorial** (2018, https://fullplatebuilds.com/2018/11/21/palm-tree-tutorial/ , `moc-fullplate-palm-009.png`): **cones threaded on a flex tube** (curved trunk), a Dark Tan bush inverted as the dead-frond skirt, **4 life rings (30340) each carrying 5 sword leaves (10884)**, an Olive Green leaf at the bottom of each set for dead fronds. [S] Reads most like a real coconut palm, but **flex tubes are not modelled in our pipeline** (need a baked LDraw sub-build).
5. **31063**: palm fronds from **curved 2 × 1 slopes (11477)** in two greens on a bent trunk. A Creator shortcut. [S inventory][P]

## 3. Hillside letter sign ("PATTAYA CITY" on Pratumnak Hill)
Full study already in the LA worktree: `../lego-la/docs/refs/la-hollywood-sign/NOTES.md` (+ `SCHEME.md`, `BUILD.md`, 25 images). Copied here: `moc-brickman-hollywood-front.jpg`, `moc-brickblvd-hollywood-micro.jpg`, `miniland-hollywood-coastermadmatt.jpg`; new here `moc-imaxedlp-hollywood-sign.jpg` (Ideas 2026, Yanko Design).
- **No official set of any hillside sign.** [S, LA notes]
- **Brickman (Ryan McNaught, 2016, minifig scale)**: **White bricks studs-up, letter face = brick sides, 1–2 studs thick**, 1-stud stair steps for rounded letters, open slot counters, on **Light Bluish Grey bar legs with grey bracing** behind; hill of terraced Dark Tan plates. ≈ 8,500 bricks. [S/P via LA notes]
- **Miniland (LEGOWOOD, HOLLYWOOD)**: white brick letters studs-up pushed into turf, no legs. [P]
- **imaxedlp (Ideas, 496 pcs)**: Light Bluish Grey letters, plates/bricks facing forward, 1 × 1 holes as counters, **bar legs on round plates**, letters at slight angles, terraced tan hill. [S]
- **BrickBlvd micro**: 2-stud SNOT letters, **round moulds for O and D** (2 × 2 round ring / round tile). [P]
- **For Pattaya**: the real sign is **white sans-serif letters "PATTAYA CITY" on the green Pratumnak hillside, on legs** [M, verify against photos], so the Brickman recipe (white, studs-up, 3-column grid, legs) on a **green** slope. 11 characters, so a background sign (BrickBlvd size, ~2 studs per letter) to stay inside the island; printed 1 × 2 tiles would be L2 and lettering rules apply, so brick-built letters are cleaner.

## Gaps (what no builder has done at our scale)
1. **Sanctuary of Truth**: never built in LEGO at any scale. Nobody has built a **natural-teak, unpainted Thai roof**; every Thai roof found is red/orange/green glazed or gold. The cross plan (four stacked-gable porches around a central spire) has no LEGO precedent.
2. **Carving everywhere**: Jonsgard carves one gable; nobody has textured a whole building's walls and spire with carving at minifig scale. We will need our own vocabulary (studs-out fields, grille tiles, scroll bricks), checked against photos.
3. **Upswept eave tips at minifig scale**: all builds put a separate finial on a straight eave; none curves the eave line itself.
4. **Pattaya Bay as a set**: no Pattaya build anywhere (Flickr "lego pattaya" = only minifig travel photos). No minifig MOC of a **beach-chair-and-umbrella row** (the rental lines along Beach Road), a **songthaew**, a **longtail boat**, a **parasail rising off a speedboat**, a **Thai seafood grill / night-market stall**, or **Walking Street neon**.
5. **Hillside sign**: done for Hollywood (white on brown); "PATTAYA CITY" is longer (11 letters), so it has to be done smaller, on a green hill.
6. **Coral under water**: builders show reefs dry or in section; nobody has shown coral *through* a trans tile surface at minifig scale. Our renderer's trans handling makes this a test-render item.
7. **Elephant**: the good 2021 mould has no LDraw file; the LDraw elephant is the 2003 design.
