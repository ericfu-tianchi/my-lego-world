### 0.1 Lifeguard towers (LA County standard)
- **Facts** (lamag.com): 158 towers on the county coast. The design is "based on models from the 1950s", with **wide eaves** and **angled, UV-protected windows**. In **2001** the unified county service adopted a **blue wood** tower as the standard. Towers are dragged inland by tractor in winter. A **bucket at the foot of the ramp** for washing sand off feet is a permanent fixture.
  - Venice's **Brooks Ave tower is painted as the rainbow Pride flag** permanently, by Board of Supervisors vote in 2017 (CBS LA, LA Blade).
  - The famous multi-colour pastel towers are **not** Venice. Most Venice towers are **baby blue** (LA Blade: "traditional baby blue").
- **Form** (`real-tower-a.jpg`, `real-tower-19.jpg`):
  - a **square cabin** with slightly **outward-leaning (raked) glass** on the sea faces;
  - a flat roof with a **deep overhanging eave on all sides**, deepest over the sea face;
  - a wraparound **deck with a pipe/plank railing**, on **four squat stilts with X-bracing** in two planes;
  - a long **ramp with rails** running from the back of the deck down to the sand at ≈ 25–30°;
  - a **flagpole** at one rear corner (US flag), and **two red-orange rescue cans** hanging from the eave;
  - the tower number painted on the side in black stencil.
- **Measured** (`real-tower-19.jpg`, calibrated on the lifeguard standing on the deck = 1.75 m; cross-checked on `real-tower-a.jpg`):
  - cabin wall height ≈ 2.25 m and cabin width ≈ 2.1 m, so the cabin is **near-cubic**;
  - stilts, sand to deck ≈ 0.95–1.1 m (sand drift varies), so **cabin height ≈ 2.2 × stilt height**;
  - the deck projects ≈ 0.6 m past the cabin on the front and side;
  - roof overhang ≈ 0.4 m, ≈ 0.6 m over the sea face;
  - the ramp is ≈ 2.5–3 × the stilt height long;
  - the flagpole stands ≈ 2.2 m above the roof.
- **At minifig scale** (1 stud ≈ 0.33 m) the cabin is **6 × 6 studs × ≈ 20 plates** and the stilts ≈ 3 bricks. That is almost exactly the size official 60328 uses. **A compressed version** that still holds a minifig: a 4 × 4 cabin, 6 × 6 deck, 2–3-brick stilts, 6–8-stud ramp.
- **Colours:**
  - baby-blue walls in sun (184,216,227), rail (206,232,232) → nearest **Aqua** (179,215,209; a rare colour), then **Bright Light Blue** (159,195,233), then Light Aqua (173,195,192). **Bright Light Blue** has by far the larger mould range.
  - trim, window frames and eave edge → **White**;
  - glass → **Trans-Black** / trans-clear over dark;
  - rescue cans → **Red / Orange**;
  - lifeguard truck → **Yellow** with red "LIFEGUARD" (Bright Light Orange in 60453).

### 0.2 Venice Beach Skatepark (opened 3 Oct 2009)
- **Facts** (Wikipedia, surfertoday, palisadesnews): 16,000 sq ft (≈ 1,490 m²) by Windward Ave and Ocean Front Walk, **on the sand**. Designed by Zach Wormhoudt with Jesse Martinez, with input from Christian Hosoi and Pat Ngoho; cost $3.5 M.
  - **A 9-ft-deep clover bowl, a shallower flow bowl, a snake run ending in bowls at both ends, and a street section** with steps, rails and platforms.
- **Look** (`real-skatepark*.jpg`):
  - **kidney and clover bowls sunk into a flat concrete apron**, with smooth transitions and a vertical wall at the deep end;
  - a **pool-coping lip** around the rim, which reads as a slightly darker edge line;
  - low concrete ledges and benches;
  - a **pipe railing** around the perimeter with **sand right up to it** and the ocean behind;
  - **no graffiti on the bowl surfaces** (they are skated clean). Graffiti sits on nearby ledges and the art walls (§0.4).
- **Colours:** concrete (131,133,128)–(144,143,133) → **Light Bluish Gray**; shaded bowl walls → **Light Bluish Gray** with Dark Bluish Gray shadows; coping → **Light Bluish Gray** or Flat Silver; railing → Dark Bluish Gray / Black.
- **Scale:** the real park would be ≈ 150 × 90 studs at strict scale. **Compress to ≈ 14–18 × 10–12 studs**: one clover or kidney bowl plus a short snake run.
  - Real depth 9 ft ≈ 7 bricks; **compress to 1–2 bricks**.
  - **Key fit:** the island is a floating slab two bricks of earth deep (`base` 3). A bowl **sunk into that earth** needs no raised walls. It is the real arrangement (bowls are dug into the beach), and it saves parts.

### 0.3 Muscle Beach Venice
- **Look** (`real-musclebeach*.jpg`):
  - a **rectangular fenced pen on a concrete pad**, on the sand side of the boardwalk;
  - a **stainless-steel post-and-mesh fence** ≈ 1.2 m high with a **chunky segmented base** (the "chain-link" relief along the plinth);
  - inside: weight benches, power racks, pull-up bars, cable machines, free weights;
  - a raised stage/platform at the back;
  - a curved corrugated-metal **"MUSCLE BEACH VENICE" pavilion**;
  - the signature: a **giant barbell gate**, a silver-grey arch topped by a bar with **huge navy-blue plates/boxes at each end**.
  - The original Muscle Beach was in Santa Monica, south of the pier (1934–1959); "Original Muscle Beach" there today has **gymnastic rings, bars and ropes on the sand** [M, plus the Commons file title "Santa Monica Original Muscle Beach"].
- **Colours:** fence and frame steel → **Flat Silver / Light Bluish Gray**; barbell plates (15,28,44) to (38,52,89) in shade → **Dark Blue**; pad → Light Bluish Gray; rubber mats → Black / Dark Bluish Gray.
- **Size** [M]: pen ≈ 30 × 20 m → compress to ≈ 8 × 6 studs with the barbell gate as its sign.

### 0.4 Venice Art Walls (veniceartwalls.com)
- **Facts:** these are the surviving walls of the **Venice Pavilion (1961)**, an area called "The Pit". Painting there was tolerated, and became **legal in 2000**. The walls stood on after the pavilion was demolished in 1999–2000. Venice is "one of five cities in the world that allows public art on public property".
- **Look** (`real-art-walls-pit.jpg`, `real-art-walls-2.jpg`):
  - several **free-standing, straight concrete walls**, each ≈ 2–2.5 m high and 10–20 m long [estimate], **standing in the sand**;
  - one tall **painted column/tower** (the pavilion remnant);
  - every face **fully covered**: wildstyle letters with black outlines and white highlights, in yellow, orange, red, violet/lavender, sky blue and lime;
  - repainted constantly.
  - A concrete path cuts across the sand to them, and palms stand behind.
- **For LEGO:** walls 1 stud thick, 6–10 studs long, 5–7 bricks high, on sand. The graffiti is an **L2 print**: Canvas-drawn wildstyle with ≤ 4 flat colours, black outlines, **no readable words** (generic letter shapes only). This fits the print rules: a generic word or none, legible as "graffiti" at thumbnail size. Count the faces against the ≤ 12 designs budget.

### 0.5 Ocean Front Walk (the boardwalk) and Windward Ave
- **Look** (`real-boardwalk.jpg`): a **wide paved promenade**, not timber, with a **Sand Blue / Medium Blue painted wave pattern** in the paving at Windward.
  - Sand side: a **palm row** in small sand pockets, a separate **bike path**, then the beach.
  - Land side: one- and two-storey stalls under awnings, a building wrapped in Rip Cronk's sky-blue **"Venice Reconstituted"** mural, and the **Surfside arcade** with round arches (Venice's 1905 colonnades).
- **Life:** roller skaters (the Venice roller-dance plaza [M]), street performers, buskers, cyclists, skateboarders, vendors. **Minifig vocabulary:** roller skate `11253`, skateboard `42511`, the barbells `29592`, plus a guitar or boombox.
- **Colours:** paving (118,114,109) under overcast → **Light Bluish Gray** / Tan concrete; mural sky blue (62,105,134 in shade) → **Medium Blue / Dark Azure**; the arcade stucco → **Tan / Light Nougat**.
- **VENICE sign:** separate letters V-E-N-I-C-E (aluminium with LEDs, **33 in tall, 63 ft span**, hung on a cable over Windward Ave in **2007** as a replica of Abbot Kinney's **1905** sign; LA Magazine). The letters are white or silver block serifs with bulbs.
  - At our scale the 63 ft span is ≈ 58 studs at strict scale, which is impossible. **It reads only over a street, and our island has no Windward Ave.** **Against**, unless the boardwalk element gets a street mouth.

### 0.6 Palms (Washingtonia robusta, the Mexican fan palm)
- **Silhouette:** a **very tall, thin, slightly tapering trunk** topped by a **small round crown** of fan leaves, often with a **brown "skirt" of dead fronds** just under the green.
- **Measured** on two photos (`real-boardwalk.jpg` nearest palm; `real-tower-19.jpg` beach palms):
  - crown spread ≈ **0.27–0.29 H**;
  - green crown height ≈ 0.25 H;
  - skirt ≈ 0.10–0.12 H;
  - trunk Ø ≈ **0.03–0.04 H**.
  - The beach palms are ≈ 20–30 m tall [M]. They far outreach the two-storey boardwalk (≈ 3–4 × building height).
- **Colours:**
  - live fronds: olive in shade (78,90,44), lime-olive in sun (133,141,45) → **Olive Green** with some **Green**; Bright Green is too toy-like for these palms, though official City sets use it;
  - skirt: dry fronds (173,143,56) → **Dark Tan**, with shadowed parts in **Reddish Brown**;
  - trunk: grey-brown → **Dark Tan / Dark Brown**, with a darker base.
- *Note:* real fan-palm leaves are palmate (fan-shaped). Every LEGO palm uses the pinnate palm-leaf moulds, and the silhouette, not the leaf type, is what reads. Some Venice palms are Canary Island date palms (thick trunk, pinnate) [M], so pinnate leaves are not wrong for Venice.

### 0.7 Sand and water (same bay as Santa Monica)
- Sand sunlit (224,193,167)–(231,201,173) → **Tan**; overcast (174,168,152)–(186,184,166) → Tan; the wet strip → **Dark Tan**.
- The sea shows a white surf band. The bay reads grey-blue → **Sand Blue**, as in `../la-santa-monica/NOTES.md` §0.7.

**Technique for the signature details:**
- **Raked sea windows:** windscreen or slope-glass moulds (e.g. a 2 × 4 × 2 windscreen) set outward. Alternatively, trans-black panels on a hinge tilted ≈ 10°.
- **Deep eaves:** a roof plate one stud larger than the cabin on all sides, two studs on the sea face, with a White tile fascia.
- **Stilts:** four 1 × 1 round bricks or 2 × 2 corner posts with **X-braces** from bars and clips. Keep it simpler with plates if the braces will not fit cleanly.
- **Ramp:** a hinged 2 × N plate at ≈ 25–30° with bar-and-clip rails, landing on the sand on a jumper. The bucket at its foot is a minifig accessory.

## 5. Palms: brick versions
1. **Official palm system (LDraw):**
   - `2563a` Plant Tree Palm Base, `6135b` Plant Tree Palm Trunk (stackable segments), `2566b` Plant Tree Palm Top, `2518` Plant Tree Palm Leaf Large, `6148` Plant Palm Leaf Small, `30339` Plant Tree Palm Leaf Quadruple, `30338` Palm Base 4 × 4.
   - In current City/Friends/Creator sets (60328, 41710, 31118, 41751 box images) the trunk is Reddish Brown segments with Bright Green large leaves [M for the exact moulds].
   - **Lesson:** a real, purpose-made palm system. The **segmented trunk reads as palm bark**. Stacked segments give height, but the trunk then looks **chunky and short**: an official palm is ≈ 8–12 bricks with a crown as wide as it is tall, the opposite of the Washingtonia silhouette.
2. **Full Plate Builds palm tutorial (2018)** · `moc-palm-fullplate.png`, https://fullplatebuilds.com/2018/11/21/palm-tree-tutorial/
   - **Cones threaded on a flex tube** for a tall, slightly bent trunk, plus a Dark Tan bush upside down under the crown as the dead-frond skirt.
   - Fronds: **4 flotation-device hubs with 5 sword leaves each, an Olive Green leaf at the bottom** for the wilted fronds.
   - **Lesson:** the **skirt under the crown** and the **olive/dry lower fronds** are exactly the Washingtonia read. *Flex tube and its cones: check they exist in LDraw, and whether a bent trunk passes the stud-connection gate. A straight stack of 1 × 1 round bricks or cones on a bar is safer.*
3. **LEGOLAND Miniland San Diego (2023)** · `miniland-sd-coronado-palms.jpg`
   - 120 palms. **Thin brown stacked trunks**, ≈ 3–4 × figure height, with small tufts of green palm leaves. They are tall and slender, not toy-chunky.
   - **Lesson:** Merlin keeps the trunk **1 stud thin and tall**, with a small crown. That is the right ratio for California.
4. **Ben Andrews, Palm Hill Skate Park** · `moc-lego-palm-hill-skate-park.jpg`
   - Tall, slightly curved trunks with Green palm-leaf crowns set in a skate deck.
5. **Flickr group "LEGO Palm Trees"** (https://www.flickr.com/groups/lego-palm-trees/): mostly Pirates-style coconut palms with chunky crowns. Nothing tall and thin. Not saved.

**Palm technique for our scale (the Washingtonia read):**
- **Trunk:** a **1 × 1 round brick stack** (or `6135b` segments if their baked bounds stay ≈ 1 stud) in **Dark Tan / Dark Brown**, ≈ **25–35 plates tall**. Trunk Ø 1 stud on 30 plates ≈ 0.03 H, which matches the real ratio.
- **Skirt:** one ring of drooping leaves in **Dark Tan / Reddish Brown** directly under the crown.
- **Crown:** `2566b` palm top with **4–6 small leaves** (`6148`) or large leaves (`2518`) in **Olive Green / Green**. *Check the baked bounds:* the crown spread should be ≈ 0.27 H ≈ 3–4 studs on a 30-plate palm. The large leaf is probably too long, and the small leaf is the likely fit.
- This conflicts with the generic `tree(kind)` armature (2 × 2 trunk, four limbs per fork). **A palm is not a forked tree.** It needs its own kind: a single 1 × 1 axis, no forks, a crown at the top. Flag for the playbook.

## Borrow / avoid
**Borrow**
- **Tower:** a baby-blue (**Bright Light Blue**) near-cubic cabin with **raked trans-black sea windows**, **deep White eaves**, a wraparound railed deck on **4 X-braced stilts**, a **long ramp** to the sand, a flagpole, two red rescue cans and a bucket at the ramp foot. Size: 4 × 4 cabin, 6 × 6 deck, cabin ≈ 2.2 × stilt height.
  - Optionally **one Pride-rainbow tower** (real, permanent, Venice-specific); count it against the print budget if the stripes are printed rather than brick-built.
- **Skatepark:** **sunk into the island earth**; Light Bluish Gray kidney/clover bowl from **inverted curved slopes**, a tile floor, a coping ring and a perimeter pipe rail, with **sand right up to it**. Skaters on `42511`; **no graffiti in the bowl**.
- **Art walls on the sand beside it:** 2–3 free-standing 1-stud walls with **wildstyle L2 prints** (no words), plus the painted column.
- **Muscle Beach:** a silver-fenced pen on a grey pad, the **giant Dark Blue barbell gate**, a rack, rings and **one figure lifting**.
- **Boardwalk:** a Light Bluish Gray paved strip with a **Sand Blue wave pattern** in the tiles and a **palm row** along it; **one roller skater** (`11253`).
- **Palms:** tall, thin, small crown, dead-frond skirt; Olive/Green over Dark Tan.

**Avoid**
- Multi-colour pastel towers: those are not Venice.
- Official chunky palms with Bright Green crowns as wide as the tree is tall.
- A raised skate ramp on top of the ground: Venice's bowls are dug in.
- Graffiti on the bowl surfaces, or graffiti with readable words.
- The VENICE sign without a street to hang it over.
- More than 2–3 figures across all of this (rule 6). Eric may grant more here because the place's function is *people*, the way Prague's square got five. The obvious set is **skater, lifter, roller skater**, plus a lifeguard on the tower if granted.

**For / against including each**
- **Skatepark: for.** It is the strongest "skate culture" element, cheap when sunk into the earth, and gives a silhouette nobody else has.
- **Lifeguard tower: for** (one, maybe two). It is the beach's signature object; small, and full of readable detail.
- **Muscle Beach: for.** It is Eric's "working out by the sea", and the barbell gate signs it. There is no prior brick version, so expect one extra iteration.
- **Art walls: for**, as 2–3 short walls next to the skatepark, the way the real ones stand. Watch the print budget.
- **Boardwalk strip with palms: for.** It ties the elements together. Keep the shopfronts out (scale rule 13).
- **VENICE sign: against** (needs a street).
- **Venice Reconstituted mural building: against.** It is a building-scale mural, too big, and the art walls already carry "murals".
