# Prague: the real landmarks (research notes, 2026-10-02)

Facts are tagged by source:
- **[W]** English Wikipedia
- **[CZ]** Czech Wikipedia
- **[P]** read off the photos in this folder
- **[M]** from memory or general knowledge, not checked against a source this round. Verify before relying on it.

Scale note: at minifig scale 1 stud ≈ 0.35 m (a 4 cm minifig stands for a 1.75 m person), so every landmark here has to be heavily compressed. The real metres below are for proportions, not for counting studs.

## 1. Charles Bridge (Karlův most), Old Town Bridge Tower, Malá Strana bridge towers

**Bridge**
- Length 515.76 m. Width 9.4–9.5 m. Deck 13 m above normal water [CZ]. Length to width is about 54 : 1, so it reads as a long, thin ribbon.
- **16 arches on 15 piers** [CZ][W]. Spans run from 16.62 m (Old Town end) to 23.38 m [CZ], so the arches are unequal and the spans vary a little along the bridge. The arches are low and nearly round (segmental to semicircular), with thick piers [P].
- Every pier has a pointed cutwater. Upstream there are **wooden ice-breakers**: sloped timber ramps lying in the water in front of the piers [CZ][P]. They are a distinctive low detail.
- **30 statues and statue groups**, mostly Baroque (1683–1714), all of them replicas since 1965 [W]. They stand on the parapet directly above the piers, roughly one per pier on each side. In photos they are dark, almost black silhouettes on tall plinths [P].
- Material: Bohemian sandstone [W]. Colour in photos: warm honey-grey on the arches and piers (lighter in sun), weathered dark at the parapet [P].
- Lamps: pseudo-Gothic lanterns on the parapet since 1866 [W]. They were later electrified. Gas lighting was put back, and in Advent a lamplighter lights them by hand [M].
- Signature: **the line of dark statues on the parapet against the sky**, plus the dark gate tower at the end of the bridge.

**Old Town Bridge Tower** (Staroměstská mostecká věž, Parléř, from 1357)
- Height **47 m**, square plan [CZ]. Body to height (shaft only) is about 1 : 3.5. With the roof it is about 1 : 4 [P].
- Gate passage 8 m wide, with a net vault and a keystone shaped as a crown [CZ].
- Top: an open gallery with crenellation. At the four corners it runs out into **four small corner turrets with steep pointed roofs**. Above them sits a **tall, steep slate roof** with a timber truss over 600 years old [CZ]. Gilded ball finials sit on the spire tips [P].
- East face (towards the Old Town): sculpture tiers with St Vitus on a bridge model between Charles IV and Wenceslas IV, St Adalbert and St Sigismund above, and coats of arms [CZ].
- Colour: sandstone ashlar [CZ], **blackened to dark brown-grey, nearly black** [P]. Roofs are dark slate grey [P].
- Signature: **a dark, nearly black Gothic tower with a steep slate spire flanked by four pointed corner turrets**.

**Malá Strana bridge towers** (the Lesser Town end)
- Lower tower, the **Judith Tower**: Romanesque, about 30 m. It has a **tall hipped slate roof with a Renaissance gable dormer** [CZ][P].
- Higher tower: 45 m, built from 1464. Its outline matches the Old Town tower: crenellated gallery, four corner turrets with spirelets, and a steep slate spire [CZ][P].
- The two towers are joined by a crenellated gate wall with one pointed arch. The walkway between them runs at 26 m [CZ].
- The pair is asymmetric: a short, fat hipped roof beside a tall, thin spire [P]. Seen from the bridge, St Nicholas (Malá Strana) shows its green dome between them [P].

## 2. Old Town Hall tower and the Astronomical Clock (Orloj)

**Tower**
- Height **69.5 m**, prismatic (square) [CZ]. Finished in 1364 and largely unchanged since [W].
- The top is a gallery with **four corner turrets with pointed roofs** and a tall central roof. It is the same family of outline as the bridge towers [M][P].
- Colour: warm beige-grey sandstone ashlar. Above the clock the stone turns rough and reddish-brown [P].
- The chapel has a **five-sided oriel** on the east side [CZ].
- The Town Hall is a row of houses joined together [CZ][W]:
  - Wolflin house, with the tower
  - Kříž house
  - Mikeš house (Neo-Renaissance)
  - House At the Rooster
  - **Minute House** (U Minuty), at the corner towards Malé náměstí. Its whole facade is covered in **black-and-white sgraffito** [W][M].

**Orloj** (on the south face of the tower)
- Vertical stack, read top to bottom [P][CZ]:
  1. A **green copper Gothic pinnacle** and a copper-edged stone hood (canopy) over everything.
  2. Two small **apostle windows**, shuttered with a blue ground. A stone angel sits between them, with a small gold rooster figure in a niche above. The rooster was added in 1865–66 [W].
  3. The **astronomical dial**, 2.6 m [CZ]. The outer ring is about 3 m [W].
  4. A gallery band.
  5. The **calendar dial**, a gold-yellow disc with 12 month medallions and a central coat of arms [P]. Josef Mánes designed the original in 1865; the current copy dates from 2018 [CZ].
- Dial colours [CZ][P]:
  - **upper zone light blue** (day sky)
  - **brown-orange crescents** for dawn and dusk (AURORA, CREPUSCULUM, ORTUS, OCCASUS)
  - **black disc low in the dial** (night)
  - outer ring blue-grey with **gold Roman numerals and gold Bohemian (Schwabacher) numerals**
  - eccentric **gold zodiac ring**
  - gold hand with a gilded sun
  - moon sphere half silver, half black
- Figures:
  - beside the astronomical dial: Vanity (mirror) and the Miser (purse) on the left; **Death (a gold-bone skeleton with an hourglass and a bell rope)** and the Turk on the right [W][CZ][P]
  - beside the calendar dial: the Philosopher and **Archangel Michael**, then the Astronomer and the Chronicler [CZ]
- Frame: dark weathered Gothic stone tracery, nearly black-brown, which makes the gold and blue pop [P].
- Proportion: the whole clock assembly is about 1 : 3 (width : height), roughly the lower third of the tower face [P].
- Signature: **a big blue-and-gold astrolabe dial with the skeleton beside it**, a gold calendar disc below, and the two apostle windows above.

## 3. Týn Church (Church of Our Lady before Týn)

- Two towers, both **80 m** [W][CZ]. The north tower was finished in 1466 and the south in 1511 [CZ]. The north tower (nicknamed "Adam") is noticeably wider than the south ("Eve") [M]. You can see this in photos as an asymmetry.
- Each tower has:
  - a square shaft of light grey-beige stone with a tall pointed window [P]
  - a gallery
  - a **tall, steep octagonal helmet** [W][CZ]
  - **four corner turrets at gallery level and four more decorative turrets halfway up the helmet**, which is "eight smaller spires in two layers of four" [W]
  - a **gilded ball finial** on every tip [P]
- That is 9 points per tower and 18 in total. Helmets and turrets are dark grey (slate or lead) [P].
- Between the towers is a Gothic gable with rows of finials and a **gilded Madonna** on a gold oval [W][P]. A gilded chalice was put back in 2016–17 [W].
- **The church's lower half is hidden from the square** by the arcaded Týn School and the houses in front of it. Those houses are cream-white with Renaissance and Baroque gables and red-tile roofs. The entrance is through a passage between them [W][CZ][P]. From the square you see only the two towers rising out of a gabled house front.
- Height ratio: the towers stand about 4× above the front houses' cornice [P].
- Signature: **twin dark, spiky multi-spired helmets with gold balls**. It is the "Disney castle" outline of Prague.

## 4. Prague Castle and St Vitus as seen from the river

- The castle complex is about 570 m long and 130 m wide, the largest ancient castle complex (Guinness) [M]. It sits on a ridge above Malá Strana.
- From the river (Alšovo nábřeží or Smetanovo nábřeží) [P] you see:
  - a **long, flat horizontal palace front**, pale cream-white plaster with many regular windows and **red-orange tile roofs**
  - **St Vitus** rising behind it on the left
  - the Strahov twin towers and the Lobkowicz palace further along
  - Malá Strana's red-tile roofs piled up below
  - a green wooded slope on the right
- **St Vitus**: 124 m × 60 m. Main tower **102.8 m**, twin west spires **82 m** [W].
  - Main tower: a green copper Renaissance helmet (an onion and lantern stack) with a gold clock [P][M]
  - West spires: a pair of **dark grey, thin Neo-Gothic spires**
  - Roof: dark, with a green copper ridge flèche [P][M]
  - The south side has the Golden Gate mosaic [W]
- Ratio from the river: the palace front is a low band. The cathedral's spires stand roughly 2–3× the palace's height above the ridge line [P].
- Signature: **a long white palace with red roofs on a hill, broken by the dark twin Gothic spires and the green-capped tower of St Vitus**.

## 5. Old Town Square burgher houses

- Mixed Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque and Rococo fronts. Most are 3–4 storeys plus a gable [P][W].
- Plaster colours in the aerial photo [P]: cream, **ochre-yellow**, **salmon/orange-pink**, **pale blue** (with white stucco frames), white, and pale green. Window surrounds are white stucco, and many windows have red flower boxes.
- Roofs: **red-orange clay tile**, steep. Some have **green copper** roofs, dormers or turrets [P].
- Ground floors: **Gothic arcades (podloubí)** with round or pointed arches. Inside, a rib vault painted white or yellow over dark stone ribs, for example at the House at the White Unicorn [P].
- Key fronts:
  - **Kinský Palace**: pink and white Rococo [W]
  - **House at the Stone Bell**: a grey Gothic stone tower-house with the bell carved on its corner [M]
  - **Storch House**: a painted facade with St Wenceslas on horseshoe-white, a Gothic oriel with a small slate spire, and gold-and-black lettering "SVATÝ VÁCLAVE VÉVODO ČESKÉ ZEMĚ" [W][P]
  - **St Nicholas (Old Town)**: white Baroque with green copper domes [W]
  - **Jan Hus Memorial** (1915) in the middle of the square: dark bronze-green on a granite base [W][M]
- Paving [P]: a large **diagonal grid of light-grey squares framed by darker-grey bands**, made of small square setts. Where the Old Town Square Execution took place there are **27 white crosses** in the pavement [W].
- Signature: **pastel gabled fronts over arcades, under red-tile roofs**.

## 6. Dancing House (Tančící dům)

- Vlado Milunić and Frank Gehry, 1992–96. **9 floors** above ground [W]. Height is about 30 m [M].
- On the riverbank corner of Rašínovo nábřeží and Jiráskovo náměstí [W].
- **"Ginger"** [W][P]:
  - a glass tower that **pinches at the waist** and leans
  - it stands on curved, leaning pillars
  - the glass is pale grey-blue with steel mullions
- **"Fred"** [W][P]:
  - a solid **cylinder in off-white or cream plaster** with **staggered, unaligned square windows with protruding frames**
  - crowned by **"Medusa"**, a dome of tangled stainless-steel strips
- The facade behind has wavy horizontal mouldings [W].
- Its neighbours are pastel Art Nouveau apartment houses [P]. Seen from the river it is about 1.5 km upstream of Charles Bridge, so the two do not share a frame in photos [M].
- Signature: **the pinched glass tower leaning on a white cylinder with a wire-tangle head**.

## 7. Prague trams

**Tatra T3** [W][P]
- Body 14.0 m × 2.5 m × 3.05 m. Two bogies, three folding doors.
- Prague livery: **red body with a cream band through the windows and cream skirt and bumper**, rounded "PCC" ends, and a yellow pantograph.
- Signature: a short, round-ended red-and-cream car, usually running as a pair.

**Škoda 15T ForCity** (Porsche design) [W][P]
- In Prague: 31.4 m × 2.46 m × 3.6 m, **three body sections**, 100 % low floor.
- Livery: **red lower skirt, silver-grey belt, black glazed upper band**. The front is a big raked black windscreen framed in red, with an orange LED destination display.
- Signature: a long, sleek tram with a black window band over red and silver.

## Also

- **Vltava banks**:
  - granite-faced embankment walls (nábřeží) with stone steps down to quays
  - the **weir (Staroměstský jez)** just upstream of Charles Bridge, a white line of water
  - islands: Kampa, Střelecký, Slovanský
  - pedal boats and river cruisers
  - **Čertovka** canal at Kampa with a mill wheel [M]
- **Swans**: mute swans gather at Kampa and the Smíchov bank near the bridge. They are white with an orange bill, and people feed them [M].
- **Street lamps** [P][M]:
  - Old Town lanterns are **dark green cast-iron posts with ornate bracket arms and glazed square lanterns**, often in pairs or triples (see tyn-from-square.jpg)
  - Charles Bridge lamps are dark lanterns on the parapet
  - some gas lamps survive and are maintained by lamplighters
- **Mozaika paving** [prague.eu][ourbeautifulprague]:
  - cubes about **6 × 6 cm**, all-mosaic since 1879
  - originally Slivenec limestone in **pink and blue-grey with white patterns**; later granite
  - named patterns include "frames and dragons", "stars and crosses" and "seventy with a lady"
  - pavements show patterned grey and white, for example diagonal squares or fans
- **Petřín Lookout Tower**: **63.5 m**, 1891, an Eiffel-style lattice with an **octagonal** cross-section and 299 steps [W]. Seen from the river it is a small steel-lattice spike on a wooded green hill [M].
- **Lennon Wall**: Velkopřevorské náměstí, Malá Strana, near Kampa. A long garden wall covered in layered, ever-changing **multicolour graffiti** (peace slogans, a John Lennon portrait) [M].

## Lettering culture

- **Street name plates** [P][M]:
  - **red enamel with white bold sans-serif capitals**
  - a **white line border with stepped (meander-like) corner ornaments**
  - a smaller second line gives the district and "PRAHA n", for example "VYSOČANY – PRAHA 9"
  - they are the typeface tradition hand-drawn 1890s–1990s and since restandardised [atypi]
- **House numbers**: two enamel plates on each house. The **red plate** holds the conscription number (číslo popisné, from 1770). The **blue plate** holds the street number. Both have white numerals [radio.cz].
- **House signs** (domovní znamení):
  - painted or stucco-relief emblems above the portal, often gilded: "U Zlatého klíče" (golden key), "U Dvou slunců" (two suns), "U Tří housliček" (three fiddles), "U Bílého jednorožce" (white unicorn), "U Kamenného zvonu" (stone bell)
  - 246 survive in Prague, about 70 in Malá Strana [prague.eu][radio.cz]
  - the lettering under the emblem is painted Roman capitals: "U ZLATÉHO …"
- **Shop signs**: hanging **wrought-iron bracket signs**, and Art Nouveau lettering painted on plaster, for example on the Storch house [P].
- **Metro**: a white **"M" in a downward-pointing triangle or arrow** (Jiří Rathouský, 1970s). Lines are A green, B yellow, C red [M].

## Hus — Jan Hus Memorial and the square's functions (added 2026-10-02)
- **Jan Hus Memorial** (Ladislav Šaloun, unveiled 6 July 1915; restored 2013–15) [W][CZ]: a wide, low, roughly elliptical base of Sedlčany granite (dark grey), about a person's height [P]; on it a hollow bronze ensemble (sheets bolted together), near-black with strong green patina [P]. Hus stands tall and gaunt left of centre, bareheaded, in a long robe, rising out of a "sea" of figures [CZ][P]; to the Týn side the Hussite warriors, to the other side the exiles after White Mountain, a young mother (national rebirth), a great tablet leaning upright among them [CZ][P]. Inscriptions cut into the base ("Milujte se, pravdy každému přejte…") [CZ]. A paved step and flower beds ring it, benches round the beds [P]. From far away: a long dark granite plinth with a dark-green figure group, one tall figure clearly above the rest [P]. Dimensions not given by the sources; proportions read off the photos [P].
- What gives the square its function today [P][M]: the crowd under the Orloj on the hour; café terraces with cream umbrellas along the house fronts; buskers and bubble-makers; horse carriages and vintage cars for tourists; seasonal market stalls (Easter / Christmas); the Marian column (re-erected 2020) near the Hus memorial; the 27 white crosses in the paving before the Old Town Hall (the nobles executed in 1621).
- Buildable and worth its parts at our scale: the Hus memorial (≈ 120 parts), two or three café tables under umbrellas (≈ 11 parts each), the Orloj crowd = the existing visitor + 1–2 more figures if Eric wants a busier square, the white crosses as 1×1 white tiles in the paving (≈ 10 parts, cheap and true). Not worth it: a horse carriage (a horse + carriage ≈ 60 parts, reads but crowds), market stalls (out of season for a summer scene), the Marian column (a second tall vertical competing with the towers).
