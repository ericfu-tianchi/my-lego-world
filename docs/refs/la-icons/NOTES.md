# Los Angeles icons: candidate survey for the island
2026-10-04 · research only, no code · images in this folder · [M] = from memory, not verified this session · about a third of the depth of `../shanghai-pearl/NOTES.md` per subject: the aim is to pick 4–5 places.

Scale used for "buildable" calls: minifig scale ≈ 1:42, so 1 stud ≈ 0.34 m. On a 60–80 stud island a hero landmark gets ≤ 0.6 × width ≈ 36–48 studs, so every building below is compressed 3–8× in plan, as with Kyoto and Prague.

## 0. Official LEGO and LEGOLAND coverage of LA

- **There is no official LEGO set of Los Angeles.** Brickset text search: "Los Angeles" returns only two E3 2005 Nvidia keychains (KCP04, KCP05). "Hollywood" returns 71000-3 *Hollywood Starlet* (CMF series 9) and 30601 *Scooby-Doo*. "Santa Monica" returns nothing.
- **Architecture Skylines:** no LA set. The US skylines are New York, Chicago, Las Vegas (21047) and San Francisco (21043). The line has not had a new skyline since Singapore (2022). A fan *Los Angeles Skyline* on LEGO Ideas (eddda007…, 1K supporters, 11 landmarks; its Capitol Records spire blinks "Hollywood" in Morse) was never made. Ideas returns 403, so I could not see it.
- **Official sets close to LA subjects** (box images saved):
  - 10232 *Palace Cinema* (2013, 2,196 pcs). Brick Fan calls it inspired by Grauman's Chinese Theatre and 1930s Hollywood: a "sidewalk of the stars", a limousine, and dark red, dark tan and gold. `brick-10232-palace-cinema.jpg`
  - 60233 *Donut Shop Opening* (City, 2019). A giant donut on the roof, Randy's style. `brick-60233-donut-shop.jpg`
  - 41701 *Street Food Market* (Friends, 2021). Has a taco truck. `brick-41701-taco-truck.jpg`
  - 10260 *Downtown Diner* (2018). A 1950s Googie-ish diner. `brick-10260-downtown-diner.jpg`
  - 3433 / 3500 NBA sets (2003). They have a **Kobe Bryant #8 minifig** in purple (road) and gold (home) Lakers kit. `brick-3433-nba-arena.jpg`
  - 10247 *Ferris Wheel* and 10261 *Roller Coaster* [M]. Fairground sets that could stand in for Santa Monica Pier.
- **LEGOLAND California, Miniland USA:**
  - The official page lists a **Los Angeles section**: "the Griffith Park Observatory perched on the hill above the Hollywood Bowl and Grauman's Chinese Theatre".
  - The **San Diego section** has a 30-ft beach and **120 LEGO palm trees**.
  - The park also has the LEGO **SoFi Stadium** (500k bricks, 25 builders).
  - Not found in Miniland: Capitol Records, Disney Hall, LAX, Santa Monica. I found no free-licence photo of the LA section; Brickimedia and Flickr scraping are both blocked.

## 1. Walt Disney Concert Hall (Frank Gehry, 2003)
**Photos:** `real-wdch-highsmith.jpg` (Carol M. Highsmith, public domain: best overall), `real-wdch-sullivan.jpg` (Jon Sullivan, PD), `real-wdch-panorama.jpg` (David G. Gordon, CC BY-SA 4.0).
- **Proportions** (measured from the Highsmith photo): about eight to ten curved stainless "sails". The tallest sail is ≈ 0.45 × the façade length. The sails lean outward and are concave, with a glass slot between them and a stone plinth with steps at the right end. Site: one city block on Grand Ave [M].
- **Colours:** matte brushed stainless (Wikipedia: matte, after the polished Founders Room panels caused glare and 60 °C hot spots) → **Flat Silver** or **Light Bluish Gray**. Glass slot → Trans-Clear over Dark Bluish Gray. Plinth and steps → **Tan**.
- **Brick versions found: 0 credible.** Searches (Brothers Brick, Flickr, Ideas, Rebrickable) turned up only an unrelated "Concert Hall" MOC-178615. There is an Ideas project id (49b3b0bd…) that I could not open. No Miniland or Masters version.
- **Buildable at our scale: hard.**
  - The sails are compound-curved, leaning and concave. At 20–30 studs, curved slopes and SNOT panels make a stepped approximation; arches and hinged plates give a few leaning planes.
  - With no proven build to copy, rule 14 ("as real as other builders manage") leaves us inventing the technique.
  - Recognisable only if the sail silhouette is right. A stepped grey blob reads as "modern building".
- **Verdict:** high fame, high risk. Leave out unless Eric insists, and if he does, plan two or three iteration rounds.

## 2. TCL (Grauman's) Chinese Theatre + Hollywood Walk of Fame
**Photos:** `real-chinese-highsmith.jpg` (Highsmith, PD, straight-on: use for measuring), `real-chinese-wide.jpg` (wolfgang.mller54, CC BY 2.0), `real-chinese-handprints.jpg` (Sidvics, CC BY-SA 4.0), `real-walkoffame-star.jpg` (Dietmar Rabich, CC BY-SA 4.0).

**Theatre**
- **Proportions** (Wikipedia: the pagoda is 90 ft / 27 m tall; read from the photo):
  - The two red columns are ≈ 0.35 H.
  - The bronze roof cone is ≈ 0.35 H, with a black eave that sweeps up into huge black-and-gold horn finials, giving a total roof width ≈ 0.85 H.
  - A 30 ft (9 m) dragon relief panel sits between the columns.
  - Low white wings with orange-red sweeping eaves flank the pagoda on both sides.
  - Two Ming guardian lions stand at the door.
  - The forecourt has ≈ 200 handprint slabs (Wikipedia).
- **Colours:**
  - Roof: oxidised bronze → **Sand Green / Olive Green** shingles.
  - Columns and wing eaves: coral-orange → **Coral** or **Orange** (the photo reads orange more than red).
  - Eaves, finials and door frame: **Black** with **Pearl Gold**.
  - Walls: **White**. Dragon panel: **Light Bluish Gray**. Lions: **White**. Forecourt: **Light Bluish Gray**.

**Walk of Fame star**
- **Real thing:** a five-point coral-pink terrazzo star rimmed in brass, set in a **3 × 3 ft charcoal terrazzo square**. The honoree's name is in brass letters, with a round brass category emblem below. Stars are set **every 6 ft** along 15 blocks of Hollywood Blvd and 3 of Vine (2,787 stars as of 2024).
- **At our scale:** 3 ft ≈ 2.7 studs, so a **2 × 2 tile** works, one every ≈ 5 studs (6 ft = 5.4 studs).
- **Print (L2):** a Dark Bluish Gray 2 × 2 tile with a **Coral** star, a **Pearl Gold** rim and a gold dot, and **no name**, since a real name is a real person. That stays within the ≤ 4 colours rule.
- 10232's "sidewalk of the stars" is the official precedent. I believe it uses printed star tiles, but I did not verify the exact print [M].
- **Pairing:** the theatre forecourt opens straight onto the star-lined Hollywood Blvd pavement, and the Dolby Theatre (Oscars) is next door. One element covers it: pagoda + forecourt of light-grey tiles, with 2–3 handprint tiles (an L2 print of two hand outlines on light grey) + a 4–6-stud strip of pavement with 3–4 star tiles. A minifig kneeling to put their hands in the prints is the "doing something" figure.

**Brick versions found: about 6.**
1. LEGOLAND California Miniland, LA section (official Merlin model shop). No photo obtained.
2. 10232 *Palace Cinema* (official, inspired by it: stars sidewalk, dark red and gold).
3–5. Three LEGO Ideas projects: c3ed715a…, a77a9236…, and 218b9184… (a "Hollywood" architecture set with 6 landmarks). From search snippets, one has 1 × 1-tile handprint slabs, guardian lions, a red pagoda with a copper roof, dragon silhouettes and a carved dragon panel.
6. Disney's Hollywood Studios has a full-size replica, not LEGO. Ignore it.

**Buildable: good.**
- The roof cone is stacked inverted/curved slopes in Sand Green.
- The sweeping eaves use the same curved-slope and wedge vocabulary as Kyoto's roofs, which we already have.
- Horn finials: black horn/claw/plant-stem moulds on clips.
- The columns are 2 × 2 Coral round or square bricks.
- The dragon is a printed 2 × 4 or 4 × 6 tile (L2) or a relief made of grey bars.
- Footprint ≈ 16 × 12 studs plus a forecourt.

**Recognisable: high** (silhouette + colour, same as the Kyoto pagoda logic).

## 3. Capitol Records Building (Welton Becket / Louis Naidorf, 1956)
**Photos:** `real-capitol-cc0.jpg` (Alexis Doine, CC0, 2026: the best top view), `real-capitol-young.jpg` (Rob Young, CC BY 2.0).
- **Proportions:**
  - Thirteen storeys, 46 m (151 ft) to the spire tip. First circular office building.
  - From the CC0 photo: the spire is ≈ 0.35 of total H. The drum is slightly taller than it is wide (H:D ≈ 1.3–1.5).
  - Each storey carries a **curved sunshade lip that projects and is cut into ≈ 8 segments**: the "stack of records".
  - On top: a black ring sign reading CAPITOL RECORDS, a white needle spire, and a red beacon that blinks "Hollywood" in Morse.
  - A low rectangular base block carries the "Hollywood Jazz 1945–1972" tile mural (26 × 88 ft).
- **Colours:** **White** drum and shades, **Black / Trans-Black** window bands, **Black** ring sign with white lettering (L2 print, an allowed short word), **White** spire, **Trans-Red** beacon.
- **Brick versions found: 4.**
  1. **Adam Ward's *Capitol Records Tower*** (2017, licensed by Capitol for its 75th anniversary, real LEGO parts + one custom spire, 642 pcs, 16 × 24 base). The drum is **8 panels around an octagon**, and each storey's sunshade is a ring of white slopes/tiles. Brick Architect: it "captures the spirit", but the octagon could have been 14-sided at the same 28-stud diameter. `brick-capitol-adamward.jpg`
  2. Ultrake, Eurobricks 2016, MOC with the jazz mural side.
  3. LEGO Ideas 43b0bc61….
  4. Inside the fan Ideas LA Skyline (eddda007…).
- **Buildable: good.** At our scale a 6–8 stud drum works: round 6 × 6 or 8 × 8 plates/dishes, alternating with a dark round-brick window band, 13 times. The real look is the stepped "record stack", which **round plates do natively**. Spire: a white bar/antenna + a cone. Footprint ≈ 8 × 8 + a 8 × 12 base block.
- **Recognisable: high** in silhouette, but it needs height (it is the natural hero/tallest tier).
- **Licensing flag:** Capitol Records treats the exterior design as a trademark (it is printed on Adam Ward's box). For a personal non-commercial app this is the same footing as the other landmarks. Print "CAPITOL" lettering only if we accept that.

## 4. LAX Theme Building (Pereira & Luckman, Paul Williams, Becket, 1961)
**Photos:** `real-lax-lek.jpg` (Steven Lek, CC BY-SA 4.0: clean side view), `real-lax-cc0.jpg` (Junkyardsparkle, CC0).
- **Proportions** (from the Lek photo):
  - **Two crossed parabolic arches.** Span ≈ 2.4 × height; each arch is ≈ 0.04 H thick and tapers.
  - A saucer restaurant disc (≈ 0.45 of span), at ≈ 0.55 H, has a dark glazed band and sits on a **blue cylindrical core**, which also pokes up above the disc.
  - A perforated white screen wall rings the base. The arch legs are concrete for 15 ft (Wikipedia).
  - Arches ≈ 135 ft high [M].
- **Colours:** **White** arches and disc, **Blue** core (the photo reads a mid royal blue), **Trans-Black** glazing, **White** screen wall.
- **Brick versions found: 2.**
  1. **Eiffleman (Grant Passmore)**, *LAX Theme Building* (2015, Brick Architect feature, LEGO Ideas de84c507…, 341 parts, 22 × 22 studs). The arches are **white curved slopes on chains of trans-clear hinge plates**, the screen wall is **Technic bricks with holes** in a ring, the core is blue round bricks, and the disc is stacked round plates. `brick-lax-eiffleman.jpg`
  2. A TurboSquid 3D model (not LEGO, ignore).
- **Buildable: medium.** At 24–30 studs of span, the arches need hinge chains (real, allowed by rule 3, and `check-asm-studs` accepts hinge joints). The perforated wall from Technic bricks is a ready-made real technique.
- **Recognisable: high** (the silhouette is unique).
- **Downside:** it is an airport building, and wide and low, so it eats island width for little height.

## 5. Urban Light at LACMA (Chris Burden, 2008)
**Photo:** `real-urbanlight-zanotti.jpg` (Joey Zanotti, CC BY 2.0; contains an identifiable person, reference only). Commons has very few photos of it because US freedom of panorama does not cover sculpture, so expect few free images [M on the reason].
- **Real:**
  - **202 restored 1920s–30s cast-iron street lamps** in a near grid of 17.4 × 17.9 m.
  - 16 lamp models, all painted uniform **grey**, of different heights. Lantern heads and globe heads are mixed, and there are 309 LED bulbs.
  - Fluted shafts with stepped octagonal bases.
- **Colours:** **Light Bluish Gray** posts, **White / Trans-Clear** globes and lanterns.
- **Brick versions found: 0** (searched Ideas, Brothers Brick, generic). No LEGO version exists, as far as search shows.
- **Buildable: very easy, with real moulds.** LEGO's classic **lamp post 2 × 2 × 7 with fluted base** [M: part 2039, also a 1 × 1 lamp post variant] *is* this kind of lamp. A **7 × 7 grid at 2-stud pitch ≈ 14 × 14 studs** reads as Urban Light without needing 202. Alternate globe tops (1 × 1 round brick/dome in white) and lantern tops (a minifig lantern or 1 × 1 cone stack) for variety. Pure repetition of a real mould, no invention.
- **Recognisable: medium–high** for anyone who has been to LA (the most-photographed spot in the city). For others it reads as "a field of lamps", which is still beautiful at night if the lamps glow.
- Cheap, a perfect plaza or "breathing room" element, and a great minifig selfie spot.

## 6. Randy's Donuts / Googie roadside
**Photos:** `real-randys-highsmith.jpg` (Highsmith, PD: the classic view), `real-randys-air.jpg` (Northwalker, CC0: plan view from the LAX approach), `real-randys-stierch.jpg` (Sarah Stierch, CC BY 4.0: close-up of the donut lettering).
- **Proportions:**
  - The **donut is 32 ft 6 in (9.9 m) in diameter**, standing on edge on the roof of a tiny one-storey 1952 drive-in kiosk (Henry J. Goodwin).
  - The donut is ≈ 2–2.5 × the kiosk height and wider than the kiosk.
  - It sits beside the I-405 Manchester off-ramp in Inglewood, under the LAX approach path.
- **Colours:** donut → **Medium Nougat / Dark Tan** with **Black** "RANDY'S DONUTS" lettering in white outline. Kiosk → **White** with glazing. Sprinkle dimples are the same colour as the dough (texture only).
- **Brick versions found: 3+.**
  1. Official 60233 *Donut Shop Opening* (a brick-built pink donut on the roof). `brick-60233-donut-shop.jpg`
  2. LEGO Ideas *Randy's Donuts* (4dd41c65…: 32 × 32, 751 pcs, 7 figs, drive-thru, giant donut).
  3. Several other Ideas donut-shop projects.
  - For Googie style generally: 10260 *Downtown Diner* (official).
- **Buildable: very good.** A standing ring can be made from four **macaroni / round-corner bricks** on SNOT brackets, or from curved slopes around a 2-stud hole [M on the exact moulds; verify in LDraw]. The lettering on curved faces is a print problem: put "RANDY'S" on 2–4 flat tiles or skip it; the donut alone reads.
- **Recognisable: very high** (a giant donut on a roof is LA cinema shorthand: *Iron Man 2*, *2012*, *Get Shorty*). It is cheap: a footprint ≈ 8 × 8.

## 7. Classic LA street: palms, cars, taco truck, In-N-Out
**Photos:** `real-palms-rodeo.jpg` (Ariel E Barry, CC BY-SA 3.0), `real-innout-lax.jpg` (Jackg98, CC BY 4.0: the In-N-Out by LAX, with the crossed palms and the plane-spotting site), `real-lowrider-fair.jpg` (Jengod, CC BY-SA 4.0).
- **Palms:**
  - The LA signature is the **Mexican fan palm**: an absurdly tall, thin trunk (25–30 m) with a small tuft crown, in rows down Beverly Hills streets [M].
  - Proportion: trunk height ≈ 25–30 × trunk width, crown ≈ 1/8 of the height.
  - In LEGO: a 1 × 1 round brick stack in **Reddish Brown / Dark Tan** + a small palm-frond top. Miniland San Diego alone has 120 palms. *Flag:* a true-proportion fan palm would be taller than every landmark, so compress it under the house tier per rule 5, or let one or two break the rule as the LA skyline cliché (Eric decides).
- **Cars:**
  - Lowrider (a '60s Chevy Impala: candy paint, wire wheels, hydraulics hop). Brick versions are plentiful as MOCs (Brothers Brick 2019 and 2025, Breaking Bad Monte Carlo, Flamingo Cutlass [search]). Lowrider colours: Dark Red / Magenta / Dark Turquoise candy plus chrome (Pearl Silver).
  - Convertible: 10232 includes a limo, and 10260 a 1950s car [M].
  - A 6-wide minifig car is a proven, readable form.
- **Taco truck:** official 41701 (Friends) has one. A minifig-scale food truck with a serving hatch is easy and readable.
- **In-N-Out:**
  - Brick versions: ≥ 4 MOCs/Ideas (Eurobricks modulars 59306 and 139228, Ideas dcaf2e3d… at 1,108 pcs).
  - Signatures: a **white box with a red band, a yellow "boomerang" arrow sign, and two crossed palms** (planted as an X at many stores [M]).
  - It is a trademark and logo, and the arrow plus lettering is brand art. Better to evoke it with a generic white-red-yellow drive-in than to print the name.

## 8. Scope additions (client: sun, beach, palms, skate, graffiti, beach workouts)

### 8a. "Hall of Fame": what the client probably means
1. **Hollywood Walk of Fame** (most likely): stars in the pavement, pairs with §2.
2. **Dolby Theatre** next door (the Oscars).
3. **Hollywood Bowl** (in Miniland California: concentric white shell arches).
4. **Star Plaza at Crypto.com Arena**: bronze statues of Lakers greats, incl. the Kobe statue unveiled 2024 [M].
5. Kobe's own Naismith Basketball **Hall of Fame** induction (2020). The client may mean this if the Kobe thread is the point.

Ask which one. My bet is the Walk of Fame.

### 8b. LA murals and Kobe
- **Kobe murals:**
  - The largest is **"Grana"** by Odeith & Nikkolas Smith, 125 × 32 ft, of Kobe and Gianna, on S. Hope St, DTLA.
  - "Kid Mamba" (Paul Daniels, Hotel Figueroa).
  - "Legends Are Forever" (Royyal Dog, The Container Yard, Arts District).
  - A Redondo Beach mural in a Dodgers jersey.
  - Hundreds more across the city (Discover LA keeps a list).
  - Common motifs: **purple and gold**, **#8 and #24**, the **mamba / snake**, **angel wings or a halo**, a basketball, Gianna's #2. **Mamba Day = 8/24**, when the **Santa Monica Pier Ferris wheel lights purple and gold**.
- **Other famous LA murals:**
  - **The Great Wall of Los Angeles** (Judy Baca, 1978, ½ mile along the Tujunga Wash).
  - **Great Wall of Crenshaw** ("Our Mighty Contribution", 2002, Crenshaw Blvd 50th–52nd: Black history and culture).
  - Leimert Park / Vision Theatre murals.
  - The **Venice Art Walls** (legal graffiti on the beach).
  - Richard Wyatt's "Hollywood Jazz" tile mural on Capitol Records (§3).
- **Likeness problem:** a portrait of Kobe (or anyone real) on a printed tile is an unauthorised likeness, and it breaks our own rule of no photos and ≤ 4 flat colours. A face cannot be done well in 4 flat colours at thumbnail size anyway.
- **Faceless option (L2, ≤ 4 colours):** a wall of **Dark Purple + Bright Light Orange/Yellow (gold)** tiles with **"8" and "24"** as big numerals (each its own 2 × 2 or 2 × 4 tile), a basketball (an orange 1 × 1 round tile with black lines), and a pair of **white wings**.
  - Does it read? **Yes** for anyone who knows Lakers/Kobe, because 8 + 24 + purple-gold is exactly what fans paint on the Mamba Day pier and on walls. **No** for anyone else, who sees a "basketball mural", which is still a legible LA image.
  - Avoid the Lakers logo and wordmark (NBA trademark). Numbers and colours alone are generic.
  - Bonus: purple and gold on the Ferris wheel is a second, subtle nod if the pier is in.

### 8c. LA hip-hop: one paragraph
West Coast hip-hop's real geography is **South LA and the southern suburbs, not Hollywood**: Compton (N.W.A, Dr. Dre, Kendrick Lamar), Long Beach (Snoop Dogg), and Crenshaw Blvd / Leimert Park (Nipsey Hussle's Marathon store on Crenshaw & Slauson; Leimert Park as the Black cultural hub) [M for the artist-place pairs]. The single most legible object is the **lowrider**: a candy-painted '64 Impala on wire wheels with its front end hopping on hydraulics. It is the shared icon of Chicano East LA cruising culture (Whittier Blvd) and West Coast rap videos, it is a proven LEGO MOC subject, and a minifig at the hydraulic switch is "doing something". A **boombox** on a shoulder is the second option (a printed 1 × 2 brick, L2). A **Crenshaw-style mural wall** is the place version. **Randy's Donuts** (Inglewood, next door) is on the cover of J Dilla's *Donuts* (Wikipedia) and sits in the same South LA stretch, so lowrider + Randy's + a mural wall is one coherent "South LA" corner.

### 8d. Beach / skate / workout: the client's own list
- **Venice Beach:**
  - The **skatepark** (2009, 16,000 ft², pool-style bowls).
  - **Muscle Beach** outdoor gym (barbells, pull-up bars, rings; the original is in Santa Monica).
  - The **Venice Art Walls** (graffiti).
  - **LA County lifeguard towers**: small raised huts on stilts with a ramp, painted in pastel colours [M].
  - **Santa Monica Pier** with the Pacific Park Ferris wheel (official 10247 for technique).
  - Brick versions: an Ideas skatepark MOC (4466f815…, 1,900 pcs). I did not search for lifeguard-tower MOCs (they are common [M]).
- **Minifig moulds to check in LDraw:** skateboard (with wheels), barbell/weight, surfboard, beach towel tile. All exist as real accessories [M]; confirm part numbers.
- **Buildable: very good**, and this is the strongest fit for "life layer" figures: a skater mid-bowl, someone pressing a barbell, a lifeguard. Sand ground (Tan), not lawn, follows rule 5.
