# Oriental Pearl Tower (东方明珠): reference notes
2026-10-02 · research only, no code · images in this folder unless a path says otherwise

## 0. Real tower: proportions to hit

**Files:** `../shanghai-real/Oriental_Pearl_Tower.jpg` (straight-on telephoto, used for measuring), `real-outline.png` (Commons silhouette SVG, stylised, **not** used for measuring), `real-detail.jpg` (upper sphere close-up: glass lattice, deck ring, underside), `real-base.jpg` (aerial of the base from Jin Mao: columns, struts, knee spheres, plaza), `real-bottom.jpg` (worm's-eye: strut/column plan relationship, dot windows), `real-from-jinmao.jpg` and `real-top-from-shanghai-tower.jpg` (oblique top views, whole tower).

**Method.** I scanned the straight-on photo row by row for non-sky pixels to get silhouette widths and feature heights. I calibrated on two published centre heights, lower sphere 93 m and upper sphere 272.5 m (Chinese press figures, quoted in search results), which gives 0.356 m/px. Check: the space-module centre comes out at 342 m (published 342 m) and the tip at about 463 m (published 468 m). Ground is hidden behind an overpass and is extrapolated (y ≈ 1345). Published figures are from zh.wikipedia: 468 m total, lower sphere Ø 50 m, five small spheres Ø 12 m, columns Ø 9 m and 7 m apart, struts Ø 7 m at 60°, antenna 118 m, module Ø 16 m (en sources say 14 m). Upper sphere Ø 45 m comes from en sources; I did not confirm it in a primary source.

| Feature | Real | ÷ H (468 m) | at H = 66 plates, true ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower sphere centre | 93 m | 0.20 | 13 plates |
| Lower sphere Ø | 50 m (photo 57 m incl. rings) | 0.107 | 2.8 studs |
| Upper sphere centre | 272.5 m | 0.58 | 38 plates |
| Upper sphere Ø | 45 m (photo 51 m incl. deck ring) | 0.096 | 2.5 studs |
| Space module centre / Ø | 342 m / 16 m | 0.73 / 0.034 | 48 plates / 0.9 stud |
| Antenna above module | ≈ 118 m | 0.25 | 17 plates |
| Column Ø / gap | 9 m / 7 m | 0.019 | 0.5 stud |
| Column cluster width (front view) | ≈ 28 m | 0.06 | 1.1 studs |
| Strut foot radius from axis | ≈ 55 m (± 5) | 0.12 | 3 studs |

- **Size ratios that matter:** upper sphere ≈ 0.9 × lower; module ≈ 0.3 × lower; column cluster ≈ 0.5 × lower-sphere width; the shaft between the spheres is ≈ 130 m ≈ 2.6 lower-sphere diameters; the spire stem above the upper sphere is ≈ 10 m wide (0.2 × lower).
- **Every LEGO version scales the spheres up** (measured by eye from the photos in §1–7): Brickman lower sphere ≈ 0.13 H, Miniland outdoor ≈ 0.155 H, 21039 ≈ 0.115 H, real 0.107 H. All keep the lower centre at **0.20 H**. Miniland and 21039 push the upper sphere up to ≈ 0.61–0.71 H by shortening the spire. **Flag for the brief:** a 6-stud lower sphere on a 66-plate tower is 0.23 H, 2.1× real and fatter than any reference. At Miniland's ratio a 6-stud sphere needs ≈ 97 plates of height, and a 66-plate tower takes ≈ 4 studs (Brickman's ratio gives 3.4).
- **Struts:** three inclined struts at **60°** (measured 59° from the photo, matching the published figure). They leave the **underside of the lower sphere just outside the columns** (≈ 69 m) and land ≈ 55 m from the axis. Each has a **knee sphere (≈ 11 m) about 60 % of the way up (≈ 41–46 m)**, and from that sphere **a second leg runs steeply down and inward to the ground**, so each strut is an inverted Y (`real-base.jpg`, `real-bottom.jpg`). `docs/countries/china.md` currently says the three spheres sit "where the struts meet the ground": **that is wrong, they are knees.**
- **Plan:** three columns in a triangle. The struts radiate **through the gaps between the columns, i.e. 60° off the columns**. From `real-bottom.jpg` (one column dead ahead, struts left and right of it) and `real-base.jpg` (a strut comes toward the camera between two columns). The front view in `Oriental_Pearl_Tower.jpg` looks into a gap: two columns side by side, the small spheres visible between them, one strut coming straight at the viewer. I found no true plan drawing.
- **Shaft between the spheres:** **six** pink-trimmed transverse "bridge" platforms tie the columns at ≈ 122, 144, 168, 192, 214 and 238 m. Each of the **five small spheres** (Ø 12 m) hangs just below one platform, at ≈ 137, 160, 183, 206 and 228 m, spaced ≈ 23 m. They sit on the axis and bulge into the gaps. The spheres are silver; the platform fascias are silver with a magenta stripe.
- **Spire stages** (from the photo): above the upper sphere, a wide latticed stem (≈ 10 m) with a flange ring at its base runs to the module (≈ 330 m). Then the module. Then four mast stages that narrow upward: ≈ 6 m × 28 m, ≈ 3 m × 26 m ending in a ring/flange, ≈ 2.4 m × 31 m, ≈ 1.5 m × 18 m to the tip. The mast reads dark grey lattice at distance, not white. Red/white banding appears only on LEGO versions.
- **Colour layering** (photo samples: rose glass ≈ RGB 157,111,135 in sun and 104–109,79–97,94 in shade, so a dusty mauve-rose; silver panels ≈ 124,126,127; columns warm concrete ≈ 152,145,131):
  - *Lower sphere:* silver cap with one ring of round red dot windows, **rose band**, thin silver ring, **rose band**, then a **silver lower half** (≈ 45 %) with a second dot ring. Two rose bands, both in the upper-middle.
  - *Upper sphere:* **rose almost from the top to just below the equator** (a triangulated geodesic lattice), then a dark window band (observation deck), a projecting silver deck ring, and a **silver bowl underneath** where the columns enter.
  - *Space module:* rose upper half with a dark window band, silver lower half, a ring of small antennae.
  - *Columns:* pale warm grey, close to the beige side of grey. 21039, Miniland and Wange all chose **Tan**; Brickman and Sheffield chose Light Bluish Gray and Tan respectively.
- **Base:** a round raised plaza with lawns. The columns stand in a round sunken ring (a pool or light well). A glazed canopy over the main entrance sits above one strut foot (`real-base.jpg`). Further out, landscaped rings and a road loop (the Lujiazui roundabout).

## 1. LEGO Architecture 21039 Shanghai (2018), official, LEGO design team
URL: https://brickarchitect.com/2018/review-21039-shanghai-skyline/ · https://brickset.com/article/39548/review-21039-shanghai · files `../shanghai-21039/tbb-12-*.jpg`, `tbb-19-*.jpg`, `tbb-21-*.jpg`
- **Spheres:** flattened lenses, not spheres. A **Magenta 3×3 radar dish** pair (Brick Architect: five 3×3 radar dishes, part 43898, in Magenta, a colour exclusive to this set) sits between Light Bluish Gray round plates. Brickset calls them "rounded modules … not actually spheres".
- **Magenta band:** the dish rim is the whole band, one solid colour with no lattice.
- **Columns:** Tan stacked round bricks with a plate-level step. Brickset: "three columns using 1×1 round bricks".
- **Struts:** Tan round-brick cylinders hung on clips/hinges from the core, ending in Light Bluish Gray clips on bars set into the base. No knee spheres, no fork. Brickset: "the spacing between the angled columns looks rather awkward."
- **Small spheres:** not modelled.
- **Spire:** stacked round plates and cones, white bar tip.
- **Base:** round platform of bow/macaroni tiles and quarter tiles (`tbb-12`). Brick Architect praises it; Brickset says the base "lacks detail".
- Reads at a glance: the magenta-on-tan contrast and the two-bead silhouette. Fails: no roundness, no knee, struts too stubby.
- **Lesson:** colour contrast (Magenta on pale) carries the identity even at micro scale. Dishes alone give lenses, not spheres, so we must stack more profile above and below the dish.

## 2. Brickman "Wonders of the World", Centuri Chan (2016)
URL: https://www.flickr.com/photos/ryanmcnaught/31219595002/ · https://www.flickr.com/photos/porkynz/27163436927/ · files `brickman-centurichan-full.jpg`, `brickman-centurichan-display.jpg`, `brickman-centurichan-base.jpg`
- 11,260 pieces, 83 build hours (Flickr captions).
- **Spheres:** studs-up stepped sculpture. Each layer is a bigger or smaller ring of bricks and plates, so the silhouette is a stepped sphere. Bands are pixel-dithered Magenta, Dark Pink and White, with a dark recessed window band at the equator (Ryan McNaught: "cutaway windows").
- **Columns:** Light Bluish Gray with dark grey reveals. **Small spheres:** silver dishes inside the gap with magenta-striped platforms, all five or six present.
- **Struts:** grey beams. **White dish knee spheres** sit at the knee, and the inner leg of each fork is modelled.
- **Base:** a big round plaza with a glazed canopy of trans-clear panels over the entrance, lawns, red trees, steps, minifigs.
- Reads well: the real proportions (lower centre 0.20 H) and the knee spheres. Weak: the stepped spheres look boxy up close, and the pastel dithering is pinker than the real rose.
- **Lesson:** the knee spheres and the entrance canopy are what make the base read as *this* tower. A dark window band at the equator adds a lot for one row of parts.

## 3. Sheffield "Bricktropolis" exhibition (2018), builder not credited on Flickr
URL: https://www.flickr.com/photos/77850304@N04/43951356862/ (Kyle Emmerson's photos) · files `bricktropolis-sheffield-full.jpg`, `bricktropolis-sheffield-front.jpg`
- A separate photo (Chris Brown) captions it "Oriental Pearl tower 48,972" bricks. I did not verify the builder.
- **Spheres:** sculpted studs-out/studs-up. The upper sphere is **solid Dark Red** over a white/trans-clear deck ring with a grey bowl. The lower sphere is Light Bluish Gray with a dark red lattice band and LEDs.
- **Columns:** Tan. **Small spheres:** grey sculpted balls under red-lit platforms.
- **Struts:** straight Tan beams. **Knees:** grey sculpted balls with the inner leg.
- **Base:** a round plaza inside a road loop.
- Reads well: the silhouette and the deck ring. Fails: red instead of rose; the colour is the first thing that looks wrong.
- **Lesson:** the hue matters as much as the shape. Red reads "generic TV tower"; it must be Magenta or rose.

## 4. LEGOLAND Shanghai Miniland, outdoor Lujiazui (2025), Merlin model shop
URL: https://www.flickr.com/photos/haettich/54967991247/ · https://www.flickr.com/photos/haettich/54968002512/ · files `legoland-shanghai-miniland-outdoor.jpg`, `legoland-shanghai-miniland-outdoor-wide.jpg`
- **Spheres:** sculpted bricks with a 1-stud pixel grid. The **triangular glass lattice is pixel-mapped**: Magenta triangles separated by Light Bluish Gray lines, plus a dark window row at the equator. The lower sphere has two lattice bands and a grey cap and lower half, as in reality. The upper sphere is lattice almost all over, above a broad grey deck disc and bowl.
- **Columns:** Tan with a dark turquoise core visible in the gaps (the lift shaft). **Platforms:** Tan with a magenta stripe.
- **Struts:** Tan sculpted slabs. **Knee spheres** are sculpted grey, **with the inner leg**. The ground ring is Dark Turquoise/Tan.
- **Spire:** Tan stem, small magenta-lattice module, flanges, white/red mast.
- **Proportions:** lower sphere ≈ 0.155 H; upper centre ≈ 0.71 H because the spire is shortened.
- The most faithful layering of all versions. At our scale the lattice cannot be pixel-mapped (spheres 4–6 studs), but the *band order* can be copied exactly.
- **Lesson:** copy the band order and the knee fork. Tan columns are an accepted LEGO reading of the warm concrete.

## 5. LEGOLAND Shanghai Miniland, indoor night city (2025), Merlin model shop
URL: https://www.flickr.com/photos/haettich/54969234575/ · file `legoland-shanghai-miniland-indoor.jpg`
- A second, larger Pearl lit for night. Rose lattice spheres with **LED-lit red rings** at the deck and equator; the lower sphere is grey with a dot-window ring.
- The **knee spheres are dark blue/grey** domes, and the forked legs are visible.
- Columns are white/tan with magenta-lit platforms.
- **Lesson:** the **dot-window ring** on the lower sphere's grey cap and bottom is a cheap readable detail. At our scale it could be a ring of 1×1 round tiles in Magenta or Dark Red on the grey band, if the geometry allows.

## 6. LEGO Store People's Square, Shanghai (2018), LEGO in-house builders
URL: https://www.flickr.com/photos/shnake1973/32077524528/ · https://www.flickr.com/photos/michisunited/48348128472/ · files `legostore-peoplessquare-upper-sphere.jpg`, `legostore-peoplessquare-columns.jpg`
- Two storeys tall. Brick Fanatics: the Pearl and the skyline mosaic together use 4 M bricks.
- **Upper sphere:** a true geodesic. Light Bluish Gray **triangular ribs** stand proud of Dark Red/Magenta panels, LED-lit. Below sits a Dark Turquoise/trans deck ring and a stepped grey bowl.
- **Columns:** Tan with vertical grooves. **Small spheres:** stepped grey-brick balls with a red LED, nested in the gap under platform rings that carry a Dark Red stripe.
- **Module:** a Dark Pink/Magenta/White band stack.
- **Mast:** Dark Bluish Gray lattice with spikes. It matches the real mast, unlike the red/white masts elsewhere.
- **Lesson:** the spire reads best as dark grey lattice. Small spheres nested *inside* the column gap, under a platform, is the real arrangement.

## 7. Wange 5224 "The Oriental Pearl Tower-China" (2019), Chinese brick brand
URL: https://www.merlinsbricks.com/sets/wan-5224/ · files `wange-5224-front.jpg`, `wange-5224-size.jpg`
- 1,106–1,109 pieces (box and SetDB). 74.5 cm tall on a 27.5 cm base (≈ 34 studs).
- **Spheres:** a studs-out drum. Dark Red **curved slopes / SNOT plates around a core** form the band, with White stepped plates above and below (cap and lower half). The sphere reads as a barrel with rounded ends.
- **Columns:** Tan 1×1 bricks with Dark Red 1×1 accents as platforms. Small spheres: none visible.
- **Struts:** straight Tan beams on white hinge joints at both ends. **No knees.**
- **Spire:** Tan stem, then a red-and-white banded mast.
- **Base:** square green lawn plate with a trans-light-blue entrance canopy and trees.
- Reads well: the height and slenderness. Fails: barrel spheres, red-not-rose, no knees, square base.
- **Lesson:** a studs-out curved-slope ring is a good way to make a *round band* at mid scale. Its weak point is the cap and bowl: the stepped plates leave a barrel. Rule out red and the square base.

## 8. Knock-off micro skyline (brand not stated), photographed by 0nuku
URL: https://www.flickr.com/photos/0nuku/45557738952/ · file `knockoff-skyline-micro-0nuku.jpg`
- About a 3-stud scale. Each sphere is two hemispheres, **Magenta on top and Light Bluish Gray below**, on a Tan round-brick stem. The module is a magenta 1×1 round piece.
- I could not identify the exact moulds from the photo; they look like minifig-head-sized 2×2 domes or round bricks.
- **Lesson:** even at micro scale, splitting the colour **top = rose, bottom = silver** reads instantly as the Pearl. For our lower sphere it is the right first-order split (rose top half, grey bottom).

## 9. Timothy Ng, "Pearl Oriental Tower" (Hong Kong, 2013)
URL: https://www.flickr.com/photos/134702549@N07/21607363662/ · file `flickr-timothyng-topdown.jpg`
- **Spheres:** stacked grey round plates (cap) over Dark Red **curved slopes / bows set studs-out** in two bands.
- **Columns:** Tan, with Dark Red lattice ties as platforms. **Struts:** Tan, no knees.

## Borrow / avoid
**Borrow**
- **Real sphere profile, not a lens.** 21039's dish pair only gives the equator. To get a sphere at 4–6 studs, put a dome/hemisphere or stepped round plates above and below the dish band. Miniland and Brickman both build the full sphere with a cap and a bowl.
- **Band order from Miniland §4:**
  - lower sphere: grey cap, rose band, thin grey ring, rose band, grey lower half;
  - upper sphere: rose almost to the equator, then a dark window row, a projecting grey deck ring, and a grey bowl;
  - module: rose top, grey bottom.
- **Colour:** Magenta for the glass. Tan (or a warm light grey) for the columns. Light Bluish Gray / Flat Silver for the panels and rings. A Dark Bluish Gray lattice mast (§6). No red/white mast.
- **Knee spheres with the inverted-Y fork** on each strut, about 60 % up (§2, §4, §5). This is the signature most small builds miss.
- **Struts in the column gaps** (60° off the columns) at 60°, leaving the lower sphere's underside just outside the columns.
- **Six platforms and five small spheres** nested in the front gap, evenly spaced (≈ 0.05 H apart).
- **A round plaza** with a central well and a glazed entrance canopy over one strut foot (§2, §9).
- **A dark window row** on the upper sphere and the module (§2, §4), and optionally a dot ring on the lower sphere (§5).

**Avoid**
- Red or Dark Red glass (§3, §7, §9). It reads as a generic tower.
- Barrel or lens spheres with flat caps (§1, §7).
- Struts without knees (§1, §7, §9).
- A square base (§7).
- **A 6-stud lower sphere on a 66-plate tower without rechecking.** It is 2.1× real and fatter than every reference; Miniland's ratio needs ≈ 97 plates for 6 studs. Decide the height and the sphere size together.
